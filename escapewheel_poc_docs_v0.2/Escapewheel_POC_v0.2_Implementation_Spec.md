# Escapewheel Portable Data Packet Specification, v0.2

Informational entry point.

## 1. Orientation

This document set describes a proof-of-principle portable representation of extracellular
electrophysiology data as a small semantic and logical standard over commodity storage formats.
[Part 00 §1](00_architecture.md#ew-architecture-scope) defines its scope, and
[Part 00 §9](00_architecture.md#ew-numbered-part-ownership) assigns the subject and status of
every numbered part.

The design tests whether neurophysiology data can be represented over commodity substrates:
Parquet for tables, Zarr for dense arrays, standard media containers for media, and JSON for the
manifest. **Escapewheel** is a placeholder name. The project does not require an NWB, HDMF, or PyNWB
runtime; Part 00 defines the exact scope and exclusions.

## 2. Authority and conformance

The numbered parts and the machine-readable artifacts they expressly incorporate form the current
authoritative specification candidate. Part 00 defines
[candidate and release status](00_architecture.md#ew-specification-candidate),
[authority by concern](00_architecture.md#ew-authority-by-concern), and
[internal consistency](00_architecture.md#ew-candidate-consistency).

The four conformance actors are summarized in
[Part 00 §2.4](00_architecture.md#ew-specification-actors). Packet and writer conformance are
defined in [Part 05 §10](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-conformance-classes), and verdict
composition in [Part 05 §9.5](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-verdict-composition). Reader
resolution is defined by Parts 03 and 04, and extension independence by Part 09.

# Part 00 — Architecture

Normative.

<a id="ew-architecture-scope" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Scope

This part defines the Escapewheel specification framework: its authority and conformance model,
architectural classifications, numbered-part ownership and status, and publication conventions. It
also states the cross-cutting architecture of an Escapewheel packet. Detailed concept meanings,
logical declarations, physical encodings, manifest fields, and construction and validation rules
belong to the subject-owning parts listed in §9.

This specification defines a portable representation of extracellular electrophysiology data as a
small semantic and logical standard over commodity storage formats. It does not define a storage
engine, a PyNWB-compatible object model, an NWB exporter, a distributed-compute model, or ontology
machinery. It has no runtime dependency on NWB, HDMF, or PyNWB, and conformance does not require a
compatibility projection into NWB.

The design objectives are that a packet be fast to query, straightforward to produce from existing
acquisition and sorting outputs, readable from more than one language, independent of HDMF as a
runtime data model, and precise enough to drive validation.

<a id="ew-specification-authority" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Specification authority and conformance

Part 00 MUST own specification-candidate identity and candidate/release terminology; conformance
targets and normative-language conventions; the normative-reference policy and shared bibliography;
actors and the terms that resolve to them; normative, informative, and mixed status; artifact
roles and authority by concern; abstraction levels and their dependency direction; packet content
classes; each numbered part's subject; and publication-state conventions.

Part 00 MUST NOT become the owner of exact keys, columns, registry counts, profile encodings,
performance measurements, implementation plans, or detailed lifecycle algorithms merely because
it summarizes the architecture. A lower-level statement that remains here during reconciliation
MUST link to its actual owner or be explicitly informative once that owner is settled.

<a id="ew-specification-candidate" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.1 Specification candidate and release

The current generated v0.2 numbered parts and every artifact that a numbered part expressly
incorporates MUST constitute the authoritative working specification. An incorporated artifact
MUST participate only for the subject and extent the incorporating part states. A file outside
that set MUST NOT override it.

The working specification MUST be called a **specification candidate** until a publication decision
identifies and freezes an exact release. *Authoritative* describes what governs current development
and conformance; *candidate* describes publication state. Candidate status MUST NOT make the
working specification optional.

A **specification release** MUST be an immutable publication whose publication record identifies
its exact members and versions. This candidate distinguishes that state without selecting final
release membership or a publication-record format.

`README.md` MUST be an informational entry point. It MAY provide navigation, candidate status,
architectural summaries, build guidance, informative references, checked status and identifier
summaries, and links to owning artifacts. It MUST NOT be the sole statement of a requirement or
supply a normative preamble through its position in a rendering. The numbered parts remain
intelligible without repository-specific developer documentation. The combined-document generator
MAY place the README first for orientation, but that ordering MUST NOT supply normative force.

`Escapewheel_POC_v0.2_Implementation_Spec.md` MUST be a reproducible convenience rendering of its
inputs. It MUST NOT become a separately edited owner or acquire normative force from its
placement.

<a id="ew-authority-by-concern" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.2 Authority by concern

Numbered prose MUST own terms, meanings, processing semantics, actor obligations, conformance, and
relationships that a structural declaration cannot express faithfully.

A machine-readable artifact MUST acquire normative authority only when a numbered part expressly
incorporates it. It MUST own only the exact structures or assignments for which that part
incorporates it. The incorporating prose MUST continue to own the vocabulary's meaning, the
semantics of declared fields, and every applicable processing or conformance rule not fully
expressed by the artifact.

Implementations and evidence MUST NOT acquire an independent normative subject by implementing or
testing a rule.

Normativity, representation, and production MUST remain independent properties. Prose is not
normative merely because it is prose; JSON is not authoritative merely because it is structured;
generated output is not informative merely because it is generated. The declared artifact role
and subject ownership determine authority.

<a id="ew-candidate-consistency" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.3 Internal consistency

Each normative fact MUST have one owner. Another artifact that presents the same fact MUST be one
of:

1. a mechanically checked projection;
2. an explicitly informative explanation that links to its owner; or
3. an explicit consistency condition between different owners' subjects.

A disagreement between owners, an unfaithful projection, or an incorporated artifact that violates
its governing prose MUST make the candidate internally inconsistent. No general prose-wins,
data-wins, generated-wins, or local-file-wins rule MAY turn that disagreement into conformance.
The inconsistent candidate remains the authoritative material to inspect and repair; a
specification release MUST NOT contain an unresolved ownership conflict.

<a id="ew-specification-actors" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.4 Conformance targets and actors

Requirements in this specification apply to four actors. Every requirement binds one of them or
states a property of an artifact.

A **writer** produces a packet from sources. It stages, validates and commits, and it is the actor
Part 05 binds throughout.

A **reader** opens a committed packet and returns its resources — rows, arrays, resolved references.
It is bound by what it returns.

A **validator** decides whether a packet conforms, and reports findings.

A **consumer** interprets values a reader returned and acts on them. It is bound by what it
concludes, and it is the only actor that may be a person rather than a program.

An actor is a part someone plays, not a distribution: one program is commonly all four. Naming them
separately is what lets a clause say who owes an obligation.

Three further terms resolve to those four. A **build** is one execution of a writer against one
recipe, so a requirement on a build binds the writer performing it. An **adapter** is the part of a
writer that reads one source, and an obligation on an adapter binds the writer that ships it. An
**implementation** is software performing one or more of the four, and is the right subject only
where a requirement binds every actor alike, as §3.6's ban on path-based identity does.

`importer` is another name for a writer, `client` and `binding` for a reader, and `caller` for a
consumer. A requirement using one binds the actor it names.

<a id="ew-normative-language" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.5 Normative language

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, REQUIRED, SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, RECOMMENDED, NOT
RECOMMENDED, MAY, and OPTIONAL are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 when, and only when,
they appear in all capitals.

<a id="ew-architecture-classifications" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 3. Architecture classifications

<a id="ew-abstraction-levels" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.1 Abstraction levels

The specification MUST call semantic, logical, and physical classifications **abstraction
levels**. A lower abstraction level MUST NOT redefine a rule owned by the level above it. A
physical profile MAY map a logical resource to a storage technology and MUST NOT change that
resource's logical identity or semantic meaning. A workflow MAY describe how an actor satisfies an
owned invariant and MUST NOT restate a second version of that invariant.

<a id="ew-semantic-standard" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
#### 3.1.1 Semantic standard

The **semantic standard** defines what concepts mean. A Trial is a bounded interval of experimental
time; a Unit is a sorted or curated spiking entity; an ElectricalSeries is sampled extracellular
voltage over time and channels.

The semantic standard is independent of storage format and of whether a concept is represented as
a table or an array. Part 01 specifies it.

<a id="ew-logical-standard" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
#### 3.1.2 Logical standard

The **logical standard** defines how semantic concepts are represented as computable resources.
Its three logical kinds are tables, dense arrays, and opaque payloads. A view or materialization is
a realization of one of those kinds rather than another kind.

The logical standard is storage-agnostic but not shape-agnostic: trials are logically table-shaped.
Part 02 and the artifacts it incorporates specify it.

<a id="ew-physical-profile" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
#### 3.1.3 Physical profile

A **physical profile** defines how logical resources are stored. Two are contemplated:

| profile | tables | arrays | manifest |
|---|---|---|---|
| portable | Parquet | Zarr | JSON manifest |
| lakehouse | Iceberg over Parquet | Zarr | JSON manifest plus Iceberg catalog references |

Only the portable profile, `escapewheel-portable-v0.1`, is specified. Part 03 specifies it and is
normative for a packet whose manifest declares its `profile_id` and binds no other packet. A
physical profile is the only abstraction level whose rules may depend on a storage technology.

<a id="ew-packet-content-classes" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.2 Packet content classes

The specification MUST call content's architectural relationship to a packet its **packet content
class**.

| serialized value | contents | canonical | required by class |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|
| `core` | domain-general normalized facts | yes | no |
| `extension` | typed facts a domain owns (Part 09) | yes | no |
| `analysis` | products reproducible from canonical resources | no | no |
| `compatibility_projection` | a projection into another representation | no | no |
| `documentation` | human-readable sidecars that assert no semantic or conformance fact | no | no |

A content class MAY reference a class beneath it and MUST NOT reference one above. Core does not
reference analysis, and neither references an extension — a discriminator names without
referencing, which is why core is allowed that one.

A packet's **canonical** resources are exactly its `core` and `extension` resources. Core is not
the whole of what is canonical: Part 09 sends a task's own facts
— which side the animal licked, whether it was rewarded — to a typed extension, and no computation
over core recovers them. Core is what every consumer can read without an extension registry. Part
02's declaration presence policies, not the content class alone, determine which core resources
every packet has.

A consumer MAY read core alone, and what it reads is complete for every core resource. It MUST NOT
conclude it has read every canonical fact the packet holds; a discriminator resolving `required`
is the case where the omission is guaranteed.

Content class MUST remain independent of abstraction level, namespace, and resource kind.
Resources of different kinds may share a content class, and resources of one kind may occupy
different content classes.

**Namespace** MUST remain the dotted-identifier ownership mechanism. Extension namespaces are
specified in Part 09. **Resource kind** MUST classify a logical resource's form independently of
its content class and physical encoding. Part 02 owns the exact serialized content-class and kind
vocabularies, declaration eligibility, presence, canonicality derivation, and lineage relation. A
packet entry names a declaration and MUST NOT repeat content class, kind, or canonicality.

<a id="ew-packet" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.3 Packet

A **packet** is one concrete dataset bundle under a declared profile. It may be a local directory,
an object-store prefix, or a manifest pointing at distributed assets.

A packet contains a packet manifest, core tables, and any of: dense arrays, media, documents and
sidecars, analysis materializations, and validation and provenance records.

A packet is primarily single-session. Multi-session packets are a permitted extension, and a
packet declares its sessions in the manifest.

<a id="ew-packet-catalog" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.4 Packet manifest

The **packet manifest** is the dataset-level map. In the portable profile it is `packet.json`,
specified in Part 04.

It records which resource instances exist, the logical declarations they instantiate, their
locations, and their instance-level validation and provenance facts. The checked registry
declaration supplies logical kind, content class, canonicality, presence, and provenance policy.
The packet's profile and the declaration's kind derive a generic physical interface under Part
03; format-owned metadata supplies the representation's version and physical features.

<a id="ew-iceberg-catalog" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.5 Iceberg catalog

An **Iceberg catalog** is distinct from the packet manifest. It is relevant only to the lakehouse
profile and manages only tabular resources. It does not catalog dense arrays, media, documents, or
semantic relationships across a packet, and therefore does not replace the packet manifest.

<a id="ew-resource-identity" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.6 Resource identity

Resources and rows reference each other by stable identifier. An implementation MUST NOT use HDF5
object references, Zarr hierarchy paths, host object identity, or file layout as the primary
identity system.

<a id="ew-core-content-class" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. Core content class

<a id="ew-core-content-requirements" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 4.1 Requirements

The core content class MUST contain canonical facts in the logical schemas of Part 02. A writer
publishes a packet, core resources included, only as
[Part 05 §9.2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-error-policy) permits. The required minimum is the closure of Part
02's declaration presence, dependency, populated-reference, and catalog rules; membership in the core class alone does not require an instance.

A fact belongs in core when common interpretation, validation, joining or analysis would break
without it (Part 09 §3). A fact a single domain owns belongs in that domain's extension, typed,
with its own vocabularies and rules — not in core, and not in a free-form column.

Core resources are normalized enough to avoid drift and no further. One row per trial in
`core.intervals`; one row per unit in a sorting and curation context in `core.units`; one row per
spike in `core.spike_events`.

### 4.2 Spike representation

`core.spike_events`, one row per spike with primitive columns only, is the sole canonical spike
representation. The ragged chunked representation of v0.1 is withdrawn, as is the manifest's
`canonical_representations` block.

`core.units` carries `n_spikes`, `first_spike_tick` and `last_spike_tick` as a denormalization that
a validator checks against `core.spike_events`.

<a id="ew-analysis-content-class" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 5. Analysis content class

<a id="ew-analysis-content-requirements" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.1 Requirements

The analysis content class MAY contain denormalized, prejoined, binned, aligned, or otherwise
transformed products. Part 06 owns its exact lineage, realization, and promotion requirements and
its optional run provenance.

Analysis resources are disposable. A writer MAY delete and recompute any of them. An analysis
resource becomes canonical only through an explicit revision of this specification.

<a id="ew-analysis-materialization-criterion" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.2 Materialization is justified by layout, not by arithmetic

A materialization is a physical realization of an analysis resource, not a logical kind. Part 06
defines when analysis views and materializations are justified and how their lineage is recorded.

## 6. Deferred NWB projection

A packet SHOULD preserve enough semantic metadata to support an NWB-like projection later. This
specification does not require PyNWB-compatible object reconstruction, NWB/HDF5 or NWB-Zarr export,
or a complete mapping onto the `NWBFile` hierarchy.

Reserved NWB names are not honoured. A projection is therefore a mapping exercise and not a
renaming.

## 7. Storage stance

### 7.1 Portable profile first

The portable profile is a JSON manifest, Parquet tables, Zarr arrays, ordinary media files, and
text sidecars. It MUST be readable without a server.

<a id="ew-external-resource-referencing" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 7.2 Referencing

A packet need not own every byte it describes. Referencing is expected for large resources: a
resource the packet does not own sets `external: true` and supplies a location. Externality changes
ownership and what can be inspected; it does not move format-owned metadata into the manifest. An
unreachable external resource's physical state remains unassessed.

Externalizing a table costs more than externalizing an array, because a table's validation rules
cannot run at all while it is unreachable.

### 7.3 Iceberg later

Iceberg is a later table-management profile, useful for table snapshots, schema and partition
evolution, concurrent writers, catalog integration, and reproducible release tags. An
implementation of this specification MUST NOT require it.

A physical profile — Iceberg's or any other — MUST declare a `profile_id` that a packet's manifest
names, MUST bind only the packets declaring it, and MUST say how each logical resource of Part 02
is stored. It MUST NOT require an amendment to Part 01 or Part 02; where one would be needed, the
defect is in those parts rather than in the profile. No clause of Part 01 or Part 02 may depend on a
clause of Part 03.

`packet_version` versions the bundle; an Iceberg snapshot would version a table's rows. A writer
MUST NOT increment `packet_version` for a rewrite of rows.

<a id="ew-implementation-thesis" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 8. Implementation thesis

The first implementation is not a generic NWB backend. It is:

1. a specification of core logical resources;
2. a validator generated from that specification;
3. an importer that turns source data into validated resources;
4. a packet manifest recording the committed resources;
5. a small access library querying tables and arrays with standard engines.

An import succeeds only when validation succeeds (Part 05 §1).

## 10. Normative-reference policy and shared references

Part 00 MUST own the specification-wide policy for external normative references. An external
authority that normative prose in exactly one numbered part invokes directly MUST have its
bibliographic entry in a normative-reference section of that part. An external authority that
normative prose in more than one numbered part invokes directly, or that the Part 00 framework
itself invokes, MUST have one shared bibliographic entry in Part 00.

Normative prose invokes an external authority **directly** when it identifies that authority as
the basis for one or more requirements. A citation to another numbered part's Escapewheel contract
is not a direct invocation of the external authorities on which that contract relies.

Each direct external normative authority MUST have exactly one bibliographic entry in the numbered
parts. Each part whose normative prose invokes an external authority directly MUST identify the
applicable entry from the prose that establishes the authority's use and MUST state the extent to
which it applies. That invocation MAY govern a defined group of requirements and need not be
repeated in each requirement. A bibliographic entry identifies the external document and edition;
it MUST NOT become the owner of the Escapewheel meaning, processing rule, actor obligation, or
conformance behavior that invokes it.

An informative mention of a technology MUST NOT make its specification normative. Moving a
bibliographic entry MUST NOT change the referenced behavior, and a reference used by several
parts through direct invocation MUST NOT be copied into several normative-reference sections. The
informational specification README MUST NOT contain a normative-reference section or become an
independent bibliographic owner.

**unversioned** entry MUST be identified as such and follows the linked external specification
during candidate development. A **living-registry** entry identifies a registry that assigns names

as its candidate edition. A numbered part MAY give an authority a living-registry entry only where
the authority is such a registry and no validator verdict depends on its content. A requirement
invoking a living registry refers to the registry as it stood when the bound actor acted; for a
spelling requirement, when the writer wrote the value, so a later registration or renaming leaves
the value conforming.

A specification release MUST identify the exact external edition on which every conformance
requirement depends, and MUST identify each living registry by its entry's name and URI in place of
an edition.

# Part 01 — Semantic Standard

Normative.

<a id="ew-semantic-standard-scope" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Scope

This part defines what the concepts in a packet mean. It does not decide how they are stored.

The semantic standard defined by this part is identified by `escapewheel-semantic-v0.1`.

Every semantic concept MUST be representable in the logical standard (Part 02). The semantic standard itself is independent of Parquet, Zarr, Iceberg, or any other substrate.

## 2. Global semantic rules

### 2.1 Stable identity

Any object referenced by another object MUST have a stable identifier.

An identifier is a signed 32-bit integer. Implementations MUST NOT encode semantic meaning in identifier values, and MUST NOT derive an identifier from a value's position in a file.

Each identifier declares its **identity**:

| identity | definition |
|---|---|
| `carried` | the source's own number, reproduced unchanged |
| `assigned` | chosen by the writer when the packet was built |

An assigned identifier's owning table MUST carry a `*_name` column holding the name it was assigned from, so that a process outside the packet can reference the row without knowing the number.

### 2.2 Time basis

Every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis, and media frame timing record MUST declare a time basis: an integer tick and a `clock_id`.

Floating-point seconds MUST NOT be the canonical representation. Seconds MAY be derived, and are defined from the session's time origin. A consumer lacking that origin MUST refuse to derive seconds rather than assume one.

Where a writer cannot map one of its clocks' observation starts onto the reference, it MUST NOT declare a time origin. An origin it could not compute shifts every second a consumer derives.

A tick is a coordinate, not a label and not a quantity. A writer MUST NOT re-base a source's sample index: for a sample clock, tick 0 is sample 0.

### 2.3 Observation

A session's extent is stated as **wall-clock time**, not as a tick range. A hull over every clock's ticks would need those ticks converted to one clock, and alignment may be anchor-based — outside the first and last anchor the mapping is undefined, which is exactly where a hull reaches. A session therefore declares when it started and MAY declare when it ended, both as instants.

A clock is observing during declared intervals, in that clock's own ticks. Anything timed by a clock inherits that clock's window and MAY refine it, but MUST NOT assert a window derived from its own timestamps.

An interval is **closed**: `start_tick` and `stop_tick` both name observed ticks, so an interval covers `stop - start + 1` ticks and a one-tick interval has `start == stop`.

### 2.4 Provenance

An imported core resource SHOULD be traceable to source data or source activities, and a derived analysis resource MUST declare lineage to the core resources it derives from.

Provenance is a source-file record, a processing-run record, and a link between them. A packet carrying no provenance is conforming; where provenance is present it is validated.

### 2.5 Units of measure

A measured quantity SHOULD declare its unit. A stored integer requiring conversion to physical units MUST declare the conversion.

A unit is declared on the column, not left to the column's name.

### 2.6 Currency

Where several derived results of the same kind and scope coexist, a consumer MUST NOT silently choose between them: it refuses, or reports the ambiguity. A preference is declared, keyed on the producing run, and a consumer selecting derived data for an activity SHOULD use the preferred run where one is declared for the matching scope — a declared preference a consumer ignores is a preference for nobody.

A run declares the scope it is about. A run declaring none is **undeterminable**, which a consumer MUST report separately from ambiguous, and the state is contagious: where more than one run exists for an `activity_type` and **any** of them declares no scope, a consumer MUST NOT conclude the group is unambiguous. The unscoped run may or may not be about the same thing as the others, and nothing in the packet says which.

Where a run supersedes another, the chain MUST be well-founded: a run MUST NOT supersede itself, directly or transitively.

### 2.7 Extension

Lab-specific concepts MAY be added through namespaced extensions (Part 09). An extension MUST NOT alter the meaning of a core concept without a new semantic version.

## 3. Concepts

### 3.1 Dataset

A collection of packets sharing a semantic and logical standard. It is a organizational concept and has no required representation.

### 3.2 Packet

One concrete bundle of resources under a declared physical profile. A packet MUST declare the sessions it contains.

### 3.3 Session

One continuous recording engagement with one subject.

- MUST have an identifier and a subject.
- MUST declare the clock its times are reported on.
- MUST declare its time origin where seconds are to be derived.

### 3.4 Subject

The animal or preparation recorded from.

- MUST have an identifier.
- SHOULD declare species, sex, and date of birth.

### 3.5 Device

A physical instrument used during acquisition.

- MUST have an identifier.
- SHOULD declare manufacturer, model, and serial number where its source states them.
- SHOULD declare the **hardware revision** and **firmware version** it was running.
- SHOULD name the device it was **attached to**, where a source states it.
- SHOULD declare **which port** of that device it occupied, where a source states it.

A serial number identifies the object; its firmware determines what the object does. Two sessions can record the same make, model and serial and need interpreting differently, so a version is not an identifier's decoration but a separate fact.

A consumer MAY walk from a probe to the hardware that acquired it, and MAY group probes by any element of the chain they share — which is the question the chain exists to answer. It MUST NOT infer attachment from a device's name: a name is unconstrained by this standard, and the reference is the statement.

Attachment names a device, and which place on that device a child occupied is a second fact. A device SHOULD declare the port, dock or slot of its parent it attached to, where a source states one. Where it declares none the coordinate is **unrecorded**, and a consumer MUST report it as such rather than parse it out of a name. Two devices sharing a parent and a port are not one device, and nothing declares that they are: the four bases of a quad-base probe occupy a single dock, so a port says where something was plugged in and not what it was.

A device is the **physical object**. Where a source presents several recording entities from one object — the bases of a quad-base probe — each is a Probe and all name the same Device; a writer MUST NOT create a second device row for one instrument. Attachment is stated, never inferred from a name, and the chain MUST terminate: a device MUST NOT be its own ancestor.

A writer records both versions **verbatim** as the source stated them, and MUST NOT normalise, pad, or parse them into components. Neither is ordered: a consumer MAY compare two version strings for equality and MUST NOT compare them for precedence, because different manufacturers' schemes share none.

Where an element of an acquisition chain reports its own versions, it is its own device. A value the source gives as unknown is recorded as the source gave it, which is distinct from a source that said nothing.

This standard names no kinds of device, so a writer MUST record every instrument its source declares — a camera on the same terms as a headstage — and identity is the serial the source states. What a device row does not carry is where an instrument was pointed: a camera's target is a fact about the instrument that `core.devices` has no column for, and it stays in `metadata` until something has to read it.

### 3.6 Probe

A device that carries electrodes.

- MUST belong to a session's device inventory.
- MAY declare manufacturer, part number, and geometry.

### 3.7 Electrode

One recording contact.

- MUST belong to one probe.
- MAY have a position in one or more coordinate frames.

### 3.8 Coordinate

A position of an electrode or unit in a declared frame.

- MUST name a frame the packet declares (§3.24).
- MUST carry one value per axis that frame declares, each on the axis it sits on.
- MUST declare the **basis** of its claim:

| basis | meaning |
|---|---|
| `planned` | where the probe was meant to go; intent, and possibly missed |
| `measured` | where it was, established after the fact by observation |
| `nominal` | where it is by construction; nothing aimed at it and nothing observed it |

The vocabulary is open. A consumer that does not recognise a basis MUST NOT treat the coordinate as `measured`.

A position is as many numbers as its frame has axes, so the values are one row per axis rather than a fixed three. Each value's unit is the axis's, and its meaning is what the axis says it holds.

A coordinate MAY carry an atlas annotation: the structure the position falls in, as an identifier and a label. An electrode is not in a region; a position is, so the annotation belongs to the coordinate rather than to the contact.

Where the identifier is present the label SHOULD be too, so a consumer without the atlas can still read the annotation. A table MUST NOT carry a structure's full name or its ancestry path as columns: both are derivable from the identifier, and an ancestry path is a list, which Part 02 §5.2 forbids in a delimited string.

A validator MUST NOT treat an absent annotation as a finding. Where a position falls outside any declared structure, or no registration covered it, the packet records a fact about the session.

A coordinate SHOULD name the processing run that produced it where the writer knows it. That is provenance — which run produced the row that is there — and not a choice between rows.

### 3.9 Clock

A monotonic counter with a declared rate and origin.

- MUST declare a tick rate, as a rational where the rate is not an integer.
- MAY declare a parent clock, and if so MUST be alignable to it.
- MUST declare either a linear transform or anchor points, never both.
- MAY bind its **tick 0 to a wall-clock instant**, which is the only route from a
  packet's ticks to real time; absent it, ticks relate to each other and to nothing else.
- A counter that resets mid-session is two clocks, one per epoch, each with its own
  identifier, origin and wall-clock binding. A packet MUST NOT represent a reset as a
  discontinuity within one clock: an anchor sequence requires both sides to increase, so there
  is no way to express it and no way to read it.

A clock's tick-zero binding and a session's start time are **independent and MUST NOT be
conflated**. One is a property of a counter, the other a fact about when work began.
Where a wall-clock binding is derived rather than declared, the tick it is anchored on MUST lie
in the same epoch as the instant it is paired with — a binding computed across a reset
describes neither clock.

An importer MUST examine a source in acquisition order and MUST NOT assume monotonicity. A reset is
visible only in the order the samples arrived; sorting first hides it.

A tick's zero is where the clock's declared origin says it is — for a sample clock whose origin is
sample 0 of a recording, tick 0 **is** that sample. A writer MUST NOT re-base a source's sample
index and a consumer MUST NOT assume any base beyond what the origin declares; that
prohibition is what makes a tick exact.

Alignment beyond the terminal anchors is defined only while the estimator's error stays within tolerance, 3 ms at p95 by default. A profile or a recipe MAY state a different tolerance; a writer MUST record the value it used.

A writer reaching past one median anchor interval MUST measure its error and MUST record the median, p95, and maximum, so a consumer can judge the packet by a stricter rule than the writer applied.

A consumer MUST **report** ticks it cannot map rather than omitting them, so a converted table has the same row count as its source with nulls where the mapping is undefined. A row that vanishes in conversion is indistinguishable from one that was never there. It MUST record how many rows it dropped for falling outside the bound **even when that count is zero**: silence is what lets a reader and a writer disagree about which rows exist.

### 3.10 Interval

A bounded span of experimental time within a session.

- MUST belong to one session.
- MUST have start and stop ticks on a declared clock, closed per §2.3.
- MUST declare its **kind**: the resource that specialises it.

A trial is one kind of interval. Blocks, epochs, and stimulation periods are others. What an interval *means* — condition, stimulus, choice, outcome, stimulation parameters — is declared by the specialising resource and MUST NOT be added to the core interval.

### 3.11 Event

An instantaneous or near-instantaneous timestamped observation: a tick, and the stream it was
observed on.

- MUST belong to one session and to one event stream.
- MUST have a tick, on the clock its stream declares.
- MAY carry a structured payload.

An event carries no type and no clock of its own: both are properties of the stream, which emits
one kind of event and declares one clock. An event does not reference an interval. Examples:
stimulus onset, reward delivery, lick, threshold crossing, TTL edge,
synchronization pulse.

### 3.12 Event stream

The context a class of events was observed in: what produced them, on what clock, what kind of
event they are, and where anything further about them lives.

- MUST declare an event type, from an open vocabulary, and the clock its events are timed on.
- MAY name a device, a processing run, and a table specialising its events.

A stream emits **one kind** of event, so a source emitting several on one channel is written as
several streams. What a stream's events carry beyond a tick is that named table's to declare, not
core's: how many marks an event has and of what type is a property of what produced it.

<a id="ew-unit-concept" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.13 Unit

A sorted or curated spiking entity.

- MUST belong to one sorting run and one curation version.
- MAY sit on several electrodes, and MAY have a position in one or more frames.

A unit is not a neuron. It is what a sorting produced.

### 3.13.1 A rate needs a denominator

A firing rate is a count over a duration, so the duration MUST be knowable. Where a unit declares observation intervals, the rate is its spike count over the total of those intervals; where it declares none, it is the count over the recording's extent. A packet SHOULD state which denominator it used, because the two differ for any unit that drifted out and back.

### 3.14 Spike event

One detected action potential of one unit, at one tick on one clock. Spike events are the canonical spiking representation.

### 3.15 Quality label

An assessment of a unit by one scorer. A unit has many, not one.

- MUST name the source that assigned it.
- MAY carry the source's own label verbatim, which is open.
- MAY carry a normalized label from a closed vocabulary.

### 3.16 ElectricalSeries

Sampled extracellular voltage over time and channels, represented as a dense array asset with a declared time axis and channel mapping.

<a id="ew-array-asset-concept" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.17 Array asset

A dense array with declared axes. An axis declares its coordinates; a regularly sampled axis
declares them as an origin, a step, and a length rather than as stored values.

An array asset holds **any** sampled quantity, whatever instrument produced it. This standard names the concepts it defines — §3.16 is the one it defines for extracellular voltage — and it does not enumerate instruments. A packet names its own arrays, and an array names the device that made it (§3.5); a rig fitted with an instrument this standard has never heard of therefore needs no extension, no new concept, and no revision.

`semantic_type` is open. A value this standard does not define is a label and carries no contract, which is the honest state of affairs for a quantity whose meaning lives in the device rather than in the format.

It is also **nullable**, and null is the ordinary case: a writer MUST NOT fill it where the row's declared facts already say what the asset holds. Requiring it is what made it a duplicate — a column carrying no contract that every row must fill is one a writer invents a value for, and `ElectricalSeries` stood on 22 of 25 arrays where the unit, the channel map and the dimensions already said voltage per channel over time. What remains is what the facts cannot express: which statistic of a waveform an array holds, or what a video shows, neither of which any other column carries.

Which **band** of a signal an array holds is a separate question, answered by a column of its own: `wideband`, `ap` and `lfp` are reserved for the corresponding signal bands, the vocabulary is open, and an array not split into bands declares none. A band value is a conventional shorthand for a filter the packet does not record, so a consumer MUST NOT read it as a statement of the filter's corners, and MUST NOT infer a band from an asset's `semantic_type`, its device or its sampling rate — a signal can be decimated without being filtered and filtered without being decimated.

An array asset SHOULD name the device that produced it, where the writer knows which one did. A
consumer MAY then resolve any array to its instrument without knowing what kind of array it is,
and MUST NOT infer an instrument from an asset's `semantic_type` or name: those are labels, and the
device is a reference. Parts 02 and 06 own logical declaration and resource-level provenance;
Parts 03 and 04 own physical encoding, location, and packet binding.

<a id="ew-media-asset-concept" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.18 Media asset

An image or video with frame timing carried in tables.

- MUST have a stable media asset ID.
- SHOULD carry a frame table where synchronization matters.

A frame row is indexed by **presentation order**: row *n* describes the frame the asset presents nth, counting from 0. A writer MUST NOT number frames in the order the container stores them, which differs wherever a codec reorders — a stream with bidirectional frames displaces one by several positions, and the resulting misattribution of time is monotonic and therefore invisible.

The index counts the frames the asset **holds**, not the exposures the capture produced. A frame lost before or during writing gets no row and no reserved index; what went missing is recorded in the columns that carry timing, not as a gap in the numbering.

A frame's time is a tick on a declared clock, like every other time in a packet (§2.1). A container's own **presentation timestamp** is a different quantity and is carried separately: an integer in the container's own time base, verbatim. A writer MUST NOT rescale it to seconds, to a clock's ticks, or to a frame number, and MUST leave it absent where it did not read the container. It is not a time on any clock the packet declares, so a consumer MUST NOT compare it between assets or use it to measure elapsed time; what it locates is the frame inside the file.

A consumer MAY pass a presentation timestamp verbatim to a container library to seek the frame a row describes, which is the exact route and assumes nothing about ordering.

The asset declares the **rate** those timestamps count at, as an exact rational — a container time base of 1/16000 s is 16000 over 1, in the shape a clock's tick rate already uses. A writer that carries presentation timestamps MUST declare it and MUST NOT declare half of it. A consumer MAY then convert a timestamp to seconds of the asset's own timeline without opening the media, which is the case a referenced asset makes ordinary: the file is the part a packet is least likely to have.

A media asset SHOULD name the device that recorded it where its source states one, as an array
asset does. Nothing else separates two such assets: a behaviour video and a movie from a
head-mounted microscope may both be written by a camera, and `semantic_type` and the asset's name
are labels rather than references. Where the source states no device the column is null, and a
consumer MUST report the instrument as unrecorded rather than read one out of the asset's name.
Parts 02–04 own its declaration, physical encoding, location, and packet binding.

### 3.19 Source file

An input the packet was built from.

### 3.20 Processing run

An activity that produced resources, declaring what it was, what it ran on, and the scope it was about.

### 3.21 Sorting run

A processing run that produced units from a recording, declaring the clock its ticks are on.

### 3.22 Curation version

A named state of a sorting's unit set, optionally derived from a parent curation.

### 3.23 Calibration

What someone measured about an instrument so that its numbers can be interpreted: how many microlitres a valve delivers when held open for 30 ms, how much light a fibre emits at a given drive, where a manipulator's own coordinates sit in the rig, where a camera is mounted.

- MUST have an identifier, a name, and a declared type from an open vocabulary.
- MUST name the session it was in force for.
- SHOULD declare when it was measured, where the source states one.
- MAY name the devices it covers, carry the points of its curve, or name the array holding it.

A calibration is a standing property of an instrument rather than an activity of this packet, so a session reports it as **in force** rather than as having happened, and it is not a Processing run. Both packaged sessions cite water calibrations measured 133 and 302 days before the recording, one of them by two sessions six months apart.

One calibration covers many devices — six manipulator modules cite one file and one timestamp between them — so which devices it covers is recorded per device and not as a property of the calibration.

A source states a calibration in at least four ways: as a curve, as an array, as a date and nothing else, and as an assertion that none was made. A calibration with no recorded content is still a calibration, and a consumer MUST read such a row as **unrecorded** rather than as absent. A source stating that no calibration was performed gets no row: the absence of a calibration is not a calibration.

### 3.24 Coordinate frame

The space a coordinate is a position in, together with its axes.

- MUST have a name, which is the session's own name for that space.
- MUST declare one row per axis it has, each carrying the source's own name for what that axis holds.
- MAY name the published atlas the space is, and the version of that atlas.
- MAY declare which way an axis's values increase, and MUST leave that undeclared where the source does not state it.

A frame has as many axes as it has: two for a shank, five for a rendering space. Three was what the column names `x_um`, `y_um` and `z_um` claimed, and of the two packaged sessions' 27,557 coordinate rows, 8,448 are shank positions that leave the third empty.

An axis's name is the source's word for the field its values came from, carried verbatim — `ccf_ml` where the source's electrodes table has a column of that name. It records where the value came from and not what the quantity is conventionally taken to be, so a consumer needing to know which way an axis runs, where no direction is declared, MUST report the coordinate unusable for that purpose rather than assume a convention.

Which published atlas a frame is, is a fact the frame declares rather than a spelling of its name: two packets both naming a frame `ccf` may be registered against CCFv3 and against a successor whose structure ids differ, and comparing names cannot tell them apart. A writer MUST NOT fill the atlas or its version from the name.

A frame belongs to one session, because a name two sessions reuse may name different spaces — the reason `core.devices` is session-scoped.

# Part 02 — Core Logical Schemas

Normative.

<a id="ew-core-logical-schema-scope" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Scope

This part defines how a logical resource is declared, what each kind of declaration contains, and
which core and analysis resources the incorporated registry comprises.

The logical standard defined by this part is identified by `escapewheel-logical-v0.1`.

The column-level definitions themselves are not written here. They are data (§9), carried in a registry document that a packet embeds and that generates the checks of Part 05. This part is the rules the registry obeys; the registry is the schema.

This part expressly incorporates the following artifacts:

| artifact | normative extent |
|---|---|
| `registry/registry.schema.json` | exact local JSON structure and conditional shape constraints |
| `registry/registry_contract.schema.json` | exact structure of a registry-contract descriptor |
| `registry/registry_contracts/escapewheel-registry-contract-v0.2.json` | exact contract selectors, phase order, invariant IDs, applicability, and owning anchors |
| `registry/logical_schemas.json` | exact current core and analysis resource declarations, including table columns, keys, references, vocabularies, and rules |

The generated core registry is what a packet embeds. `registry/tables.md` is a generated,
informative listing of the same declarations. This part owns the declaration model, conformance
algorithms, and meanings. A disagreement among the part and its incorporated artifacts makes the
specification candidate internally inconsistent.

<a id="ew-table-resource-requirements" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Logical resource declarations

Each member name of the root `resources` object is a declaration ID. A declaration ID MUST match
`^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*\.[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`. The core registry is the exact declaration authority for
the `core` and `analysis` namespaces. An extension registry is the sole declaration authority for
the namespace equal to its `extension_id`.

A generated table-only implementation view MAY select table-kind declarations, but it MUST be
derived from `resources` and MUST NOT be serialized as a second authority.

<a id="ew-resource-common-fields" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.1 Common fields

Every resource declaration contains exactly these common fields, plus the kind-specific field §2.3
permits:

- `content_class`, governed by §2.2;
- `kind`, one of `table`, `dense_array`, and `opaque`;
- `instances`, a closed object governed by §2.4;
- `requires`, an array of distinct declaration IDs, empty where there is no explicit dependency;
  and
- `lineage`, one of `forbidden`, `optional`, and `required`.

<a id="ew-resource-content-class" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.2 Content class and canonicality

The `content_class` vocabulary and its consequences are:

| value | meaning | canonical | required by class |
|---|---|:---:|:---:|
| `core` | domain-general normalized facts | yes | no |
| `extension` | typed facts owned by a domain | yes | no |
| `analysis` | products reproducible from canonical resources | no | no |
| `compatibility_projection` | a projection into another representation | no | no |
| `documentation` | human-readable sidecars that assert no semantic or conformance fact | no | no |

Canonicality is derived from this table and MUST NOT be independently declared. Promoting a
resource into the canonical set changes its declaration's content class through a specification
or extension-registry change; an implementation MUST NOT promote it with a Boolean.

A documentation resource MAY describe another resource without creating a dependency or
supplying conformance evidence. A machine-interpreted control artifact does not acquire
documentation semantics merely because its serialization is a document.

The core registry MUST NOT declare `extension`. An extension registry MAY declare `extension`,
`analysis`, `compatibility_projection`, or `documentation` in its own namespace and MUST NOT
declare `core`. Every `analysis` and `compatibility_projection` declaration requires lineage.
Every `documentation` declaration forbids lineage. These three
classes are optional under §2.5.

<a id="ew-resource-kind" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.3 Kind

The logical kind states which generic payload structure, if any, Escapewheel interprets:

| kind | generic payload contract | kind-specific declaration |
|---|---|---|
| `table` | rows and declared columns | required `table` object |
| `dense_array` | an n-dimensional dense array with declared logical axes | required closed `array` object containing `axes_resource` |
| `opaque` | a payload with no generic Escapewheel logical model | none |

A table declaration MUST contain `table`, MUST NOT contain `array`, and MUST use singleton
instances. Its `table` object contains an ordered column list (§3), a primary key or `null`,
references (§7), and Tier 2 rules (§10.2).

A dense-array declaration MUST contain `array`, MUST NOT contain `table`, and MUST use catalog
instances. Its `axes_resource` names a visible singleton table declaration. The axes table MUST
have exactly one binding reference to the array declaration's catalog table: the reference targets
the complete catalog primary key, its local columns lead the axes table's primary key, and that
primary key adds exactly one required structural-index column. Presence of a dense-array instance
makes its axes-table singleton necessary. Part 03 follows this declaration and MUST NOT hardcode an
axes resource.

An opaque declaration contains neither kind-specific object and may use any instance mode. Opaque
qualifies only the generic payload model. Escapewheel still identifies, locates, integrity-checks,
and records provenance for the resource; related declarations and tables may describe its
scientific meaning. Generic code is not thereby required to parse, decode, or render its payload.

Stored materialization and view are realizations, not kinds. `view`, `materialized_table`,
`dense_array_asset`, `media_asset`, and `document_asset` MUST NOT be registry kinds.

<a id="ew-resource-instance-model" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.4 Instance modes

An `instances` object matches exactly one closed form:

| mode | required fields | packet binding |
|---|---|---|
| singleton | `mode: "singleton"`; `presence` is `required` or `optional` | the declaration ID is the only permitted packet `resource_id` |
| catalog | `mode: "catalog"`; `catalog_resource`; `resource_id_column` | zero or more resources bind one-to-one to rows of the named singleton table |
| family | `mode: "family"` | zero or more resources bind only by declaration ID |

A family resource ID is packet-unique and dotted within a namespace the declaration's registry
owns. A catalog resource and column satisfy §2.6.

<a id="ew-resource-presence" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.5 Presence and dependencies

`core.sessions` is a required singleton. Every other currently incorporated table is an optional
singleton. An extension selects required or optional for each singleton; binding that extension
registry in a packet under [Part 04 §8](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-registry-bindings)
activates its required declarations. Merely installing a registry does not.

A packet contains an instance when any of these conditions applies:

- it is a required singleton in an active registry;
- a present declaration names its singleton declaration in `requires`;
- a populated table reference targets its singleton table declaration; or
- a catalog row requires that exact family instance under §2.6.

An absent optional singleton creates no empty table. A present table may contain zero rows unless
another rule requires rows. Catalog and family declarations have no minimum instance count.

Each `requires` member resolves under the same visibility and direction rules as a reference and
names a singleton. A present instance requires the transitive closure of those dependencies.
Dependency cycles are permitted only where every member is a singleton; presence of one then
requires the entire cycle.

<a id="ew-resource-catalog-binding" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.6 Catalog binding

A catalog declaration names a visible singleton table declaration and one column. That column is a
required string identifier with carried identity and free vocabulary. An extension catalog may
name a core table or one in the same extension; a core catalog MUST NOT name an extension table.
Within one visible core-and-extension context, no two catalog declarations may name the same
table-and-column pair.

For every catalog row, a conforming packet contains exactly one entry whose `resource_id` equals
the row's catalog resource-ID value and whose `declaration_id` names that catalog family. Every
entry in the family matches exactly one row. Catalog resource-ID values and packet resource IDs are
unique.

`core.array_assets`, `analysis.array_assets`, and `core.media_assets` each carry the required
string identifier `resource_id`, with carried identity and free vocabulary. For this exact field,
§4's general integer `_id` rule does not apply. The assigned `array_asset_id` and `media_asset_id`
remain row identity, and the corresponding names remain source-label bridges. These three tables
carry neither `uri` nor `format`; location belongs to Part 04 and physical encoding to Part 03.

<a id="ew-resource-provenance-policy" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 2.7 Lineage policy

The `lineage` field governs the packet entry's `derived_from`. `forbidden` requires absence,
`optional` permits presence, and `required` requires presence. A present lineage is a nonempty array of
distinct existing packet resource IDs.

Lineage edges obey this relation:

| derived class | permitted source classes |
|---|---|
| `core` | `core` |
| `extension` | visible `core` and same-extension `extension` |
| `analysis` | `core`, `extension`, `analysis` |
| `compatibility_projection` | `core`, `extension`, `analysis` |
| `documentation` | none |

The lineage graph contains no self-edge or cycle. Because every analysis resource has nonempty
lineage, every finite analysis chain terminates in canonical core or extension resources. A writer
and validator apply this policy without regard to kind. A reader may expose the resolved facts
without validating the registry or possessing a validation receipt for ordinary access.

<a id="ew-column-declaration" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 3. Column declaration

Every column MUST declare a name, a type, a presence, and a role. The role and type determine which
other fields the column requires or permits.

| attribute | required | clause |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | yes | §4 |
| `type` | yes | §5 |
| `presence` | yes | §6 |
| `role` | yes | §8.1 |
| `identity` | for identifiers | §8.2 |
| `finite` | for `float32` and `float64` columns | §5.4 |
| `enum` | for closed vocabularies | §9.3 |
| `resolves`, `discriminates`, `positions_in`, `discriminates_via` | as applicable for discriminators | §8.3 |
| `unit`, `unit_from`, `clock_from` | where applicable | §8.4 |
| `authority` | where named nomenclatures cover the column | §9.3 |
| `description` | for an open vocabulary | §9.3 |

A description MUST NOT contradict or override a machine declaration. Concise, non-duplicative
wording remains authoring style rather than a registry-conformance comparison of English text.
The one description compared literally is the standard `metadata` description in §8.6.

<a id="ew-logical-column-naming" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. Naming

Resource names and column names MUST be lowercase snake case. Registry conformance enforces these
relations in both directions where stated:

- an identifier has an `_id` name, and an `_id` name declares an integer identifier, except that
  the exact catalog field `resource_id` is a string identifier with carried identity;
- a coordinate has a `_tick` name, and a `_tick` name declares a coordinate;
- a structural index has an `_index` name, and an `_index` name declares a structural index or
  sequence position;
- an `_name` name declares a string; and
- an `_uri` name, or the bare name `uri`, declares a string.

A registry checker determines discriminator behavior from its declaration and MUST NOT infer it
from a `_kind` suffix. Column order, adjacency, reserved-word preferences, plural-name preferences,
and the vocabulary of same-spelled columns are authoring conventions rather than registry
conformance requirements.

A source-provenance label — `local_electrode_label`, `local_channel_label`, `local_unit_label` — is a `string` carrying whatever base the producing software used, and a consumer MUST NOT use it as an offset into any packet resource.

## 5. Type system

### 5.1 Permitted types

A column's type MUST be one of:

```text
string  int32  int64  float32  float64  bool  timestamp  date  json_string
```

### 5.2 No nested types

A column's type MUST NOT be `list`, `struct`, or `map`. A repeated value becomes a child table; a semi-structured value becomes a `json_string` in an optional column.

This keeps a conforming reader able to satisfy every required column without a JSON parser, and keeps a native reader viable in a language without an embedded SQL engine.

A value that varies per row and is queried belongs in a typed column of a specialising resource; a value constant across the table belongs once, on the table that owns the whole. What may go in the two `json_string` columns core declares is §8.6 for `metadata` and §12.6 for `core.processing_runs.parameters`.

### 5.3 Fixed representations

A writer MUST NOT use wraparound, truncating or saturating semantics for a value whose role is `identifier`, `coordinate`, `structural_index` or `sequence_position`, nor for arithmetic over interval bounds. Where a conversion's result would fall outside the declared type, it MUST fail before the value is written, naming the column and the value. A validator is not required to re-derive this: a wrapped value is a valid value of its type and the evidence is gone by the time rules run.

Tick columns MUST be `int64`. Wall-clock columns MUST be `timestamp` with microsecond precision in
UTC. Identifier columns MUST be `int32`, except where the number of distinct values may exceed
`int32`, where the column MUST be `int64`, and except the catalog `resource_id` defined by §2.6.
No core table is near the integer bound; an extension declaring an integer identifier over more
than 2^31 rows declares `int64` for it.

<a id="ew-finite-values" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.4 Finite values

A value of a `float32` or `float64` column is **non-finite** when it is NaN, positive infinity, or
negative infinity. Every other non-null value of those types is finite, and null is neither.

Every column whose type is `float32` or `float64` MUST declare `finite`, a boolean, and a column of
any other type MUST NOT declare it. There is no default, so an absent declaration is a defect
rather than a claim.

- `finite: true` declares that the column holds no non-finite value. A packet MUST NOT hold a
  non-finite value in such a column.
- `finite: false` admits non-finite values. A Tier 2 rule reading such a column sees them in the
  order [Part 11 §5](11_tier_2_rule_language.md#ew-tier-2-deterministic-semantics) defines.

A NaN is therefore a value only of a float column declaring `finite: false`. An absent value remains
null under [Part 03 §5.6](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-one-null), so a source's NaN or
infinity meaning that a value was not recorded or not computable becomes null in any float column.

## 6. Presence

A column is `required`, `nullable` or `optional`:

| state | the column | its values |
|---|---|---|
| `required` | MUST be present | MUST be non-null in every row |
| `nullable` | MUST be present | MAY be null |
| `optional` | MAY be absent | MAY be null where present |

A conforming reader must be able to satisfy every required column without a JSON parser, so a
column holding `json_string` is always optional.

A present nullable column is preferred to an absent one, because a stable column set simplifies query code. A schema MUST NOT declare a column that duplicates what the data already declares: `core.array_assets` carries no dtype or shape, because the array declares them.

Duplication is permitted only where it buys otherwise-unavailable access. Axis names are carried in `core.array_dimensions` because a Zarr binding in another language cannot see them.

<a id="ew-logical-references" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. References

A table declares each foreign key as a reference naming the target resource, this table's columns, and the target's columns. A reference MAY be composite, nullable, or self-referencing.

Where a reference has a populated source value, the packet MUST contain the target singleton table
resource. This presence consequence is in addition to row-level foreign-key resolution.

A reference's source and target lists MUST be nonempty, contain no duplicate member, and have equal
arity. Every source and target member MUST resolve. The target list MUST equal the target table's
complete, non-null primary key in declared order.

Each paired column MUST have the same type and role and, where it is an identifier, the same
identity. A data column paired with a discriminator as the declared shared key is the sole role
exception. A join is on values: columns of different types resolve to each other not at all, and
relating an assigned identifier to a carried one resolves to nothing.

Where §5.3 requires `int64` of an identifier whose distinct values may exceed `int32`, every column referencing it is `int64` too — the escape is taken on both sides or on neither, since a narrow column referencing a wide one truncates silently.

<a id="ew-session-scope" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 7.1 Session scope

A table is **session-scoped** exactly where it has a session-preserving reference path ending at
`core.sessions`; the zero-edge path makes `core.sessions` the base case. A session-scoped table
MUST carry a required `int32` identifier named `session_id` with assigned identity. A table without
such a path MUST NOT carry that column.

A reference is a session-preserving edge only where:

1. its source contains `session_id` exactly once and maps it at the same position to the target's
   `session_id`;
2. every source member is required;
3. the target is the complete non-null primary key required by §7; and
4. every pair satisfies §7's type, role, and identity compatibility rules.

The path MUST consist only of session-preserving edges and MUST terminate at
`core.sessions.session_id`. Graph reachability, a cycle, or a reference carrying a different
identifier does not prove session scope. Packet validation enforces the ordinary foreign-key
anti-join at every edge and need not execute a recursive row query.

A writer fills the column from the parent row it already read, or from the session it was constructed with, and MUST NOT infer it from a name: a file path and a packet directory both spell a session, and neither is a statement this specification constrains.

## 8. Column semantics

### 8.1 Role

Every column MUST declare exactly one role, from a closed vocabulary with no default:

| role | meaning |
|---|---|
| `data` | a value to be read |
| `identifier` | a stable identity, per §8.2 |
| `coordinate` | a position on an axis, such as a tick |
| `structural_index` | a zero-based offset into a stored sequence |
| `sequence_position` | an ordinal within an ordered set |
| `discriminator` | a value naming the resource another column resolves against |

The role is what makes index-base conversion derivable from a packet rather than hard-coded in each one-based client. A `structural_index` converts at the client boundary; a `coordinate` does not.

### 8.1.1 Index base

A **structural index** addresses a position in a stored resource and MUST be a **0-based offset**. A client converting to a 1-based language MUST convert at the boundary; the format picks one base and clients adapt, because an offset whose base changes per reader is not an offset.

A **source label** is copied verbatim from acquisition or sorting software. Its base is whatever that software used, and a consumer MUST NOT use it as an offset into any packet resource.

A **coordinate** is neither: copied verbatim like a source label, but used arithmetically, which a source label MUST NOT be. Its zero is where its clock's origin says it is.

Because a reference relates columns of the same role (§7), converting every structural index at a boundary is join-preserving by construction.

<a id="ew-identifier-semantics" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 8.2 Identity

An `identifier` column MUST declare `carried` or `assigned`. A table owns an assigned identifier
exactly where the identifier is in its primary key and is not a source member of a reference the
table declares. For every owned assigned identifier, the table MUST declare exactly one
corresponding name formed by replacing `_id` with `_name`. The name MUST be a string data column
and MUST NOT declare `identity`.

A table that merely carries or references an assigned identifier MUST NOT declare that same-base
`*_name` column. A carried identifier likewise gets no corresponding name column: its value is
already the source's own identity.

An assigned identifier's `*_name` MUST be unique within its packet — the 16 name columns in core and analysis that pair with one, not the 6 that name a kind of thing and are expected to repeat, such as `core.sorting_runs.sorter_name`. `registry/check.py` sorts them and requires the second kind to be declared. No rule can check it: a writer numbering an assigned identifier per distinct name emits one row per name, so two things wrongly sharing one are already merged by the time the packet exists — and that merge is indistinguishable from the correct case where four streams naming one serial become one device. The obligation falls on whatever composes the name.

<a id="ew-discriminator-semantics" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 8.3 Discriminator

A `discriminator` column MUST be a `string` holding a dotted resource identifier — a namespace and
a table name, each lowercase snake_case — naming a table in the packet's registry or in one of its
extensions.

**Which column carries it.** A discriminator MAY be declared on the table whose rows it
specialises, or on a table those rows reach through one declared reference. Where it is not on the
specialised table it MUST declare `discriminates_via`, holding the referencing column as a dotted
`resource.column`. That named column MUST belong to a resource declaring exactly one reference in
which the column is a source member and the discriminator's table is the target. This is a reverse
one-hop declaration from the specialised rows to the discriminator.

Where a column carries a discriminator's value as part of a shared key, it is **not** a discriminator and
MUST NOT declare the role: it resolves nothing a consumer of that row does not already know. Such a
column is `data`, and a reference relating it to the discriminator it copies is the one role
mismatch a reference may carry.

**Whether it must resolve.** A discriminator MUST declare `resolves`, required and with no default:

| `resolves` | an unresolvable value is |
|---|---|
| `required` | a defect: the row is uninterpretable |
| `optional` | a fact: the packet does not hold that resource |

Where a discriminator is paired with a value column, the two columns have the same `required` or
`nullable` presence; neither may be optional. Their values MUST agree on presence — a row declares
both or neither. A row declaring neither is **undeterminable**, which a
consumer MUST report apart from a row whose value resolves to nothing: the fix for one is to record
the missing half, and for the other to correct what it names.

**How it resolves.** A reader SHOULD offer the resolution: read the kind, take that resource's key
from the registry, join. A discriminator nothing reads is a free-form name with extra steps, which
is the observed fate of the mechanism this one replaces. Three shapes exist:

- **Shared key.** The discriminated row and the named resource share a key, so the join is on that
  key. A resource a shared-key discriminator may name MUST declare a reference back to the
  discriminated table, and that reference MUST cover the discriminated table's **whole key**. A
  reference on part of it targets a set that is not unique — it identifies a row of some kind,
  which is not a foreign key, and the generated anti-join passes while resolving to several rows.
  A detail table therefore carries the discriminator's value as a constant column, restating in
  every row what its own identity implies; a column holding one value costs almost nothing once
  encoded, and what it buys is a reference that identifies a row. The reference runs extension →
  core, so a discriminator names downward while the integrity constraint points upward.
- **Paired value.** A separate column holds an identifier in the named resource's key space, and
  the discriminator MUST declare `discriminates` naming that column. A paired value holds one
  identifier, so the named resource's primary key MUST be a single column, and the value MUST be a
  `string` matching that key where it is `carried` or its `*_name` where it is `assigned`.
- **By position.** The named resource's rows are the positions the discriminated column indexes,
  located by a column that resource declares. Resolution is neither a key join nor a value-to-key
  join, so the joinability rules above do not apply, and a consumer MUST NOT read them as applying. The
  discriminator MUST declare `positions_in`, naming the column of its own table whose value is that
  position column, and MUST NOT declare it beside `discriminates`. Without the
  declaration this shape and the shared key are indistinguishable, and the exclusion above binds a
  reader while a generator reads every discriminator without a paired value as shared-key.

**What is generated.** Where `resolves` is `required`, two checks follow from the structure: a
forward anti-join against the resources the manifest declares, and a reverse check per named kind
returning every row of the discriminating table whose key has no counterpart in the resource its
discriminator names, both at **error**. The reverse check is generated over the kinds a packet's
rows actually name, and applies to the shared-key shape alone. Where `resolves` is `optional`,
neither is generated and an unresolvable value is reported as `info`.

A by-position discriminator gets a third instead of the reverse one: the column `positions_in`
names MUST hold a column of the named resource whose role is `structural_index`, and the registry
supplies each named resource's such columns as literals. A role is invisible to a
query and visible to whatever writes one.

Every generated check MUST be built over **every resource the packet declares, extensions
included**. Building them over core alone lets an extension kind match no branch and pass in
silence, which is the one case the mechanism exists to cover.

Core MAY name an extension's resource through a discriminator. It MUST NOT reference one: naming
does not create a dependency, so core stays checkable alone, an absent target is a reportable fact,
and no integrity constraint couples the versions.

A row's kind-specific facts live in a table sharing that row's key: the detail table's primary key
MUST begin with the discriminated
table's own identifier. `core.devices` names such a table through `device_kind`, and
core MUST NOT declare one — Part 01 §3.17 makes this standard name no instruments, so a camera's
target or a thermistor's range belongs to an extension. A fact no detail table
models stays in `metadata`, and what justifies promoting one out of it is a rule or a
query that has to read it.

<a id="ew-unit-and-clock-context" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 8.4 Unit and clock

A `data` column carrying a physical quantity SHOULD declare its `unit`, or `unit_from` naming a column that carries it. A `coordinate` column MUST declare `clock_from` where the clock its ticks are on is not the one that row's `clock_id` names, and MUST declare it where the table has no `clock_id` at all. Measured across the registry, 6 of 20 coordinates declare one and the other 14 resolve to their own row's clock; none has neither.

`unit_from` and `clock_from` each name a column of the same table or a one-hop
`resource.column`. The latter form MUST resolve through exactly one reference the table declares.
A consumer resolves the source rather than guessing.

A **unit code** is an expression in the case-sensitive syntax of UCUM version 2.2, revision date
2024-06-17, as §13 identifies it. A **unit column** is a string column whose `authority` entries
include `https://ucum.org/ucum`. A column's literal `unit` MUST be a unit code, and a packet MUST
hold a unit code in every non-null value of a unit column. An annotation in curly braces, such as
`{adc}`, is a unit code and denotes a dimensionless quantity, which is the unit of a quantity
normalised by something the packet measured.

`unit` and `unit_from` are mutually exclusive. A `unit_from` target MUST be a unit column. For a
coordinate, the clock source is its explicit `clock_from` target where present and otherwise the
same table's `clock_id`. That source MUST be an identifier participating in a reference to
`core.clocks.clock_id`. `clock_from` MUST NOT occur on another role.

A consumer MAY label a quantity with its declared unit, and MUST NOT silently compare or combine two columns whose declared units differ. One vocabulary is what makes that rule run: `volts` and `V` differ while naming one unit, so without it the rule refuses a legitimate comparison exactly as readily as it catches a wrong one.

<a id="ew-functional-dependence" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 8.5 Functional dependence

A column MAY declare `determined_by`, naming the columns whose values fix its own: two rows agreeing
on every column named agree on this one, and an empty list declares a constant. Every member MUST
resolve to a column of the same table, and no member may occur twice. The declaration is
structural, decidable from the registry with no packet in hand.

It is a statement about values and holds under any profile. What a profile does with it is the profile's: Part 03 §5.3 uses it to decide whether a table's stored order settles its primary key, and requires the scan establishing that order to verify the declaration against rows, because a wrong one waives a check rather than failing one.

<a id="ew-free-form-metadata" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 8.6 Free-form metadata

A column named `metadata` MUST be an optional `json_string` data column with this exact
description: “What the source stated about this row that the standard does not model, carried in
the source's own vocabulary and read by no rule.”

A table's registry entry MUST declare `metadata` where its primary key includes an `identifier` column that is not part of a declared reference, MUST NOT declare one where every key column is part of a declared reference, and MAY declare one otherwise. An identifier of the table's own is what makes a row a thing rather than a statement about other things; a key made entirely of other tables' identifiers makes it a relationship, and nothing can be said about a relationship that is not a fact about one of its ends. Seventeen core tables fall in the first band and three in the second. Both bounds are decidable from the registry with no packet in hand, and `registry/check.py` enforces both against core and against every extension.

A validator SHOULD report, at `info`, a declared `metadata` column that no row of the packet fills. Where the key neither requires the column nor forbids it, declaring one is a judgement, and this is the evidence against which it is revisited: `core.spike_events` carried the column on 286,896,563 rows that no adapter ever filled.

A writer MAY fill `metadata` on every row of a table that declares it. Parquet reads the columns a query projects and no others, so the column costs a consumer who does not ask for it 1.9 kB of extra footer over 5,000,000 rows, and one who does 394.6 ms against 29.6 ms.

A writer MUST NOT emit a `metadata` key holding a fact that a column of the logical schema holds, unless the duplication buys access that is otherwise unavailable, and MUST NOT emit one that contradicts such a column under any circumstance. The same otherwise-unavailable-access exception applies throughout the schema, so the bag is exactly as strict as the schema around it; a contradiction takes no exception, since it buys nothing by definition. A fact required to interpret the data belongs in a column, never in a key.

No rule checks either half and none is added. The content is schemaless by design, so a constraint on it is unenforceable in principle rather than merely in practice, and an adapter names a key in the source's vocabulary where core names a column in its own — across both packaged sessions, zero of the 20 distinct keys share a name with any column in the registry. The obligation binds the writer, which is the only party that knows what it put in the bag.

A writer SHOULD put prose in a prose column where the table has one, and not in `metadata`. A human-readable string has no structure to preserve, so a JSON envelope costs a serialisation, puts the value where no rule reads it and no filter uses it, and buys none of the otherwise-unavailable access the exception above requires.

### 8.7 Coded values

A writer that can decode a source's code MUST store the decoded value, declaring the vocabulary it decodes onto as §9.3 requires of any other. A code is a token standing for something the emitting system names elsewhere, and a packet holding one without the key leaves no rule, query or reader able to tell one value from another.

A writer that cannot decode one stores the code itself, verbatim, in a `string` column declaring an open vocabulary, whose description names where the key lives. An undecoded code is a label carrying no contract, and an open vocabulary is how this standard declares one (Part 01 §3.17); an integer column declares nothing of the sort, since §9.3 permits a vocabulary on no other type and a code stored as an integer is indistinguishable from a count.

A source's own token is a string, kept as the source emitted it, and a consumer MUST NOT use it as an offset. This applies to a scorer's label and to a source-provenance label alike.

No rule checks either half, and none is added. Nothing structural tells a code from a count, so the obligation binds the writer, which is the party that knows whether it had a key.

## 9. The registry

<a id="ew-registry-document" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.1 The schema is one document

Resource declarations, table structure, and validation rules are carried in one JSON document. Every registry document
MUST carry `$schema`, `registry_id`, `registry_version`, `logical_standard_version`,
`rule_language`, and `resources`, and MUST NOT carry `tables`.

`registry_id` names the document to a packet that binds it and matches `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`. A core
registry's `registry_id` MUST be `core`. An extension registry's `registry_id` MUST equal its
`extension_id`, and therefore MUST NOT be `core`, `analysis`, or `nwb`.

A core registry MUST carry `semantic_standard_version` and MUST NOT carry `extension_id` or
`extends`. An extension registry MUST carry `extension_id` and MUST NOT carry
`semantic_standard_version` or `extends`. The presence of `extension_id` determines the document
class; caller context MUST NOT redefine it.

The selected rule language applies atomically to every Tier 2 rule. A rule MUST NOT repeat or
override it, and one document MUST NOT mix languages. Root, resource, instance, array, table,
column, reference, authority, and rule objects are closed. Registry-build `derived_from` and `since` fields are therefore
invalid; this does not affect the packet manifest's resource-lineage `derived_from`.

<a id="ew-registry-self-description" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.2 Self-description

A packet binds each registry that declares its resources under
[Part 04 §8](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-registry-bindings), and
[Part 03 §3.3](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-portable-bound-registries) owns how the portable
profile stores a bound registry.

The incorporated registry meta-schema has `$id`
`https://escapewheel.org/schemas/registry/0.7.0`. Every registry document conforming to that
schema MUST carry that value in `$schema`. The incorporated core registry has `registry_id: core`,
`registry_version: 0.7.0`, `semantic_standard_version: escapewheel-semantic-v0.1`,
`logical_standard_version: escapewheel-logical-v0.1`, and
`rule_language: escapewheel-rule-sql-v0.1`.

An extension's `registry_version` identifies that extension registry and advances independently of
the core registry's version. An extension conforming to the incorporated meta-schema MUST carry
the full `escapewheel-logical-v0.1` logical-standard identifier and MUST NOT carry `extends`.
The core specification does not assign an extension's version. An extension registry migrated to
meta-schema 0.7.0 MUST carry a `registry_version` different from the one it carried under 0.6.0.
No checker verifies this, since a checker reads only the document before it and not the version
that document replaced.

A checker selects schemas and contracts only from local artifacts and MUST NOT dereference an
identifier. An old registry document MUST NOT be silently interpreted under the new meta-schema
identifier. These development identifiers MUST NOT later be reused for an incompatible contract.

<a id="ew-registry-vocabularies" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.3 Vocabularies

Every `string` column declares a `vocabulary`, whatever its role, except a discriminator, whose value space §8.3 fixes; a column of any other type declares none. There is no default, so an absent declaration is a defect rather than a claim of openness.

| value | what it says |
|---|---|
| `closed` | the column also declares `enum`, and a validator MUST reject a value outside it |
| `open` | the values name kinds of thing, comparable between packets; a validator MUST NOT reject an unrecognized one, except as stated below for a unit column, and the column MAY declare `reserved_values` |
| `free` | not a vocabulary — a name, a description, a URI, a digest, a version string, a person; nothing may be reserved and no report is due |

Four columns are closed:

| column | values |
|---|---|
| `core.processing_runs.status` | `succeeded`, `failed`, `partial` |
| `core.units.quality_label` | `sua`, `mua`, `noise` |
| `core.unit_quality_labels.normalized_label` | `sua`, `mua`, `noise` |
| `core.subjects.sex` | `male`, `female`, `hermaphrodite`, `other` |

A scorer's own `label` is open, because aggregating over scorers is incompatible with one closed set.

A value MAY be reserved only where it is read **whole**: where a consumer comparing two packets learns something from the value alone, without knowing what either packet was doing. Where an external nomenclature defines the values instead, the registry names it rather than copying a set the standard does not itself define. A validator SHOULD report a value outside a reserved set at `info`, and MUST NOT fail on one.

An `open` column MUST carry a nonempty description stating the form its values take. A string data
column with an open or closed vocabulary MAY declare `authority`, a nonempty array whose entries
each carry a human-readable `name` and an absolute `uri` as RFC 3986
([Part 00 §10](00_architecture.md#ew-normative-references)) defines one. No URI may occur twice in one
array. An authority URI is identity rather than a fetch instruction: a validator MUST NOT
dereference one when checking a registry or a packet, and MUST NOT reject a value for being absent
from its column's authority. Neither this rule nor the `open` row above covers a unit column's value
that is not a unit code, which §8.4 makes nonconforming, because UCUM defines its codes by a grammar
rather than a list. An entry named `UCUM` MUST
use `https://ucum.org/ucum`, and an entry using that URI MUST be named `UCUM`.

Where a writer can identify the name that a nomenclature in an open column's `authority` registers
for the thing a value names, it MUST write that name as the nomenclature spells it. Otherwise, as
for a strain not yet registered or a source value matching no registered name, a writer MUST write
the value as the source states it. A validator verifies neither obligation, since it neither
dereferences an authority nor rejects a value for being absent from one. A unit column's value is
the exception: §8.4 requires it to be a unit code whatever the source states, and a validator
checks it by UCUM's grammar under [Part 05 §5.3](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-validation-shim).
[§13](#ew-logical-schema-normative-references) identifies each nomenclature the core registry names
in this way, and the edition that applies.

A closed column's `enum` is its whole value set and spells every value it admits. On a closed
column, `authority` records where the `enum`'s spelling came from and invokes no nomenclature.

A column's declarations are rendered in `registry/tables.md` from the fields holding them — the vocabulary, the closed set itself, the reserved values, the authorities by name, and a discriminator's `resolves`. That document is what a person reads to learn what a column is, and it showed none of them, which is why 35 descriptions had come to say them in prose.

<a id="ew-registry-conformance" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.4 Registry-document conformance

The incorporated registry-contract descriptor enumerates one complete, language-neutral
conformance contract. Its closed root contains `$schema`, `id`, `registry_schema`,
`semantic_standard`, `logical_standard`, `rule_languages`, `phases`, and `invariants`. Each closed
invariant entry contains exactly `id`, `phase`, `classes`, and `anchors`. The descriptor contains
identities and applicability only; it MUST NOT contain algorithms, executable expressions,
messages, exception lists, or implementation selectors. The prose in this part and Part 09 owns
the algorithms and meanings.

The incorporated descriptor has ID `escapewheel-registry-contract-v0.4` and selects the 0.7.0
meta-schema. That meta-schema MUST declare `finite` as a boolean member of a column, require it
where `type` is `float32` or `float64`, and forbid it otherwise; the `shape` phase therefore decides
§5.4's declaration rule, and the descriptor adds no invariant for it. A checker MUST evaluate its
phases in this order:

1. `json`;
2. `schema_selection`;
3. `shape`;
4. `contract_selection`;
5. `semantic`;
6. `rule_static`; and
7. `external_domain`.

A byte-oriented entry point MUST decode UTF-8 JSON and reject every duplicate object member. A

After JSON decoding, a checker MUST inspect only root `$schema` to select a local meta-schema. A
missing or non-string selector makes the document invalid; an unknown selector makes evaluation

extension, the tuple substitutes the required core context's semantic-standard identifier. No
component may be inferred or ignored. An unknown or ambiguous tuple makes evaluation unsupported.
A descriptor ID and a tuple that selected a contract MUST NOT be reinterpreted after publication.

Without usable core context, an extension's visible class, registry-schema, logical-standard, and
rule-language components MUST still select one unambiguous local descriptor before local checks
run. No match or more than one match is unsupported. One match supplies the invariant inventory;
only core-dependent evaluation is incomplete. An extension's core context is usable only where it
selected the same contract, is valid, and declares the extension's logical-standard identifier.
An absent, unsupported, incomplete, invalid, or mismatched core context makes core-dependent
extension phases incomplete, not invalid. Shape and available extension-local invariants still
run, and an independent invalid result dominates incompleteness.

Applicable semantic invariants run in descriptor order. Tier 2 rules then receive Part 11 static
validation. External lexical-domain validation runs last and offline.

The complete result contains the selected descriptor ID where selection succeeded, exactly one
overall state from `valid`, `invalid`, `unsupported`, and `incomplete`, and one record for every

`incomplete`, and `skipped`, and ordered outcome records for each check that did not pass and each

An outcome record contains its state and stable schema, invariant, or rule-language outcome ID;
the owning resource or rule ID where applicable; and an RFC 6901 instance pointer. A skipped

### 9.5 Registry-result serialization

A portable registry result MUST validate against the incorporated closed Draft 2020-12 schema
`registry/registry_result.schema.json`, whose identifier is
`https://escapewheel.org/schemas/registry-result/0.1.0`. It contains the selected descriptor ID, or
null when contract selection did not succeed; the complete document state; and exactly the seven
phase records in the order defined in §9.4.

Each phase record contains its phase, state, and outcomes. A `skipped` phase also contains a stable
reason; another phase MUST NOT contain a phase reason. A `passed` or `skipped` phase has no
outcomes. An `invalid`, `unsupported`, or `incomplete` phase has at least one outcome.

Each outcome contains its state, stable ID, and instance JSON Pointer. It may contain its owner,
reason, schema-location JSON Pointer, and informative diagnostic detail. A skipped outcome contains
a stable reason. The `instance` and `schema_location` fields conform to RFC 6901. Diagnostic
`detail` does not affect conformance, equality, or ordering.

The incorporated schema owns this representation's structure. Section 9.4 owns phase algorithms,
state precedence, applicability and ordering of outcomes, reason and outcome semantics, and when
the descriptor ID is null.

## 10. Validation rules

<a id="ew-tier-1-rules" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 10.1 Tier 1: generated from structure

A validator MUST generate, from the declaration alone: required-column non-null checks, foreign-key anti-joins, and closed-vocabulary membership. A nullable foreign key is guarded with a null test.

A validator MUST also generate, from the declaration alone, for every column declaring
`finite: true`, a check that reports each row holding a non-finite value (§5.4) in that column, at
level `error`, under the check ID `<declaration_id>.<column>.finite`, such as
`core.clocks.tick_rate_hz.finite`. Where the column is `optional` and a table omits it, the check
reports nothing. The finding belongs to the registry holding the declaration under
[Part 05 §9.5](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-verdict-composition), and
[Part 05 §5.4](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-validation-extensions) applies the check to the columns
an extension declares.

A validator MUST establish that a table's primary key is unique, by query or by any means a physical profile defines as establishing it. That a key is unique holds under any profile and is this part's; how a validator may establish it depends on how rows are stored, and is the profile's.

<a id="ew-tier-2-rules" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 10.2 Tier 2: declared

A rule the structure cannot imply declares `id`, `level`, `message`, and `sql`, or uses a named
template that the registry build expands to those fields. Its registry-level `rule_language`
selects the interpretation of `sql`; [Part 11](11_tier_2_rule_language.md) defines the current
language, relation mapping, and finding relation. A rule is logically attached to the resource
whose `rules` array contains it, but it may name other resources the selected language permits.

Rule IDs MUST be unique within a registry document. A rule held by `namespace.table` MUST have the
exact ID `namespace.table.local_rule_id`, where `local_rule_id` is one lowercase snake-case
identifier containing no dot.

A rule's SQL MUST NOT reference a column whose presence is `optional`. A rule is executed against the file, and `optional` permits the column to be absent from it (§6), so a rule naming one does not miss a check — the query fails to parse, and the run meant to find defects reports an error of its own. A constraint that wants an optional column either makes it `nullable`, which declares it always present and sometimes empty, or moves to the shim of §10.3.

The language contract determines the result relation. A completed empty result passes; a completed
non-empty result produces the declared finding. Invalid, unsupported, and incomplete evaluation
are not findings and cannot establish a pass (Part 11 §8).

A rule declares a level of `error`, `warning`, or `info`. A rule MAY be `advisory`, in which case
it is off by default and MUST NOT be `error`, so that enabling it cannot change whether a packet
previously passed. Stated as the guarantee it is: **running with advisory rules enabled MUST NOT
change whether a packet passed**. A validator MUST expose the choice, and a report MUST state whether
advisory rules were run: two reports over one packet may legitimately differ, and one that does
not say which mode produced it cannot be compared with the other.

### 10.3 What is not a rule

A Tier 2 rule MUST NOT express physical order, file presence, Parquet types, Zarr rank and codecs, or checksums. A query cannot observe them, so a storage-layer check written as SQL fails silently rather than loudly. These are checked by the shim of Part 05 §5.3.

## 12. Rules for particular resources

Sections 2 through 10 bind every declaration and table payload. What follows binds particular
tables, where identifying a
row, resolving a value, or reading an absence takes more than the generic rules give.

<a id="ew-unit-identity-architecture" data-spec-anchor="alias"></a>
<a id="ew-unit-identity" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 12.1 Unit identity

`unit_id` identifies a unit on its own. The packet assigns it, numbering units by
ascending `(sorting_run_id, curation_id, local_unit_label)` so a rebuild reproduces the same
numbers; `core.units` records the run and the curation as ordinary columns, and
`local_unit_label` records what the sorter called it.

A packet carrying `core.units` MUST carry `core.sorting_runs` and `core.curation_versions`.

A table keyed on a unit carries `unit_id` and not the run or the curation, and a
consumer relating a unit to anything outside the packet uses `(sorting_run_id, local_unit_label)`.

`core.units` also declares what its spikes are counted on and measured in: `clock_id`, required,
and `amplitude_unit`, nullable and open. Both are facts of the sorting run, and a
spike cannot reach the run — §8.4 admits one hop and spike → unit → run is two — so they live on
the table a spike references directly. `core.spike_events.t_tick` therefore declares
`clock_from: core.units.clock_id` and `amplitude` declares `unit_from: core.units.amplitude_unit`,
and neither `core.spike_events` nor `core.unit_observation_intervals` carries a
`clock_id` of its own. The run still determines both values, since a unit names its run
in a reference it MUST carry.

### 12.2 Channel indices in a sorting run

A sorting drops bad channels before it runs, so a channel index a source emits counts the channels
it kept and not the probe's contacts. `core.sorting_run_channels` records that list in the run's
own order.

A writer that can read it SHOULD emit the table, MUST NOT derive it from `core.unit_electrodes` or
from a bad-channel list, and SHOULD emit nothing where it cannot read it.

A consumer holding such an index MUST resolve it through that table and MUST NOT read it as a
position in `core.electrodes`; where the run declares no channels the index is unresolvable, and
the consumer MUST report that rather than resolve it against the contact list.

Where a run declares its channels, every `core.unit_electrodes` row for one of its units MUST name
an electrode among them.

### 12.3 Calibration

A calibration is a standing property of an instrument rather than an activity of this packet, so
`core.calibrations` records the session it was **in force** for and, separately, when it was
measured; a consumer MUST NOT read the two as one fact. A calibration in force for
several sessions is recorded once per session, as for any row whose results reach more than one
session.

The devices a calibration covers are a child table, since one calibration of the rig's manipulators
is cited by six probes; a calibration covering a device the packet does not hold gets no
row there, which is a fact rather than a defect, and a writer that records the calibration SHOULD
record the devices it names.

A paired series is carried as `core.calibration_points`, whose two value columns take their units
of measure from the calibration, since the unit varies per calibration and so cannot be a literal
on either column. Content that is an array rather than a curve names the array asset
holding it.

Where a row has no points, no array asset and no detail kind, it records that the calibration was in force
and that the packet holds nothing further, which a consumer MUST read as **unrecorded** rather than
as absent. A source stating that no calibration was performed gets no row at all.

`core.calibrations` declares `metadata` and the two child tables MUST NOT, because its key holds an
identifier of its own where theirs are keyed by other tables' identifiers.

### 12.4 A clock's rate

A clock MUST NOT declare both `tick_rate_hz` and a rational rate; `core.clocks.tick_rate_declared` already requires at least one, so exactly one is present. The float is for a source that measured its rate rather than stating an exact one, and forcing a rational on it would make a writer invent a denominator — a rounding decision this specification does not make on a source's behalf.

### 12.5 A clock's observation window

A packet MUST declare at least one row of `core.clock_observation_intervals` for every clock any of its rows is timestamped on. More than one is permitted, for an acquisition paused and resumed. A clock that exists only as a parent declares none: the chain is required to be well founded, so such a clock is declared, and nothing is observed on it for an inherited interval to reach.

`core.clocks.observation_declared` checks the part of that a query can see — a clock that has anchors, or that a session names as its `reference_clock_id`, and declares no interval. A clock aligned by `transform_slope` alone carries data and has no anchors, so the obligation reaches it and the rule does not; the obligation binds the writer, which knows what it timestamped.

### 12.6 A clock's alignment record

Three facts about how a packet's clocks were aligned are columns, because a consumer needs them to place a tick and Part 02 §8.6's line puts such a fact in a column and never in a key.

`core.sessions.alignment_bound` holds how far past its anchors the writer mapped, in median anchor intervals. A writer establishing a bound wider than the default of one median anchor interval MUST record it; a consumer reading a null MUST map at that default, which is a fact and not a defect. It is on the session because the bound is a property of the times the packet states, as `t_zero_tick` is, so one session declares one bound and no rule is needed to say which run wins. A value below one is refused.

`core.clocks.withheld_first_anchor` and `withheld_last_anchor` record the terminal-anchor test on that clock, and a writer running it MUST record both. Three states distinguish the outcomes: null is a clock the test did not run on, false is a terminal it kept, true is one it withheld — so a silent zero and an untested clock differ structurally rather than by a rule nothing can observe. A clock with no anchors MUST NOT declare either.

`core.clock_phase_steps` holds one row per step in a clock's alignment to its parent, a clock having possibly several (§5.2). A writer detecting a step MUST record it, and a step is reported and never corrected: the map is exact at every anchor, so it carries the step faithfully, and subtracting it would need to know whether the step also moved the devices timestamped on that parent, which the packet does not record. A clock whose `parent_clock_id` is null MUST NOT have rows here.

`core.processing_runs.parameters` keeps the evidence behind each decision and none of the three facts themselves. A writer SHOULD record the measurement's median, p95 and maximum error there, with the distance and the tolerance it judged them against. A consumer needs the bound to place a tick and not the residual that justified it.

### 12.7 A frame's axes

A frame declares one row of `core.coordinate_frame_axes` per axis it has, and two axes of one frame MUST NOT carry the same `axis_name`, which `core.coordinate_frame_axes.axis_names_distinct` checks. An axis name is the source's own word for the field the values came from, so two axes sharing one means a writer put two quantities under one name and the packet cannot say which value is on which axis.

A coordinate's values reference the axis rows of their own frame on `(session_id, coordinate_frame, axis_index)`, so a value on an axis the frame never declared is an orphan Tier 1 reports, and a frame's axis count cannot disagree with the shape of its coordinates.

A coordinate MAY carry values on fewer axes than its frame declares, where its source states none on the rest, and a consumer MUST NOT read an absent axis row as a zero: it is a position the source did not state on that axis. That is what a null in the struck `x_um`, `y_um` and `z_um` said, in the form the child table has.

`axis_value` declares `unit_from` of `core.coordinate_frame_axes.unit`, resolved one hop through that reference (§8.4): the unit is the axis's, so a frame stated in millimetres needs no second set of columns and no column name carries a unit.

## 1. Scope

This part defines how the logical resources of Part 02 are stored in the portable profile. A packet under this profile MUST be readable without a server.

This part incorporates `registry/physical_profile.schema.json` and
`registry/physical_profiles/escapewheel-portable-v0.1.json`. The schema owns the reusable closed
shape. The descriptor owns the profile's exact identifiers, kind-to-interface mapping, interface
forms, ownership compatibility, variants, reader floors, floor roles, and anchors. This prose owns
their meaning and actor obligations. A conforming distributed copy MUST pass the schema and the
generic cross-member checker at a packaging or equivalent conformance boundary; an implementation
MAY cache that result and MUST NOT revalidate an unchanged bundled descriptor for each packet.

This part also incorporates `registry/resources.schema.json`, whose `$id` is
`https://escapewheel.org/schemas/resources/0.5.0`, as the exact shape of the resource document of
§11.3. Its closed envelope admits an optional `$schema` string and requires `resources`, an array
of closed entries. An entry requires `resource_id`, `declaration_id`, and `uri`, and admits
`external`, `source`, `digest`, `size_bytes`, `verified_at`, and `members`. The delivery schema
defines each of those members, and the entry's conditional shape, exactly as the manifest schema
[Part 04 §1](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-catalog-scope) incorporates defines them; a difference
makes the specification candidate internally inconsistent.

The portable physical profile defined by this part is identified by
`escapewheel-portable-v0.1`. Its requirements directly invoke the shared JSON, URI, and RFC 1952
authorities identified in [Part 00 §10](00_architecture.md#ew-normative-references) and the
portable-profile authorities identified in [Part 03 §12](#ew-portable-profile-normative-references).
The application columns in those sections state their extent. The `digest` field belongs to
[Part 04 §4.1](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-resource-entry-fields), and its functions, a prefix's
value, and when a writer records one to
[Part 04 §4.3](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-resource-digests); this part determines what a portable
resource's digest covers rather than invoking a digest authority independently.

This part is normative for a packet whose manifest declares `profile_id: escapewheel-portable-v0.1` and binds no other packet. It is the only part whose rules may depend on a storage technology. Where a number here supports a rule rather than stating one, the rule stays and the number is Part 10's.

## 2. Descriptor and interfaces

The packet's `profile_id` and a resource's checked logical kind select its interface. A packet
entry MUST NOT select or repeat that interface, and a declaration ID, namespace, URI suffix, or
directory placement MUST NOT act as an alternative selector.

| logical kind | generic interface |
|---|---|
| `table` | `parquet` |
| `dense_array` | `zarr` |
| `opaque` | none |

The descriptor's mapping keys MUST equal the logical standard's complete kind vocabulary. Every
non-null mapped interface MUST exist exactly once, and every declared interface MUST be mapped.
An opaque resource has no generic value interface: a generic reader lists its identity and
location but need not retrieve, parse, decode, or render it. Unknown opaque representations do not
invalidate a packet. Every current interface is `materialized`; ownership is not a realization

An interface's `reader_floor` states what every conforming reader supports for logical-value
access. `reader_and_packet_writer` also bounds packet-owned writer output; `reader_only` does not.
A valid feature outside a reader-only floor is outside the portability guarantee, but that fact
alone neither makes the upstream representation malformed nor decides packet conformance.

### 3.2 Files that MUST NOT be written

`schemas/semantic_version.txt` is struck. It restated `semantic_standard_version`, which the manifest is required to carry, and so bought no otherwise-available access.

<a id="ew-portable-bound-registries" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.3 Bound registries

A writer MUST store each registry that the manifest binds under
[Part 04 §8](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-registry-bindings) at its descriptor's `path`, as
exactly one gzip member under RFC 1952 using the DEFLATE compression method of RFC 1951. The
decompressed bytes MUST be exactly the bytes the descriptor's `sha256` covers. A gzip header's
modification time, operating system, file name, and comment carry no meaning, and a reader MUST
ignore them.

A conforming packet MUST contain every bound registry. A writer MUST NOT bind a registry by remote
location or omit the stored copy, and a reader or validator MUST NOT resolve a bound registry from
anywhere else. A descriptor's `path` names a file for the immutability, amendment, and cleanup rules
of §§8.1, 9.1, and 9.5.

<a id="ew-staging-area" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.4 The staging area

In this profile a build's staging area ([Part 05 §2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-import-pipeline))
is the directory `staging/` directly under the packet root, and a writer MUST stage an import or an
amendment there and nowhere else under the packet root. Under the packet root, promotion moves a
staged resource to its final path by a rename within one filesystem; the fixed name lets a writer
and a cleanup tell a staging area from a directory this specification does not govern (§9.5).

## 4. URI rules

A URI in the manifest or in a table MAY be relative to the packet root or absolute, or — where the entry names a `source` — relative to that source (Part 04 §5.5). A portable local packet SHOULD use relative URIs.

An absolute URI MAY name an external asset. A manifest entry for an external asset MUST declare it external (Part 04 §5).

relative URI resolves under that prefix

## 5. Table storage

<a id="ew-table-encoding" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.1 Encoding

A table MUST be exactly one object containing one valid Parquet file. A directory, prefix, glob,
explicit member set, or multi-file dataset MUST NOT realize a table in this profile. Compression
SHOULD be Zstandard where available.

A large table SHOULD use row groups sized for analytical scans. Part 10 carries the sizes measured for one.

An identifier column is `int32`, or `int64` where its range requires it (Part 02 §5.3) and MUST NOT be dictionary-encoded in a way that requires unification across row groups before it can be compared.

<a id="ew-table-partitioning" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.2 Partitioning

Partitioned tables are not part of this profile. A future interface may define immutable member
selection and cross-file ordering; the present `parquet` interface is one object.

### 5.3 Ordering

A table MUST be stored in an order that settles its primary key by adjacency: every key column appears in the sort, and every sort column up to and including the last key column is either a key column itself or one the registry declares `determined_by` a subset of the key (Part 02 §8.5). Where §5.4 gives no order for a table, the order required is its primary key's. A validator MUST report a departure as an **error**.

Two rows sharing a key are then neighbours, because nothing in the sort can order between them: a column that is a function of the key takes the same value in both. So the scan that establishes order settles uniqueness in the same pass, which is the means Part 02 §10.1 leaves to a profile to supply. A validator establishes order by scan rather than by query, because physical order is not visible to SQL (Part 02 §10.3).

The scan MUST verify each `determined_by` declaration it relies on: of two adjacent rows sharing a primary key, every sort column declared `determined_by` a subset of that key MUST hold the same value in both, and a validator MUST report a difference as an **error**. A wrong declaration otherwise waives a check rather than failing one. The comparison reaches a declaration that is false between two adjacent rows, which the scan reports as a duplicate key as well, the second finding saying which to repair; a declaration false enough to sort two duplicates apart is beyond a scan of neighbours.

Where the scan finds a departure, uniqueness MUST be settled by aggregate rather than by adjacency, since a duplicate anywhere but beside its twin is invisible to a scan of neighbours.

A reader MUST NOT depend on stored order for correctness. Establishing the order is the validator's work, and a reader that assumed it would be trusting a property of the profile rather than of the logical standard.

One table is stored in an order other than its primary key's, `analysis.spike_events`, under a `MUST`; Part 03 §5.4 gives it, and it satisfies this section because `clock_id` and `time_bucket_tick` are each declared `determined_by` a subset of the key. `core.events` needs no override: its key is `(session_id, stream_id, t_tick)`, the order it is stored in. `core.spike_events` needs none either: its key is `(session_id, unit_id, t_tick)`, and because units are numbered run-major the default order clusters a probe's spikes.

### 5.4 The overriding orders

| resource | order |
|---|---|
| `analysis.spike_events` | `session_id, clock_id, time_bucket_tick, unit_id, t_tick` |

A writer **MUST** emit `analysis.spike_events` in that order. It is the one resource whose layout is part of its reason for existing: bucketing by time and sorting by unit within each bucket leaves both columns with informative row-group statistics, so a unit filter and a time filter each prune.

A writer **SHOULD** start a new row group at each bucket boundary. A group cut at a fixed row count straddles boundaries and spans two buckets' worth of time. Row-group size and encoding are otherwise not normative: the right values follow from session length and unit count rather than from the schema.

<a id="ew-parquet-reader-floor" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.5 The Parquet floor

A reader MUST support footer-reported compressions `UNCOMPRESSED`, `SNAPPY`, `GZIP`, and `ZSTD`;
encodings `PLAIN`, `RLE`, `RLE_DICTIONARY`, and `DELTA_BINARY_PACKED`; and data-page types
`DATA_PAGE` and `DATA_PAGE_V2`, in a file whose footer and columns are not encrypted. A packet writer MUST keep every written
table within that same floor and SHOULD use Zstd. It MUST configure its page type and absence of
encryption before writing, inspect the footer afterwards, and refuse an outside-floor compression
or encoding. `PLAIN_DICTIONARY` is valid upstream but outside this writer floor.

The descriptor is the exact machine-readable owner of these members and declares this interface
`materialized`, `logical_values`, `single_object`, available for packet and external ownership,
with floor role `reader_and_packet_writer`. `FileMetaData.version` is not a substitute for checking
the independent floor features.

A validator assessing a Parquet resource MUST compare each column chunk's footer-reported
compression and encodings with the floor's `compressions` and `encodings`. It need not read page
headers or determine data-page types. A validator that does not compare data-page types MUST state
in its report that it did not assess them.

A writer SHOULD choose the encoding of each integer, float, string, timestamp and date column from
the column's declarations and from four counts over the rows it holds when it chooses: the rows,
nulls included; the distinct non-null values; the runs, a run being a maximal sequence of equal
values once nulls are set aside; and, for an integer, whether its non-null values never decrease.
Where a writer writes a table whole the rows are the table's; where it streams a table,
they are the row group it is about to write, or the first row group where the writer keeps one
encoding for the whole file. The first of the four rules below that applies decides.

1. A writer SHOULD write plain a column in which every row is null.
2. A writer SHOULD dictionary-encode a column holding one distinct non-null value where it holds
   50,000 rows or more, and below that SHOULD encode it with `DELTA_BINARY_PACKED` where it is an
   integer and plain otherwise. A dictionary page costs its bytes in every row group,
   and only a long row group repays them (Part 10 §2.8).
3. An integer column is **ordered** where its name ends `_tick`; where it is the first column of its
   table's sort order other than `session_id` and its role is `identifier`; where its role is
   `structural_index` or `sequence_position` (Part 02 §8.1); where it is an `assigned` identifier
   (Part 02 §8.2); or where its non-null values never decrease. A writer SHOULD encode an ordered
   integer with `DELTA_BINARY_PACKED`, and SHOULD write it plain where it holds at least 50 rows for
   each run. A declaration states no distribution, but it marks a column likely to climb
   within groups, which no count over the whole column sees (Part 10 §2.9).
4. A writer SHOULD dictionary-encode any other column, a float included, where it holds at least 50
   rows for each distinct non-null value, and SHOULD write it plain otherwise. Repetition
   is what a dictionary indexes, and no declaration says how often a value repeats (Part 10 §2.10).

None of the four is the property it stands in for, so a writer MUST be able to override each per
column, and every override MUST record what it measured and on what. An exception
recording nothing cannot be re-judged when the data changes. A writer SHOULD refuse an exception
naming a column no table declares, which is decidable with no packet in hand.

A writer SHOULD report, at `info`, a column whose encoding costs more than **1.5x** the smallest
available to it, measured over a sample of the rows it wrote. It SHOULD measure a column
by its own compressed chunks rather than by a file carrying it, and SHOULD judge a string, timestamp
or date column between a dictionary and plain, the two encodings the rules choose among for it.
These rules are good guesses, and the report is how the author of a table learns that
theirs is one a guess misses. A report and not a refusal: every encoding above is permitted, so a
packet is conforming whichever it uses.

Wall-clock timestamps MUST be Parquet `TIMESTAMP` with `isAdjustedToUTC=true` and `MICROS`
precision; A wall-clock column MUST NOT use `INT96`. Dates MUST be Parquet `DATE`, int32 days since the Unix epoch.

<a id="ew-one-null" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.6 One null

Under `escapewheel-portable-v0.1` a column value is present or null and there is no second marker. Parquet's definition level carries one bit and Arrow's validity bitmap carries one, so a value that is inapplicable, one that was never recorded, and one a source recorded as unknown all arrive as the same null.

NaN is not the exception it looks like. It is a value of a float column rather than an absent one, so it reaches only a float column declaring `finite: false` under [Part 02 §5.4](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-finite-values), and Parquet's statistics exclude it from a page's bounds — a page holding only NaN publishes no minimum and no maximum.

A writer MUST NOT adopt a sentinel value meaning absent; an absent value is null. Where the reason for the absence matters, core records the fact that distinguishes it rather than encoding the reason in the value: a run's `scope` distinguishes an undeterminable scope from an unambiguous one, and `normalized_label` is null where a source's label has no honest projection.

## 6. Array storage

<a id="ew-array-version" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.1 Version

A packet-owned array MUST be Zarr v3. A referenced array MAY be Zarr v2 or v3. A reader or
validator determines the version from the resource's `zarr_format` metadata when it accesses or
assesses that resource; a packet entry MUST NOT state it.

An array MUST use a **regular chunk grid**: every chunk the same shape, the last along each axis permitted to be partial. An irregular grid is expressible in Zarr and is not readable by every binding.

No packet-owned resource may have metadata declaring Zarr v2. Lack of implementation support for
one selected resource MUST fail that resource specifically and MUST NOT reject unopened resources.

### 6.2 Axis order

Array axes are stored order, slowest-varying first. An array's codec chain MUST NOT include `transpose`.

A column-major binding enumerates axes in reverse. The client adapts; the format does not.

<a id="ew-array-axis-coordinates" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.3 Axis coordinates

Every axis MUST declare its coordinates in the dense-array declaration's `array.axes_resource`, in
one of its declared coordinate forms. A regularly sampled axis declares an origin tick, a step,
and a length rather than storing per-sample values.

A step MAY be rational, declared as a numerator and denominator: a 30 ms bin at 31,250 Hz is 937.5 ticks.


An axis MUST declare no more than one coordinate form: two descriptions of the same positions can disagree and nothing would say which governs.

A rational step is a numerator and a denominator. `step_tick_den`, where present, MUST be positive, the numerator MUST be present wherever an origin is, and the pair SHOULD be in lowest terms so two packets describing the same axis describe it identically.

Where an array declares both a sampling rate and a regular tick axis, the two describe the same thing and MUST agree. The rate is retained as a validated denormalization, not as a second source of truth.

<a id="ew-array-codecs" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.4 Codecs

A writer MAY use any codec permitted by the applicable Zarr specification, subject to §6.2's
`transpose` prohibition and the regular-grid rule. The resource's own metadata is the sole owner of
the actual pipeline; the manifest does not repeat it.

The v3 reader floor admits exactly these unsharded codec sequences, in pipeline order: `bytes`;
`bytes` then `gzip`; `bytes` then `zstd`; and `bytes` then `blosc` with `cname` `zstd`. It also
admits a sharded pipeline whose shard holds one of those sequences, under the rules below. A
conforming reader MUST expose the logical values of every valid v3 array whose pipeline matches the
floor. The descriptor's `interfaces.zarr.variants.v3.reader_floor` is exactly:

```json
{
  "codec_sequences": [
    [{"name": "bytes"}],
    [{"name": "bytes"}, {"name": "gzip"}],
    [{"name": "bytes"}, {"name": "zstd"}],
    [{"name": "bytes"}, {"name": "blosc", "configuration": {"cname": "zstd"}}]
  ],
  "sharding": {
    "index_codec_sequences": [[{"name": "bytes"}], [{"name": "bytes"}, {"name": "crc32c"}]],
    "index_location": "end"
  }
}
```

A reader or validator comparing a v3 array's metadata with the floor MUST apply these rules. A
codec matches a codec pattern when its `name` equals the pattern's and every configuration member
the pattern states compares equal. Members the pattern does not state do not participate, and a
codec stated by its name alone has no configuration members. An unsharded pipeline matches a floor
sequence when both have the same length and each codec matches the pattern at its position. A
sharded pipeline matches the floor when:

- the pipeline is exactly one `sharding_indexed` codec;
- that codec's `codecs` match an entry of `codec_sequences`;
- its `index_codecs` match an entry of `sharding.index_codec_sequences`; and
- its `index_location` equals `sharding.index_location`, an absent `index_location` comparing as
  `end`, the default the sharding-indexed codec specification gives it.

A floor without `sharding` admits no sharded pipeline, and a pipeline matching no entry is outside
the floor. Metadata the applicable Zarr specification makes invalid, including an invalid
configuration of a codec the validator implements, is upstream invalidity rather than a floor
observation. A validator MUST treat a codec name it does not recognize as valid metadata outside
the floor, because Zarr v3 admits extension codecs.

The v2 reader floor is exactly `filters: null` with compressor null, compressor `id: zstd`, or
compressor `id: blosc` and `cname: zstd`. In a v2 pattern, JSON null matches only null, every stated
member compares equal, and unmentioned configuration members do not participate. The descriptor
declares the Zarr interface `materialized`, `logical_values`, and `prefix`, with floor role
`reader_only`; v3 permits packet and external ownership, while v2 permits external ownership only.
It does not enumerate optional codecs.

The physical-profile schema's `$id`, and the `$schema` value it requires of a descriptor, are
`https://escapewheel.org/schemas/physical-profile/0.2.0`, and the portable descriptor's `$schema`
names it. The schema declares the v3 floor with these definitions:

| definition | shape |
|---|---|
| `codec_pattern` | closed object; requires `name`, a string; admits `configuration` only where `name` is `blosc`, and there `configuration` is a closed object requiring `cname`, a string |
| `codec_sequence` | array of one or more `codec_pattern` |
| `sharding_floor` | closed object; requires `index_codec_sequences`, an array of one or more distinct `codec_sequence`, and `index_location`, `start` or `end` |
| `zarr_v3_floor` | closed object; requires `codec_sequences`, an array of one or more distinct `codec_sequence`; admits `sharding`, a `sharding_floor` |

<a id="ew-array-metadata-placement" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.5 What the array declares, and what the tables declare

`core.array_assets` MUST NOT carry dtype or shape. The Zarr array declares them, and restating them buys no otherwise-unavailable access.

The declaration-selected axes table carries axis names, because a Zarr binding in another language
cannot see them. A physical checker resolves that table from the dense-array declaration rather
than from a fixed namespace or resource ID.

Zarr attributes MAY duplicate helpful metadata. Logical and scientific facts required for
validation MUST also be represented in their logical resources or packet fields. Self-describing
physical facts—version, shape, dtype, chunking, and codec configuration—MUST be obtained from the
resource and need not be copied into a table or the manifest.

## 7. Metadata placement

| kind | location |
|---|---|
| packet-level facts | the manifest |
| queryable scientific facts | tables |
| semi-structured, lab-specific values | a `json_string` column, or a sidecar |
| human-readable context | `docs/` |

A value used for filtering, grouping, joining, or validation MUST be a first-class typed column.

## 8. Immutability

### 8.1 Packet-owned bytes

A writer MUST NOT modify a resource the packet owns once it is committed. A correction is a new packet version, not an edit.

This promise is what allows an amendment to skip revalidating untouched files, so it is verified rather than assumed: a writer records a checksum for every packet-owned resource (§10).

### 8.2 External bytes

This specification promises nothing about bytes the packet does not own. What a packet records about an external resource is what was true when it was checked, not a claim about now.

## 9. Amendment, versioning, and retention

### 9.1 What an amendment is

An **amendment** produces a new version of an existing packet and MUST NOT modify any file the superseded manifest names. A table that gains rows is written as a **new file**, and the amended manifest names the new file; no byte of the old one changes. Immutability is thereby a property of files, and a resource's *content* may be superseded by naming different bytes.

An amendment MUST set a `packet_version` that sorts strictly after the superseded one, compared as a semantic version.

### 9.2 What an amendment may and may not do

| | |
|---|---|
| Add an analysis resource | MAY |
| Add rows to a provenance table (`core.processing_runs`, `core.source_files`, `core.processing_run_inputs`), as a new file | MAY |
| Remove an analysis resource | MAY — Part 06 makes them disposable |
| Add a core resource | MAY |
| Change the content of a core resource other than the provenance tables | MUST NOT |
| Remove a core resource | MUST NOT |
| Change `packet_id`, `profile_id`, or the standard versions | MUST NOT |

The asymmetry is the design: an amendment **extends** what a packet says and does not revise it. A packet whose core data was wrong is a new packet, not a new version of an old one, because anything already derived from the old core is silently invalidated and nothing in the format can detect that.

Provenance tables are the exception because they are append-only by nature — a row records that something ran, and a later run does not change what an earlier one did.

<a id="ew-superseded-manifest-retention" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.3 The manifest

Every superseded manifest is retained under `manifests/`, so a later cleanup can distinguish a file an old version named from a file nothing ever named. A retained manifest keeps its own `created_at` and its own validation record; an amendment MUST NOT rewrite either.

Where an amendment binds a different registry document, the packet retains the superseded copy at
the path the superseded manifest records. A writer MUST refuse an amendment that would bind,
anywhere in the packet's current and retained manifests, two different digests under the same
registry ID and version, because that version would then be ambiguous within one packet.

<a id="ew-incremental-revalidation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.4 Scope of revalidation

An amendment MUST validate the whole packet, not only what it added. What is bounded is the
*work*, not the verdict: the resources an amendment did not write are immutable, so their
verdict is determined rather than assumed, and re-establishing it costs nothing.

A writer performing an amendment MUST establish the physical order of the resources it writes, and a validator MUST NOT
require it to re-establish that order for any other packet-owned resource.

A validator MUST derive a complete input set from the layer that owns each check. A generated
Tier 1 check takes its inputs from the registry declarations that generated it: the attached
resource, both ends of a foreign key, and every specialization or discriminator branch it reads.
A Tier 2 check takes its inputs from the parsed base-resource set Part 11 §3 requires static
validation to return. A Part 05 shim check declares its inputs explicitly.

A validator MUST re-run a check when it is attached to a resource the amendment writes or any
member of its input set was written. A Tier 2 dependency MUST NOT be inferred by searching query
text: an alias, common table expression, literal, or comment that resembles a resource does not
create one. The session-inventory shim declares the packet manifest and `core.sessions`; installing
a manifest writes the manifest input for this purpose. A shim without a complete declaration MUST
run rather than be skipped.

A validator **MUST make that test over-inclusive where it is uncertain**. Matching a resource name inside
a string literal or a comment re-runs a check that did not need it, which costs time; failing to
match one costs correctness. A validator that cannot show an input is immutable MUST run the
check: skipping a check whose inputs *could* have changed stays forbidden.

<a id="ew-packet-cleanup" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.5 Cleanup

Cleanup is a separate operation and MUST proceed orphans first, superseded manifests second. Expiring a manifest first would make an orphan indistinguishable from a stray, permanently.

A **stray** is a file under the packet root that no manifest ever named, that lies outside the
staging area (§3.4), and that neither is listed by nor lies inside a directory listed by a promotion
record whose `packet_version` is unpublished ([Part 05 §6](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-import-commit)).
A writer MUST NOT remove a stray. A packet may carry files this specification does not govern, and
cleanup that deletes the ungoverned is a data-loss bug wearing an operation's name. The operation
reports by default and acts only when asked.

An amendment **MUST NOT** delete anything as part of amending, other than a staging area as
[Part 05 §2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-import-pipeline) permits and the paths Part 05 §6 lets it
remove when it acts on a promotion record. A file that no manifest version names any longer MAY be
deleted, but by cleanup, as a separate operation — which is what keeps an amendment's failure from
having removed something.

A packet MAY declare its own retention, and a writer SHOULD emit one: how many superseded manifests to keep, and an optional age beyond which one may be dropped. Both are **floors** — a manifest is expired only when both permit it — and both default to one kept version and no age limit, because a packet is archival where a table is operational. A packet never amended retains nothing and carries no `manifests/` directory at all, so the common case costs nothing.

### 9.6 What a version is not

`packet_version` versions the bundle. A writer MUST NOT increment it for a rewrite of a table's rows under a future lakehouse profile.

<a id="ew-owned-resource-digests" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 10. Checksums

In this profile a packet-owned table's `digest` covers its one Parquet file, and a packet-owned
array's covers its Zarr prefix as
[Part 04 §4.3](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-resource-digests) defines a prefix's value. Part 04 §4.3
requires a writer to record one for every packet-owned resource, which is the one case where a
digest verifies what the packet itself claims.

A digest is whole-file, not per-row-group. Parquet cannot be appended to, so "which pages moved" is not a question the format can be asked, and Parquet's own page CRC32 is a corruption detector that a rewritten file passes.

Verification of packet-owned and external resources is layered
([Part 04 §5.3](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-external-resource-verification)).

<a id="ew-reaching-a-source" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 11. Reaching a source

An adapter reads one source and delivers what it read, and a build assembles a packet from several (Part 05 §3.1). An adapter delivers only where a build points it ([Part 05 §2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-import-pipeline)), and in this profile by these routes:

- rows, returned to the build where it runs the adapter in process, or across the protocol a build reaches the adapter by (§11.1), with a `flight-v0.1` adapter serving them at `socket`;
- a packet-owned array, written into `array_location` where it crosses a protocol, and in process either written there or handed to the build with its values (§11.3);
- a `files-v0.1` delivery, written into `delivery_root` (§11.1), with the resource document for any referenced resource it delivers (§11.3); and
- a report, written at the `report_path` the recipe names (Part 09 §5.8).

This section is what passes between them: the protocols rows may cross by, what a build supplies an adapter, what a delivery states, and how an adapter declares itself. It is the profile's rather than the pipeline's because every rule in it would change if the profile changed, and because an adapter is the first thing an implementation in another language writes.

An **adapter** here is code that reads one source, whether the reference implementation ships it or a third party does. A clause binding an adapter binds one it does not ship identically.

<a id="ew-protocol-groups" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 11.1 Protocol groups

A profile names a **group** for each way rows cross a process boundary, and the protocols in it. `escapewheel-portable-v0.1` names one group, out of process, holding three:

| protocol | rows cross as |
|---|---|
| `files-v0.1` | Parquet in `delivery_root`, one file per resource, named by its resource identifier with the suffix `.parquet` |
| `stream-v0.1` | a sequence of Arrow IPC streams on standard output, each schema naming its resource |
| `flight-v0.1` | Arrow Flight over a Unix socket the build names, one descriptor per resource |

An adapter **released independently of a build** MUST declare and support at least one protocol in a group, and a build MUST support every protocol in every group this profile names. The asymmetry is deliberate: there is one build and there will be many adapters, so no negotiation can fail. A build meeting such an adapter that declares no protocol of the group MUST refuse it, naming the group, before any source is opened.

An adapter a build ships with need not declare one, and a build reaches it directly. The test is the release boundary rather than who wrote the code: an adapter inside a build's distribution is upgraded in the commit that changes a dependency, where one released apart is on the far side of a version boundary the build moves without asking, and the protocol is what keeps that move from being an outage. A build MAY serve an adapter it ships over any protocol of the group, which is how one becomes reachable out of process without declaring anything.

A build reaching out of process an adapter that declares its protocols MUST reach it by one of them: the protocol the recipe entry names, or any the adapter declares where the entry names none. A build MUST refuse a recipe entry naming a protocol its adapter does not declare, naming the source and the protocol, before it opens any source. A build reaches an adapter it ships that declares no protocol, where it runs that adapter out of process, over the protocol the entry names or any of the group. A build runs an adapter in the writer's process only as Part 05 §3.9 says, and reaches it there by no protocol, whatever the adapter declares.

A protocol carries its version in its name, semantically versioned from `v0.1`. A profile MUST NOT change what a named protocol means; a protocol that changes how rows cross is a new member of the group, and a build MUST NOT read support for one version as support for another. Retiring a protocol is a change to this profile and not a decision a build makes.

An adapter a recipe opts into the writer's process (Part 05 §3.9) is bound by this section too: the opt-in decides where a build runs an adapter, not what the adapter supports.

<a id="ew-build-supplied-values" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 11.2 What a build supplies

An adapter MUST declare every value a build supplies it, and a build MUST supply exactly what the adapter declares and MUST NOT decide it from the source's kind. Everything supplied is data: a build MUST NOT require an adapter to accept a live handle — an open store, a connection, an object of the implementation's own — because what crosses a process boundary is text, and an adapter with two ways in has one that depends on where it is running.

The vocabulary is closed. Each term names one value and the form it takes, so an adapter and a build written by different people pass the same thing:

| term | what a build passes |
|---|---|
| `root` | the source's location as a URI, which the adapter opens for itself |
| `session_id` | the session's own name, never the integer a build assigns (§11.3) |
| `reference_clock` | the reference clock's row: identifier, rational tick rate, origin |
| `clocks` | one such row per clock declared before this source runs |
| `first_samples` | one record per stream: its name, and the integer its samples start at |
| `units` | one record per unit: the source's label for it, and the identifier assigned |
| `wanted` | the resource IDs the recipe leaves this source owning, so it produces no more |
| `array_location` | a location under the build's staging area ([Part 05 §2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-import-pipeline)) to write an array to (§11.3) |
| an option, by its name | the value the recipe carries under it — a `zone`, an `interval_kind` |

A build MUST resolve each declared name against that list first and the recipe's options second, and MUST refuse a recipe where a name resolves to neither. A profile adding a term amends this section.

An adapter MUST declare in `accepts` every option it reads and can be constructed without, and in `needs` every value it cannot; it MUST NOT name one option in both, and MUST NOT name in `accepts` a term of the list above or a term a protocol requires. Declaring a name in `needs` is what makes a recipe carry it, so an option holding a default is declarable only as an accepted one. An adapter MUST apply its own default where the recipe sets no value, so what an omitted option means is the adapter's statement rather than a value the build invents.

A build MUST supply an option the recipe sets and the adapter names in either list, and MUST NOT require a recipe to set one named in `accepts`. A build MUST refuse a recipe whose source entry sets an option the adapter names in neither, naming the source and the option, before any source is opened. A build MUST NOT supply an option the adapter names in neither list, including one it adds itself rather than reading from the recipe, and MUST NOT apply a facility of its own to an adapter that does not name the option carrying it.

A build MUST refuse a recipe where a term the list carries cannot be filled, naming the term and the source, and MUST NOT pass such a term absent or empty: `first_samples` needs a source that states them and `units` one that produced them, and an adapter cannot tell a term nobody filled from one whose value is legitimately empty.

A build MUST pass a collection as records carrying its key as columns. A mapping's keys are a type the crossing does not keep: an integer key returns as a string, and a compound key does not cross at all.

Some terms belong to a protocol rather than to an adapter. `flight-v0.1` requires `socket`, the address to serve on; `files-v0.1` requires `delivery_root`, the directory to write the delivery into; both `files-v0.1` and `stream-v0.1` require `report_path` where a report is expected (Part 09 §5.8). A build MUST supply the terms of the protocol it reaches an adapter by (§11.1), and no other protocol's; in process it supplies no protocol's terms. An adapter MUST NOT declare one, because the protocol it is reached by is what asks for it.

What crosses is one JSON object keyed by the term. A build MUST write it to the command's standard input and MUST close that stream when it has, MUST NOT pass it as an argument, and MUST NOT add any argument to the command the recipe carries. An adapter MUST read its standard input to end of file and parse it as that object before it writes any rows, and a build supplies nothing else on that stream. An option appears under `options`, keyed by its name, so no option collides with a term.

An argument list is not the channel because it has a size no profile can raise — 131,072 bytes for one argument on Linux, 32,767 characters for a whole command line on Windows — and a term carrying one record per unit reached 322,799 bytes on a real session, where an adapter's whole object measured 721 against a fixture that had none. Standard input carries nothing under any protocol of the group and has no such limit.

The wanted set is a term a build supplies and an adapter MAY act on. A build settles ownership before any source is opened (Part 05 §3.4), so an adapter told what is wanted may extract no more; acting on it is permitted rather than required, because for some sources producing three resources costs what producing all of them costs, and a build cannot tell an adapter that extracted three from one that extracted everything and discarded the rest.

<a id="ew-referenced-resource-delivery" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 11.3 What a delivery states

A delivery carries one table per resource asked for. Which columns that table holds is a statement: an adapter MUST deliver a column wherever any row of that resource carries a value, and an absent column says the source produced no value for it in any row, which is Part 02 §5's presence and nothing else. A build MUST NOT read an absent column as saying anything more.

An adapter MUST hand over identifiers as the names its source used and never as numbers, because a writer mints every assigned identifier packet-wide and the numbers do not exist until it has (Part 05 §4.3). Types, column order, numbering and the declared sort are the build's: it holds the registry and settles all four on arrival.

A resource an adapter owns and has no rows for MUST arrive empty rather than not arriving. An empty delivery states that the source had nothing to say; a resource that does not arrive states that the run failed, and a build that cannot tell them apart cannot report either.

An adapter's arrays MUST NOT cross as rows. A dense array crosses by identity and location. Where
the packet will own the array, a build MUST supply a location under the staging area (Part 05 §2),
and an adapter reached by a protocol MUST write the array there, so promotion (Part 05 §6) moves it
to its final path by a rename within one filesystem (§3.4). An adapter a build runs in process MAY instead hand the build the
array's values, and the build writes the array there. Where the array is external (§8.2), a build
supplies no location and the producer returns the URI its source already has.

**A packet-owned array crosses as the bytes themselves.** A build MUST supply `array_location`, one
directory per source, since two sources may each deliver an asset of one name and a shared directory
would make them one array. Whichever of the adapter and the build writes an array into it MUST write
it as Zarr v3 (§6.1), in a directory named by the catalog row's `resource_id` with the suffix
`.zarr`. The build resolves the unique catalog-family declaration for that table-and-column pair,
uses the row's `resource_id` as packet identity, and MUST NOT substitute an assigned row ID or
source label. It MUST refuse a delivery where bytes and catalog rows do not bind one-to-one.

Nothing else crosses for such an array, and a build MUST NOT require an adapter to restate a Zarr
version, codec pipeline, layout, or other self-describing physical fact. A build inspects reachable
output as needed; `size_bytes` and `digest` come from the packet-owned bytes it digests (Part 03
§8.1). An adapter delivering an array therefore writes bytes where one delivering rows performs no
I/O at all, which is the cost of an array not being records.

**A referenced resource crosses as a resource document.** An adapter that delivers one MUST deliver
that document with it, under the reserved resource identifier `resources` (§11.4), which under
`files-v0.1` is the file `resources.json` in `delivery_root`: a JSON object carrying `resources`, an
array with one entry per such resource. Each entry is a manifest resource entry as [Part 04
§4.1](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-resource-entry-fields) declares one, without the members a
build supplies, so it MUST NOT carry `validation_state` or `derived_from`. A build preserves that
binding, rejects one that does not resolve or admit the resource ID, and merges the entry into the
manifest. Its shape is the delivery schema §1 incorporates. A build MUST refuse a document that does
not satisfy it before writing a manifest, naming what is wrong.

The document is part of the delivery and not a report. A build MUST NOT require a report and MUST NOT fail on one's contents (Part 09 §5.8), so a fact a manifest cannot be written without does not travel in one; a resource that does not arrive is already a failed run (Part 05 §4.4), which is the rule this needs and does not restate. A build MUST NOT count the document among the resources the recipe left the source owning.

### 11.4 The Arrow IPC floor

An Arrow IPC stream carries one schema and an adapter returns many resources, so under `stream-v0.1` the streams concatenate: each ends with the format's own end-of-stream marker, and the resource identifier rides in the schema's key-value metadata under the key `resource_id`. That key and two reserved values of it — `end`, and `resources` for the document §11.3 requires of an adapter delivering a referenced resource — are the whole of what this protocol adds to Arrow. A resource identifier is dotted, so no undotted token collides with either. A document carried this way travels as text in a single-row table rather than as a column per field, so the manifest's schema stays the one description of an entry.

"Arrow IPC" names a family, so this profile pins one member of it, as §5.5 pins Parquet's compressions. An adapter MUST write uncompressed record-batch bodies at metadata version V5, and MUST send each dictionary whole rather than as a delta against one already sent. Compressing buys nothing on a pipe and costs an adapter author a codec in a language whose Arrow binding may not carry one; V5 has been the default since Arrow 1.0, and a delta dictionary is the least evenly implemented part of the format.

An adapter MUST terminate every resource's stream with the end-of-stream marker and a build MUST consume exactly to it, so the next resource's schema begins at the next byte. An adapter that omits one delivers bytes no reader can frame; a build that consumes past one loses every resource after it and reports a truncation on a delivery that finished.

A reader treats end of file as end of stream, so a delivery cut at a message boundary parses clean and cannot be told from a short one. `stream-v0.1` therefore ends a delivery with an empty stream whose `resource_id` is `end`. A build MUST refuse a delivery that did not end with it, and MUST name the last resource it read as the one the adapter died inside rather than the first that never arrived. A resource identifier is dotted, so no undotted token can collide with the reserved value.

Where rows travel on standard output they are bytes. An adapter MUST write the stream to a binary handle and a build MUST read one, because a platform translating a line ending corrupts the stream, which then fails as malformed data rather than as an encoding error.

<a id="ew-adapter-declaration-document" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 11.5 The declaration document

A build reads what an adapter declares before it opens any source, and for a class it reads that by importing the adapter — which this section forbids requiring. An adapter a build cannot import MUST publish its declarations as a JSON document the recipe names, and a build MUST read that document before it launches anything.

The document carries what belongs to the adapter and nothing that belongs to the machine: `kind`, `protocols`, `produces`, `contributes`, `states`, `needs`, `accepts` and `consumes`. The command, the root and the options stay in the recipe, because which interpreter is installed where is the recipe author's fact and the resources an adapter can supply is not. This section incorporates `registry/adapter.schema.json`, whose `$id` is `https://escapewheel.org/schemas/adapter/0.1.0`, as the exact shape of the document. The schema owns member names, JSON types, required members, closure, and patterns; this prose owns what each member means and every refusal. The schema's descriptions are informative.

A document MUST carry all eight members, and each member listing names is an array. `protocols` names every protocol the adapter supports, at least one and none twice (§11.1); in any other member, an empty array declares none. `accepts` names options in the same form as `needs` (§11.2), and `contributes` names, as dotted resource identifiers, the resources the adapter supplies only in part (Part 05 §3.4).

A build MUST treat a document and an importable declaration of the same facts as one declaration, and MUST NOT prefer either where both are present. An implementation shipping both MUST generate the document rather than maintain it.

A build MUST check an importable declaration as the document holding the same facts, so a class and a document are refused for the same defects under the same terms. An importable declaration of an adapter a build ships may omit `protocols`, as §11.1 lets it; one of an adapter released independently of a build may not, even where a recipe runs it in process.

A build MUST refuse a declaration that fails the schema, naming where it fails, and one whose `protocols` names a protocol of no group this profile names, whose `produces` names a resource no loaded registry declares, whose `contributes` names a resource its `produces` does not, whose `states` names a field no recipe block carries, or whose `needs` names what §11.2 resolves to neither a term nor an option. A build MUST also refuse one whose `accepts` names a term, names an option its `needs` also names, or is not an array of names. None of those need the adapter opened or imported.

`states` names the recipe fields a source can state and the values need the source read, so no static document carries them: they travel under `stated` in the run's report (Part 09 §5.8), keyed by the same `block.field` name. An adapter declaring a non-empty `states` MUST return them. A build MUST read a declared field absent from the report as unstated rather than as disagreeing, since a source stating nothing is a fact and not a mismatch (Part 05 §3.6).

### 11.6 Reaching an adapter that must be running

Where a protocol needs the adapter running — `flight-v0.1` does, `files-v0.1` and `stream-v0.1` do not — the protocol MUST say how a build establishes that. `flight-v0.1` says it by connection: a build retries the address it supplied until the socket answers or the adapter's process exits, and reports the exit. A readiness line on standard output is weaker, because a build waiting for a line that never comes cannot tell *not yet* from *never* unless it watches the process as well, which is the whole of what retrying already does.

The retry puts one obligation on the adapter: it MUST NOT bind before it can answer `ListFlights`. A build's first call arrives as soon as the socket answers, so an adapter that binds and then extracts serves discovery from a half-built server.

A build that started a process owns it and terminates it once it holds the rows and the report, so an adapter MUST NOT rely on running code after its last response.

<a id="ew-packet-catalog-principles" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Principles

The manifest is the packet's map. A reader MUST resolve resources through it and MUST NOT infer them from file layout.

The manifest binds every resource instance. Part 02's declaration supplies logical content class,
kind, canonicality, instance mode, presence, table contract, and lineage policy; the packet
entry supplies instance and location facts. Part 03 derives a generic interface from the selected
profile and logical kind, while the resource owns self-describing physical metadata. An Iceberg catalog manages only tabular resources and
therefore does not replace the packet manifest.

The manifest identifies by name. `packet_id`, every registry ID, every entry's `resource_id`, and
the session list hold names rather than assigned numbers, because the manifest is read before any
table and a number resolves against nothing.

### 4.2 Resolved logical facts

The checked declaration named by `declaration_id` supplies the entry's owning registry, content
class, logical kind, instance mode, canonicality, table contract where applicable, presence, and

from URI, directory placement, namespace alone, or logical kind.

A singleton entry's `resource_id` equals its declaration ID. A catalog entry binds through the
catalog row's `resource_id` under Part 02 §2.6. A family entry uses a packet-unique dotted resource
ID in a namespace its declaration's registry owns. An unknown declaration or inadmissible instance
binding makes a conformance claim invalid.

<a id="ew-resource-digests" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 4.3 Resource digests

An entry's `digest` records a digest of the resource's bytes and names the function that produced
it, its **algorithm**, which is one of:

| algorithm | function | authority | single-object width |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sha256` | SHA-256 | FIPS 180-4 | 64 digits |
| `crc64nvme` | the NVMe 64-bit CRC | NVM Express NVM Command Set Specification, §5.3.1.3 | 16 digits |
| `crc32c` | CRC32C | RFC 9260, Appendix A | 8 digits |
| `crc32` | the gzip CRC-32 | RFC 1952, §8 | 8 digits |

[Part 04 §11](#ew-packet-catalog-normative-references) identifies the editions of FIPS 180-4, the
NVM Command Set Specification, and RFC 9260, and
[Part 00 §10](00_architecture.md#ew-normative-references) that of RFC 1952.

A single object's digest value is the function of the object's bytes, written as lowercase
hexadecimal of exactly the width above, most significant digit first. For a CRC, the digits are the
checksum as an unsigned integer. The value of the nine ASCII bytes `123456789` is
`15e2b0d3c33891ebb0f1ef609ec419420c20e320ce94c65fbc8c3312448eb225` under `sha256`,
`ae8b14860a799888` under `crc64nvme`, `e3069283` under `crc32c`, and `cbf43926` under `crc32`.

A resource whose URI names a prefix holds one or more objects. A prefix holding none does not
exist, and a validator reports it as it reports a resource that does not exist.

A prefix's digest value is the SHA-256 of the UTF-8 encoding of
`[["<key>",<size>,"<value>"], ...]`, where each `<value>` is that object's digest value under the
resource's algorithm. The listing is compact JSON with `,` and `:` separators, no whitespace, and
ASCII-escaped keys, with one entry per object, `<key>` relative to the resource's URI with `/`
separators, and entries sorted by `<key>` in byte order. A prefix's value therefore has 64 digits
under every algorithm. `size_bytes` is the sum of the objects' sizes.

Sorting the keys is what makes the value reproducible without knowing the store, since two
implementations enumerate an object-store prefix and a local directory in different orders.
Listing each object's key beside its own value means a chunk moving between keys, which makes a
different Zarr array, changes the resource's value.

A writer MUST record a `digest` for every resource the packet owns, and MAY record it under any
admitted algorithm. An external resource's `digest` is optional, and §5.2 states what a writer
records about a URI it cannot reach. A checker reports `packet.resource.digest_missing` (§10) for
each entry that does not declare `external: true` and records no `digest`.

## 5. External resources

### 5.1 Declaration

A resource the packet does not own MUST set `external: true`. A resource whose `uri` is absolute MUST declare `external: true`; **the converse does not hold**, so a packet MAY declare a resource external and still resolve it relatively, which is how a packet published beside its data records that it does not own it.

Referencing is expected for large resources. Externalizing a table costs more than externalizing an array: its validation rules cannot run at all while it is unreachable.

<a id="ew-external-resource-record" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.2 What is recorded

`digest`, `size_bytes`, and `verified_at` record what was true when the resource was checked. They are not a claim about now.

A writer that cannot reach a URI MUST record nothing rather than copying a value from a local stand-in.

<a id="ew-external-resource-verification" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.3 Verification levels

Verification of an external resource is layered, and a report MUST state which level it reached.

| level | establishes |
|---|---|
| 1 | the manifest is internally consistent |
| 2 | packet-owned resources' recorded digests hold |
| 3a | the external resource exists and lists |
| 3b | its recorded per-member store digests still match |
| 3c | its recorded `digest` holds |

Structural validation MUST NOT reach the network. Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode.

A validator establishes that a recorded digest holds by computing it from the bytes under §4.3. A
validator MAY instead establish it by comparing it with checksums the store reports, object by
object. A store's checksum counts only where the store reports it as a full-object value in the
resource's algorithm; a validator MUST NOT compare a composite value, and MUST read any object for
which the store reports no full-object value. For a single object it compares the store's value,
converted to §4.3's form, with the recorded value. For a prefix it builds §4.3's listing from those
values and compares its SHA-256.

A report MUST state, for each digest it establishes, whether the validator computed it, matched the
store's checksums, or combined the two. Level 2 establishes that packet-owned resources' recorded
digests hold, and level 3c that an external resource's recorded digest holds, each reached by
either route. Level 3b remains the comparison of recorded store digests (§5.4).
When an external resource is unavailable, its physical version, codec pipeline, compression,
encoding, dtype, shape, and chunking are unassessed; a validator MUST NOT infer them from its URI,
the packet, an adapter claim, or a stand-in.

Opening the manifest, resolving declarations, listing resources, and selecting a session MUST NOT
require a validation receipt or physical inspection of every resource. Before exposing logical
values, a reader inspects only the metadata needed for the selected resource and fails that
resource specifically when support is absent.

<a id="ew-prefix-resources" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.4 Prefix resources

Where a resource is a prefix rather than a single object — an external store, or a packet-owned Zarr array — its `digest` and `size_bytes` are defined over its objects by [§4.3](#ew-resource-digests).

Such a resource MAY additionally declare a `members` array recording what was seen, one member per object. A member records its object's single-object value as `digest`, a string under its entry's algorithm, and MAY carry a `store_digest`. The incorporated schema requires every member to record `digest` where its entry records one, and forbids a member recording one where its entry does not. Carrying each member's own value is what lets a consumer that fetched three chunks verify those three without the other two hundred.

A member array is a record, not a resolution rule: a reader hands the prefix to the array library and does not enumerate it.

Where `members` accompanies `digest`, the listing digested MUST be the declared one, so the enumeration and the digest cannot disagree.

A member's `store_digest` records evidence that an object changed in place. A writer MUST record a store's entity tag, where it records one, verbatim under the algorithm `etag`, whether the object was uploaded in one part or several and whatever its encryption. A writer MUST NOT record an entity tag under any other algorithm name, and a validator MUST NOT read one as a digest of the object's bytes.

Where a validator is asked to verify content **without reading**, it compares each member's recorded `store_digest` against the value the store reports now — a third level between listing and reading. A member whose value differs has changed; a member with no recorded `store_digest`, or whose store reports none, is **not checked**, and the report MUST say how many were skipped.

A store digest answers whether anything changed in place. It never establishes that a `digest` holds, and it is unstable across a copy. A validator MUST report what a verification pass skipped, and **MUST NOT** report a store-digest pass as a checksum verification: it establishes something weaker and store-specific, so it gets its own finding.

<a id="ew-source-resolution" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.5 Source resolution

An external resource entry MAY carry `source`, an identifier naming the body of data it belongs to;
its `uri` is then a path relative to wherever that source is, and neither field is a location. An
entry carrying no `source` keeps the meaning §5.1 gives it.

Where a source is, is stated by a **source resolution document**: a JSON document, conventionally
`sources.json`, carrying `sources_standard_version` and a `sources` object mapping each source name
to an entry with a required `uri`. This section incorporates `registry/sources.schema.json`, whose
`$id` is `https://escapewheel.org/schemas/sources/0.1.0`, as the exact shape of a source resolution
document. The schema owns member names, JSON types, required members, closure, patterns, and the
version constant; this prose owns what each member means. The schema's descriptions are
informative. A source name is any string an external resource entry's `source` can hold.

A resolution document MUST NOT be a packet resource. Immutability (§5.1) does not reach
it, which is what lets a location be corrected when a mirror moves without rebuilding the packet.

A reader MUST accept one or more resolution documents from its caller, and resolves a `source` in
this order: the documents the caller supplied, in the order supplied; then `sources.json` at the
packet root if it is there; then the source is unresolved. A writer SHOULD write that default.

A writer that writes a resolution document MUST write one that satisfies the schema. A reader MAY
validate a resolution document against the schema, and need not. A reader MUST NOT resolve a source
through a document whose `sources_standard_version` it does not know.

A reader MUST report an unresolved `source` as unresolved, and a validator MUST NOT fail structural validation for one:
which mirrors a reader can reach is a property of the reader. Structural validation MUST NOT
require a resolution document.

A consumer MUST NOT read resolution through a different document as invalidating a recorded `digest`,
`size_bytes` or `members`. Where a reader verifies a resolved source and finds a mismatch, it MUST
report it against the resource entry, and MUST NOT silently prefer either the recorded digest or
the bytes it found.

<a id="ew-packet-codecs" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 6. Physical metadata is not projected

`profile_id` is the manifest's sole physical-profile selector. Generic interfaces derive under Part
03, and resource-owned metadata supplies actual versions, layouts, compression, encodings, and
codec pipelines. The manifest carries no replacement capability union or per-resource selector.

<a id="ew-resource-lineage" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. Resource lineage

The declaration's `lineage` policy, which Part 02 §2.7 defines, governs `derived_from` for every
logical kind. A present `derived_from` is a nonempty array of distinct existing packet resource IDs
and obeys that section's content-class relation and acyclic graph rule.

An entry names no producing run. Provenance resides in `core.source_files`,
`core.processing_runs`, and `core.processing_run_inputs`, and the manifest records outputs at
resource granularity only. [Part 06 §5](06_analysis_layer.md#ew-analysis-materialization-provenance)

## 8. Registry bindings

Each member name is a registry ID under
[Part 02 §9.1](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-registry-document), and each value is a closed
registry descriptor:

<!-- BEGIN GENERATED MANIFEST FIELDS: registry -->

|---|---|:---:|---|

the same `path`. The document at a descriptor's path MUST:

- carry `registry_id` equal to the descriptor's member name;
- be a core registry exactly when the descriptor's role is `core`;
- carry `registry_version` equal to the descriptor's `version`; and
- carry `logical_standard_version` equal to the manifest's, and, for the core registry,
  `semantic_standard_version` equal to the manifest's.

A packet MUST bind the core registry and every extension registry that declares one of its
resources. Binding an extension registry activates its required declarations under
[Part 02 §2.5](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-resource-presence). Every `declaration_id` resolves

<a id="ew-packet-manifest-conformance" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 10. Manifest conformance

A checker evaluates manifest conformance in these phases, in order:

1. `json`, which decodes the manifest as UTF-8 JSON under RFC 8259;
2. `shape`, which validates it against the incorporated schema;
3. `entries`, which runs checks that need only the manifest;
4. `registry_binding`, which acquires and compares every bound registry; and
5. `declaration`, which runs checks derived from the bound declarations.

A checker MUST NOT run a later phase over a manifest with a finding in `json`, `shape`, or
`entries`. A checker evaluating manifest shape MUST assert the `date-time` format that JSON Schema
Validation Draft 2020-12 defines. A shape finding carries the check ID `packet.manifest.schema`
and the schema-location pointer. Every finding in the table below is at `error` and names the
owning resource ID or registry ID where one applies.

| check ID | phase | a finding exists when |
|---|---|---|
| `packet.manifest.invalid_json` | json | the bytes are not UTF-8 or not JSON |
| `packet.manifest.duplicate_member` | json | an object repeats a member name |
| `packet.manifest.schema` | shape | the manifest violates the incorporated schema |
| `packet.resource.id_unique` | entries | two entries share a `resource_id` |
| `packet.resource.member_unique` | entries | two members of one entry share a `key` |
| `packet.resource.member_size` | entries | an entry with `members` records `size_bytes` unequal to the sum of the members' sizes |
| `packet.resource.member_digest` | entries | an entry records `digest` and `members`, and its value differs from the §4.3 value of that member list |
| `packet.resource.digest_missing` | entries | an entry that does not declare `external: true` records no `digest` |
| `packet.registry.core_count` | entries | the number of core descriptors differs from one |
| `packet.registry.path_unique` | entries | two descriptors share a `path` |
| `packet.registry.missing` | registry_binding | nothing exists at a descriptor's path |
| `packet.registry.unreadable` | registry_binding | the stored copy does not decode as the packet's physical profile requires |
| `packet.registry.digest` | registry_binding | the decoded bytes' SHA-256 differs from `sha256` |
| `packet.registry.identity` | registry_binding | the document's `registry_id` differs from the descriptor's name |
| `packet.registry.role` | registry_binding | the document's class differs from the descriptor's role |
| `packet.registry.version` | registry_binding | the document's `registry_version` differs from `version` |
| `packet.registry.standard` | registry_binding | a standard identifier differs from the manifest's under §8 |
| `packet.resource.declaration_unknown` | declaration | `declaration_id` names no declaration in a bound registry |
| `packet.resource.singleton_identity` | declaration | a singleton entry's `resource_id` differs from its declaration ID |
| `packet.resource.family_namespace` | declaration | a family entry's `resource_id` lies outside its declaration's namespace |
| `packet.resource.required_missing` | declaration | an active required singleton has no entry |
| `packet.resource.dependency_missing` | declaration | a present declaration's transitive `requires` names an absent singleton |
| `packet.resource.array_axes_missing` | declaration | a dense-array entry's axes resource is absent |
| `packet.resource.reference_target_missing` | declaration | a populated table reference targets an absent singleton |
| `packet.resource.catalog_table_unreadable` | declaration | a catalog table's resource-ID column cannot be read |
| `packet.resource.catalog_row_binding` | declaration | a catalog row and a family entry do not bind one-to-one under Part 02 §2.6 |
| `packet.resource.derived_from_required` | declaration | the `lineage` policy is `required` and `derived_from` is absent |
| `packet.resource.derived_from_forbidden` | declaration | the `lineage` policy is `forbidden` and `derived_from` is present |
| `packet.resource.lineage_unresolved` | declaration | a `derived_from` member names no entry with a bound declaration |
| `packet.resource.lineage_class` | declaration | a lineage edge violates Part 02 §2.7's content-class relation |
| `packet.resource.lineage_cycle` | declaration | the lineage graph has a self-edge or cycle |

In the `registry_binding` phase, a checker MUST evaluate `missing`, `unreadable`, and `digest`
first. Only for decoded bytes that match the digest does it then obtain the
[Part 02 §9.4](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-registry-conformance) registry result through the
byte-oriented entry point, supplying the core context to every extension, and evaluate the four
comparison checks for each shape-valid document. The `declaration` phase runs only from checked
contexts that [Part 05 §14.5](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-validator-registry-boundary) permits
to direct packet checks.

A binding finding for an extension registry withholds only that registry. The `declaration` phase
MUST run from every bound registry that passed binding, and a checker MUST NOT report a
declaration finding for an entry whose `declaration_id` namespace is the ID of an extension
registry that failed. A failed core registry withholds the whole `declaration` phase, because every
extension's checked context needs core.
[Part 05 §9.5](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-verdict-composition) attributes each finding and
composes the verdicts.

A reader loading a manifest MUST decode it as UTF-8 JSON, MUST reject a repeated object member, and
MUST reject a repeated `resource_id` before it builds a lookup by resource ID or exposes an entry.
It MUST check the JSON type of each member it reads and fail naming a member that is absent where
the schema requires it or has the wrong type. A reader need not apply the incorporated schema or
assert its closure, patterns, or formats. A reader MAY expose the decoded JSON of a rejected
manifest as raw data but MUST NOT resolve declarations or resources through it.

A reader resolving a declaration MUST acquire the bound registry that declares it through its
descriptor, decode it as the packet's physical profile requires, and verify its digest. When the
registry is missing, unreadable, or fails the digest, the reader MUST fail the operation that needed
it and name the registry ID. It MUST NOT substitute other bytes or silently omit a bound registry.
Operations that need no such declaration proceed, and a reader need not run any other manifest check
or obtain a registry result for ordinary access.

A writer MUST serialize each registry document, compute its digest over those exact bytes, store it
as the packet's physical profile requires, and record its descriptor before writing the final
manifest. A writer MUST NOT publish a manifest with a finding in the `json`, `shape`, `entries`, or
`registry_binding` phase, and publishes any manifest only as
[Part 05 §9.2](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-error-policy) permits.

# Part 05 — Validation, Import, and Reader Operations

Normative.

<a id="ew-import-validation-principle" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Core principle

**Validation is importation.** Copying bytes into a packet directory is staging, not import.

A resource is imported when a build has staged it, validated it with the whole staged packet,
promoted it, and published a manifest naming it, in the order §2 states.

A staged resource MUST NOT appear in the committed manifest. Validation state does not change the
content class or canonicality selected by its declaration; publication is the gate.

<a id="ew-import-pipeline" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Pipeline

A build MUST perform these steps in this order:

1. discover what each source offers, writing nothing (§4.1);
2. deliver each source's resources through the locations and routes the build supplies (§4.2);
3. normalize them to the logical schemas and write the result to the staging area (§4.3);
4. record the import's provenance in the staging area (§7);
5. materialize analysis resources from the staged packet into the staging area (§8);
6. validate the whole staged packet (§5);
7. promote the staged resources to their final locations (§6); and
8. publish the packet manifest (§6).

A build MAY interleave steps 1 to 3 across sources, within the constraints §4.3 places on
normalization, and a packet-owned array delivered into the staging area is staged by its delivery.
A build MUST finish steps 4 and 5 before it begins step 6, MUST NOT begin step 7 until step 6 has
returned a packet verdict of `passed` ([§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition)), and MUST NOT begin step 8
until step 7 has finished.

The **staging area** is the location a build or an amendment owns while it runs and into which it
writes what it will publish. What it holds belongs to no packet until promotion moves it to its
final location. A physical profile names where its staging area lies; the portable profile's is
[Part 03 §3.4](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-staging-area).

A writer MAY empty or remove a staging area:

- after it publishes the manifest of the build or amendment that owns it;
- when it begins a build at the same packet root; and
- when it begins an amendment of the packet at that root.

A writer MUST NOT empty or remove a staging area that holds a promotion record until it has acted
on the record as §6 requires.

A writer MUST NOT combine parsing, normalization, validation, and writing into one opaque step.

An adapter MUST deliver only through the channels and locations a build supplies it, and a build
MUST read a delivery from nowhere else. A build owns each location it chooses for a delivery, and
may choose the place the delivered bytes will stay, as
[Part 03 §11.3](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-referenced-resource-delivery) has a packet-owned
array written under the staging area. A build MUST supply each such location empty, a directory
holding nothing or a path where nothing exists, since a build identifies what an adapter wrote
there by name and a name an earlier run left would pass for the delivery. An adapter MUST NOT rely
on what a location held before its run, or on what it writes there remaining after the build. This
rule governs a delivery and nothing else an adapter writes;
[Part 03 §11](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-reaching-a-source) names the portable profile's
routes.

## 3. The session recipe

<a id="ew-recipe-purpose" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.1 Purpose

One source is rarely sufficient: integer sample indices come from a sorter, task structure and registration from an exported file, and session identity from acquisition metadata. The unit of work is therefore a **recipe** naming several sources, not a single input file.

A recipe declares, for one packet:

- each source, once per kind: its kind, its root, and its options;
- which resources each source supplies;
- which source owns a resource more than one listed source can supply (§3.3);
- the clocks the packet declares, and their parents;
- what to materialize;
- session facts no source carries.

A recipe MAY declare a fact a source it reads also states; §3.6 says which of the two the packet gets. `core.subjects` is not a resource the recipe supplies unconditionally: a source that states a subject MAY own it, arbitrated as any other resource two sources can produce, and the recipe's `[subject]` block supplies the table only where no source owns it.

A recipe is a [TOML 1.0](#ew-validation-normative-references) document. This part incorporates `registry/recipe.schema.json`, whose `$id` is `https://escapewheel.org/schemas/recipe/0.1.0`, as the shape of a recipe's decoded form. The schema owns the member names, types, required members, closure and patterns; this prose owns what each member means and every refusal of §3.5. TOML dates and times decode to values JSON Schema has no type for, so the schema leaves the facts under `[session]` and `[subject]` untyped, and §3.6 types each when it normalizes it. An omitted member keeps the meaning this part gives its absence. The table is an informative projection of the schema.

| member | form | constraint |
|---|---|---|
| `packet` | table | requires `id`; admits `out`, `registry`, `extensions`, `description`, and `in_process` |
| `session`, `subject` | table | keys are field names; values are untyped |
| `overrides` | table | keys are `block.field` names; each value a non-empty string (§3.6) |
| `encoding` | table | keys are `resource.column` names; each entry carries exactly `encoding`, one of `delta`, `plain`, or `dictionary`, and a non-empty `reason` (§3.8) |
| `clock` | array of tables | each requires `clock_id`, `name`, `tick_rate_num`, and `origin`, and admits `tick_rate_den` and `parent_clock_id` |
| `source` | array of tables | each requires `kind`; `owns` and `contributes` are arrays of dotted resource identifiers, `storage_options` a table, `in_process` a boolean (§3.9), and `delivery` a table admitting `command`, an array of strings (§3.7), and `protocol`, `declaration`, and `report_path`; every other key is an option |
| `preference` | table | keys are dotted resource identifiers; each entry is §3.3's |
| `materialize` | array of tables | each requires `resource`; every other key is a parameter |
| `commit` | table | members are a build's own limits |

A recipe MUST NOT list a source kind in more than one `source` entry, and a build MUST refuse one that does, naming the kind. A preference, an ownership refusal, and a build's reports name a source by its kind.

Each entry of the `[packet]` member `extensions` names one extension registry, and a build MUST
bind every registry so named under
[Part 09 §5.3](09_metadata_and_extensions.md#ew-extension-registration). This specification does
not fix how a build resolves an entry to a registry document, whether it reads the entry as a path
beside the recipe or as the identifier of a registry an implementation has installed.

A recipe MUST NOT list one extension in more than one `extensions` entry, and a build MUST refuse
one that does, naming the `extension_id` and each entry that resolves to it, before it opens any
source. Two entries list one extension when the registry documents they resolve to carry the same
`extension_id`, whether the entries repeat one string, pair a path with an identifier, or name two
versions of the registry.

### 3.2 The recipe is the provenance record

A writer MUST record the recipe in the import run's parameters, and its digest as the run's `code_uri`. A committed packet therefore states what produced it.

<a id="ew-recipe-ownership" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.3 Ownership

Each source declares its resources as `owns` or `contributes`.

| declaration | meaning |
|---|---|
| `owns` | this source is solely responsible; records another source emits for the resource are discarded |
| `contributes` | this source adds rows alongside others |

`contributes` exists because some resources genuinely have several suppliers. Without the distinction, the last adapter to run wins silently.

A recipe MUST NOT declare `owns` for a resource that more than one of its sources produces rows for, unless it declares a preference between them. Exclusivity is for a resource one source is responsible for; where two sources each hold part, `owns` discards the part the owner does not hold, and no check sees it because the resource is claimed.

A recipe declares each preference as an entry of its top-level `preference` table, keyed by the resource's dotted identifier. The entry carries `source`, the kind of the source the recipe prefers, and `reason`, a non-empty statement of why that source knows more, and nothing else.

A preference declares ownership. A build MUST read the preferred source as owning the resource whatever its entry declares, and MUST discard every other source's rows for it, subject to §3.4's refusal of a discarded stated value. A build MUST apply each declared preference, and MUST NOT hold a preference of its own or accept one from any artifact other than the recipe: a built-in winner is one no recipe can see, and a recipe that has not heard of a new contest is better refused than silently arbitrated.

A preference MUST NOT name a resource only one source produces. Such an entry arbitrates nothing and exempts a report that will never be due, so it is a claim no build tests, and it goes stale the moment the second producer stops producing.

<a id="ew-source-declarations" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.4 What a source can produce

Every source adapter MUST declare the set of resource identifiers it can supply. It is static: a property of the adapter, readable with nothing open, and distinct from what a particular source root happens to hold.

An adapter declares six such facts, all read before any source is opened: the resources it can supply; those of them whose rows it supplies only in part, such as the clocks of its own streams; the recipe fields it can state (§3.6); the values a build must supply it (Part 03 §11.2); the options it reads and can run without (Part 03 §11.2); and the kinds whose output it reads, which is what orders a build. An adapter a build cannot import publishes them as the declaration document Part 03 §11.5 specifies, where they are `produces`, `contributes`, `states`, `needs`, `accepts` and `consumes`; what an implementation calls them in code is Part 07 §4's.

A build MUST read a source entry that declares no `owns` as follows. Of the resources its adapter declares it can supply, leaving out any another entry declares under `owns` and any a preference gives to another source, the entry contributes those its adapter supplies only in part and owns the rest. It also contributes every resource its own `contributes` names, and owns none of those. An entry that declares `owns` owns exactly what it names and contributes what its `contributes` names. **A recipe states what it narrows; silence means the whole.**

A build MUST refuse a recipe in which two entries without `owns` are left owning one resource, naming both sources and the resource.

A build MUST report rows a source produced for a resource another source owns, except where the recipe declares a preference between them. That another source claims it is not the exemption: a preference says two sources offer the same rows and names the one that knows more, and without one they are producing different rows. The neighbouring case, a resource *no* source claims, is reported as a warning before a byte is read.

A build MUST refuse where a preference discards a value another source stated: where a passed-over source's row holds a non-null value in a column the preferred source's row leaves null, or does not produce at all. Where a preferred source produces no rows for the resource and another does, that is the rule's limiting case. The comparison is over declared columns and not over `metadata`, which §8 of Part 02 makes schemaless by design, so two sources filling it differently is not a difference a build can weigh. That refusal is what makes the exemption above sound: the exemption rests on the loser's copy being redundant, and a preference is declared before either source is opened.

A build MUST NOT compose a row from more than one source. The merged row would be a fact no source stated. A recipe resolves the refusal by naming the fact in `[overrides]` with its reason, by preferring the other source, or by correcting the source that states less.

A build MUST also be able to determine, for each source a recipe names and without opening it, which recipe fields that source can **state**, and MUST refuse a recipe naming a source it cannot determine that for. What an implementation calls that declaration in code is Part 07 §4's, and the key it takes in a declaration document is Part 03 §11.5's.

<a id="ew-recipe-check" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.5 Checking a recipe

A build MUST check a recipe against the registry before it opens any source. A build MUST refuse:

- a resource owned by more than one source;
- an owned resource the registry does not define;
- an owned table whose **required** foreign key targets a table nothing supplies;
- a clock declared twice, referenced but absent, or parented to nothing;
- a required source option that is not set.

A build MUST also refuse, on the same terms and before any source is opened:

- an adapter declaring a value Part 03 §11.2 resolves to neither a term nor a recipe option;
- an adapter declaring a term that list carries and this recipe cannot fill, naming the term and the source;
- an adapter released independently of a build declaring no protocol of a group the profile names, or any adapter declaring one it does not name;
- a declaration failing the schema Part 03 §11.5 incorporates, whether a document or an importable declaration, naming where it fails;
- a source entry naming a protocol its adapter does not declare, where the adapter declares any, naming the source and the protocol;
- a declaration whose resources include one no loaded registry declares;
- a declaration whose stated fields include one no recipe block carries;
- a source entry setting an option the adapter names in neither `needs` nor `accepts`, naming the source and the option;
- a declaration whose accepted options include a term, or one its `needs` also names;
- a declaration naming a resource it supplies only in part that is not one it declares it can supply, naming the adapter and the resource;
- a preference whose `source` names a kind the recipe does not list, naming the resource;
- a preference whose `source` names a kind whose adapter does not declare that it can supply the resource, naming the resource;
- a preference for a resource another entry declares under `owns` or `contributes`, naming the resource and that entry's kind, since the recipe then says both that one source knows more and that the other supplies the resource.

A build MUST refuse a recipe that is not TOML 1.0 or whose decoded form fails the schema §3.1 incorporates, naming where it fails, before it opens any source.

A build MUST report as a **warning**:

- an owned table whose **nullable** foreign key targets a table nothing supplies;
- a resource some listed source declares in `PRODUCES` that no source claims;
- a field the recipe declares that some listed source states and no override covers.

A warning is not an error because narrowing is legitimate, and only the recipe's author knows whether this one was meant.

### 3.6 Authority for a declared fact

Where a recipe declares a fact and a source the same recipe reads states that fact, a build MUST report a difference between the two, naming the field, the declared value, the source's value and the file it came from. A build MUST refuse only where the fact identifies something a reader resolves across packets; otherwise it proceeds, and `--strict` refuses. `[subject].subject_id` is that case and the only one: a subject is not scoped to a session, so a wrong one agrees with another packet's animal instead of colliding with the true value.

Two values are compared at the type the packet will store, both normalized to it first. A source stating no value for a declared fact is not a disagreement: the declaration stands, a writer MUST NOT report it as a mismatch, and SHOULD note that it went unchecked.

A recipe MAY declare that its own value supersedes the source's, by naming the field in a top-level `[overrides]` table keyed `block.field`. The value MUST be a non-empty reason. Declaring an override is never required: it records a decision and silences the report, and a recipe declaring none is conforming. A build MUST report an override for a field the source agrees with, or that no source states, and MUST NOT refuse on either.

The comparison needs the source read, so it happens as each source is built, and a build MUST refuse before any resource is staged. A source's statement of a fact is whatever its adapter exposes for that fact; which of several values inside a file it stands behind is the adapter's judgement and not the recipe's.

A fact the source **derives** is the source's. `core.sessions.session_start_time` and `session_end_time` come from the session source's declared stream windows, and a writer MUST use the source's value where a session source is present and MUST report a declaration it did not use rather than discarding it silently.

### 3.7 When a source runs a command

A command that exits non-zero MUST fail the build, and a build MUST name the source whose command failed together with the resources that source owns, rather than reporting the first resource it then finds missing. A command belongs to a source and a source is not an extension: one command may produce tables of several extensions, so a finding naming one of them would be wrong about the rest.

A recipe MUST carry a command as an argument list, and a build MUST NOT derive one by splitting a string. A parse is a quoting convention the recipe never declared: POSIX rules applied on every host eat a backslash everywhere, so `C:\Python\python.exe` becomes `C:Pythonpython.exe` and fails as a missing executable rather than as a malformed command. A list needs no convention, because the host's own process interface takes one.

Where the command is one a build reaches a source through (Part 03 §11.2), the build MUST write what it supplies that source to the command's standard input and MUST close the stream when it has, without waiting for the command to exit, and MUST read what the command produces while it writes. A command that converts a raw source before it is read is not one of these and is supplied nothing. A pipe holds tens of kilobytes and a supplied object may be far larger, so a build that wrote one whole before reading rows would wait on an adapter that was waiting on it.

A build MUST capture a command's standard output and standard error, and MUST report them with a failure of that command. Where the command succeeds, a build SHOULD keep them readable afterwards. Where the recipe puts rows on standard output (Part 03 §11.1), a build MUST NOT report standard output as a diagnosis, and standard error is where a command that also emits data prints one. Holding them in memory is not what this asks: a command may run for minutes and its progress is worth watching while it does, so a build writes the streams to a file and reports that file and its last lines.

### 3.8 A recipe declares an encoding

A recipe MAY declare a column's Parquet encoding, in an `[encoding]` table keyed `resource.column`,
each entry naming one of `delta`, `plain` or `dictionary` and the reason it was measured. An entry
MUST carry a reason, since a bare choice cannot be re-judged when the data moves.

A writer's own exceptions MUST name only tables of the layer it implements, and an extension's MUST
be declarable without the writer importing anything the extension ships — a writer may require no
code of an extension, so a declaration arriving as a Python object would be one only an
importable extension could make.

The recipe is also the right granularity rather than the only reachable one. An encoding is a fact
about one packet's values: a column repeating often enough to want a dictionary in one session may
not in the next, where a schema-level declaration would claim it of every packet. §3.6's
`[overrides]` puts a chosen value and its reason in a recipe for the same reason — the choice
belongs to the build.

<a id="ew-in-process-opt-in" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 3.9 Whether a source runs in the writer's process

A build MUST deliver a source whose adapter declares a protocol out of process unless the recipe declares that it runs in the writer's, and a writer MUST NOT run such a source in process where the recipe does not say so. A build MUST run an adapter it ships that declares no protocol in process unless its recipe entry gives it a `delivery` table naming a command (§§3.1 and 3.7), and then serves it over a protocol of the group (Part 03 §11.1); a build MUST NOT require a recipe to opt such an adapter into the writer's process.

The recipe declares it because the recipe author chose the environment. One process resolves one dependency closure, so whether a given set of adapters installs together is a fact about one machine on one day rather than a property of any adapter, and the person assembling the recipe is the only party who can find out and the one who carries it when the answer changes.

What the opt-in buys is a saved transfer and what it costs is a solvable environment. A build that cannot import an adapter the recipe opted in MUST refuse, naming the source and the import that failed, before any source is opened.

<a id="ew-import-sources" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. Discover, deliver, and normalize

### 4.1 Discover

For each source, report what it offers per resource, and how many rows. Discovery MUST NOT write to the packet.

<a id="ew-import-delivery" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 4.2 Deliver

A build reads each source's delivery where it supplied it (§2), which need not be the staging area,
and writes what it normalizes from it into the staging area (§4.3). A writer MUST stream a table
whose row count is large rather than materializing it as host objects.

### 4.3 Normalize

Map source objects onto the logical schemas: assign identifiers packet-wide before any table is written, apply the declared sort, and coerce types to the registry's.

Normalization MUST be deterministic in the recipe. Two runs of the same recipe over the same sources produce the same packet.

An interval is closed (01 §2.3). A writer MUST convert a source whose intervals are half-open — `[start, stop)` — which is a subtraction on the stop bound. A writer MUST NOT carry a half-open bound through unchanged.

An adapter compensating for a known defect in its source MUST declare the compensation, and a writer MUST record every compensation applied in the import run's parameters. Choosing not to correct a known defect is equally a compensation and is declared as a pass-through.

### 4.4 When a source's delivery is complete

What a build expects of a source is the set of resources it owns, settled from `owns` and what the adapter declares before any I/O (§3.4). A run succeeded when every resource in that set arrived and the process exited clean.

A resource an adapter owns and has nothing for MUST arrive empty rather than not arriving (Part 03 §11.3). That is what makes the test usable: a build reads a resource that did not arrive as the run having failed, and never as the source having had nothing to say.

A non-zero exit is a failure whatever arrived. A build MUST report one as a failure even where every resource is present, because an adapter saying it failed is not overruled by its own output, and a command dying between two resources delivers the first one complete and valid.

A build MUST report a delivery it cannot parse as a failure where the command exited zero, naming what was short. What no protocol catches is a short table from a run that exits zero: where an adapter declares a row count a build MUST check it, and an adapter that cannot know its size before it sends declares none, which a build MUST read as unknown rather than as zero.

A build MUST report everything the protocol it used carries about a failure — standard error for `files-v0.1` and `stream-v0.1`, and the error's message together with its detail for `flight-v0.1`, where a failing adapter writes nothing to standard error because the diagnosis travels on the connection it was asked over. A build SHOULD try every resource it was waiting for and report every failure together rather than stopping at the first: the answer a person wants is which resources did not arrive, not which one did not arrive first.

<a id="ew-import-validation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 5. Validate

<a id="ew-tier-1-validation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.1 Tier 1

Generated from structure (Part 02 §10.1): required-column non-null, primary-key uniqueness, foreign-key anti-joins, closed-vocabulary membership, and the finiteness of every column declaring `finite: true`.

<a id="ew-tier-2-validation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.2 Tier 2

Declared per table under Part 02 §10.2 and interpreted by the registry's selected language. Before
execution, a validator MUST support that exact language and MUST parse, statically validate, bind,
and type-check the rule as Part 11 requires. It MUST execute only a checked query. A language or
profile defect makes the registry invalid rather than producing a packet finding.

<a id="ew-validation-shim" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.3 The shim

A validator MUST first evaluate manifest conformance under
[Part 04 §10](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-manifest-conformance), which owns the declaration
resolution, instance, presence, dependency, catalog, reference-target, and lineage checks, before
dispatching kind-specific checks.

A validator MUST derive the physical interface from the checked logical kind and the selected
profile, then check available resource-owned metadata: physical order, file presence, Parquet
types and footer features, Zarr version, rank and codecs, and checksums outside SQL. A query cannot
observe them, so such a check written as SQL fails silently rather than loudly. Dense-array checks
obtain the axes table from the resolved declaration.

A validator MUST assess floor membership for every packet-owned resource whose interface declares a
reader floor, and for every external one whose format-owned metadata it reads under
[Part 04 §5.3](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-external-resource-verification). Membership is judged under
[Part 03 §5.5](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-parquet-reader-floor) for Parquet and
[Part 03 §6.4](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-array-codecs) for Zarr. For each assessed resource
using a feature outside its floor, it MUST report one finding under the check ID
`physical.outside_reader_floor`, naming:

- the resource;
- its interface and, for Zarr, its variant; and
- the observed features that matched no floor entry: each outside compression or encoding for
  Parquet, the whole observed pipeline for Zarr v3, and `filters` and `compressor` for Zarr v2.

The finding's level is `warning` for a packet-owned resource whose interface's floor role is
`reader_and_packet_writer`, and `info` for every other resource. A validator MUST NOT report it at
`error`. The finding concerns one resource, so [§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition) attributes it to the
registry whose declaration the resource's entry names.

A validator MUST report each external resource whose interface declares a reader floor and whose
format-owned metadata it did not read, one finding per resource, at `info` under the check ID
`external.physical_unassessed`, naming the resource. [§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition) attributes it
as it does the outside-floor finding.

The shim MUST compare the manifest's session inventory with `core.sessions.session_name` under the
check ID `core.sessions.manifest_sessions_match`, at `error`, with the message carried by the
registry before that check moved here. It reports each non-null table value absent from the
manifest, preserving duplicates, and each manifest session absent from every table row. The shim
declares the packet manifest and `core.sessions` as inputs. No Tier 2 rule may depend on the shim's
implementation mechanism.

The shim MUST parse each distinct non-null value of each
[unit column](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-unit-and-clock-context) in every table resource whose
declaration a registry directing packet checks holds, under the UCUM edition Part 02 §8.4 names. It
MUST report each value that is not a unit code at `error` under the check ID
`<declaration_id>.<column>.ucum_code`, such as `core.units.amplitude_unit.ucum_code`, naming that
value once. The finding belongs to the registry holding that declaration under
[§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition). The shim declares each such table as an input.

<a id="ew-validation-extensions" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.4 Extensions

A validator MUST apply to each resource an extension declares every check of §§5.1–5.3 that a core
resource receives because of its logical kind or its declarations, directed by that extension's
checked context. A check that applies to one named core resource, such as the session-inventory
check of §5.3, does not extend. [§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition) assigns the extension's findings to
it and composes its verdict with core's.

<a id="ew-import-commit" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 6. Commit

A **commit** is steps 7 and 8 of §2: **promotion**, which moves a validated staged packet's
resources to their final locations, and **publication** of its manifest at `packet.json`, last and
atomically. Commit MUST be atomic with respect to a concurrent reader: a reader MUST observe either
the previously committed manifest or the newly published one, and MUST NOT observe a manifest naming
a resource that is not yet in place. A writer MUST NOT publish a manifest until every packet-owned
resource it names is at its final location and has passed validation.

The publication mechanism is the profile's. A filesystem profile MAY publish by atomic rename
within one directory; an object-store profile MAY publish by a single-object write. A writer MUST
NOT be required to perform an atomic **directory** rename, which no object store
offers and which the packet layout does not need — the manifest is one object.

Where the store offers a conditional write, a writer publishing a new version SHOULD use it, so
two writers cannot silently overwrite one another's head. Where it offers none, a writer MUST NOT
claim it detected a conflict it could not detect.

A writer whose validation returns a packet verdict other than `passed` promotes nothing, MUST NOT
write a manifest at the packet root, and SHOULD leave its staging area in place for inspection.

Before a build or an amendment writes anything outside its staging area, the writer MUST write a
**promotion record** into the staging area and make it durable. The record states the
`packet_version` the writer will publish and lists, relative to the packet root, every file and
directory the writer will create outside the staging area before it publishes, other than the
manifest. For an import these are the promoted resource directories, the bound registries, the
source resolution document, and any file publication writes before it installs the manifest; for
an amendment, every file it writes and the superseded manifest it retains
([Part 03 §9.3](03_portable_physical_profile.md#ew-superseded-manifest-retention)). A writer MUST
refuse to promote where a path its record lists already exists, naming the path, and MUST NOT
create outside the staging area a path its record neither lists nor places inside a listed
directory. This specification does not fix the record's format.

A listed path, with everything inside it, belongs to the build or amendment that wrote the record
until a manifest carrying the record's `packet_version` is published. A writer beginning a build or
an amendment at a packet root whose staging area holds a promotion record MUST read the record
before it empties the staging area or writes anything else, and then:

- where no manifest is published at the packet root, or the published manifest's `packet_version`
  sorts before the record's as a semantic version, the writer MAY remove every listed path that
  exists, and then proceeds as a new build or amendment; and
- where the published manifest's `packet_version` equals the record's or sorts after it, the record
  is stale, and the writer MUST NOT remove a listed path.

A writer that cannot read a promotion record MUST NOT remove any path outside the staging area on
the record's account, and MUST refuse to build or amend at that packet root, naming the record.

<a id="ew-import-provenance" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. Provenance

A writer MUST record, in the staging area at step 4 of §2:

- one `core.processing_runs` row for the import, declaring its `scope` and `scope_kind`;
- one `core.source_files` row for each distinct `root` among the recipe's sources (§3.1), whose
  `uri` is that root as the recipe states it; and
- one `core.processing_run_inputs` row linking each of those `core.source_files` rows to the import
  run.

A writer need not read a source to fill `sha256`, `size_bytes`, `format`, or `modified_at`, each of
which may be null.

<a id="ew-import-materialization" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 8. Materialize

Materialization is step 5 of §2: a build opens the staged packet, computes each derived resource
from it, and writes the result to the staging area, so step 6 validates it with core before
anything is promoted.

Deriving into an already-committed packet is an amendment (03 §9.1), not part of an import.

## 9. Reporting

### 9.1 Findings

A finding declares a level, the check that produced it, and a message. Where a check is a query, the offending rows are the finding.

| level | meaning |
|---|---|
| `error` | the packet does not conform |
| `warning` | conformant, but likely not intended |
| `info` | recorded, not judged |

<a id="ew-error-policy" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.2 Error policy

A writer MUST NOT publish a manifest, whether as a first commit or as a later version, unless the
verdict of the packet it describes is `passed` under [§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition), and MUST
record `passed` as that manifest's `validation.status`. A packet whose extension fails is therefore
published in no partial form; its writer corrects the extension or builds the packet without it.

A validator MUST NOT downgrade an error to make a packet pass. An advisory rule MUST NOT be an error (Part 02 §10.2), so enabling advisory checks cannot change whether a packet previously passed.

### 9.3 Comparison against a prior packet

Where an import replaces an existing packet, it SHOULD report every resource the prior manifest declared that the new one does not. This is a warning: narrowing is legitimate, and no other check sees a resource that is simply absent.

<a id="ew-incomplete-validation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.4 Incomplete validation

A report MUST distinguish an invalid registry rule, an unsupported selected language, an
incomplete valid evaluation, and a completed query that produced packet findings. An unreadable
input, engine failure, exact overflow, out-of-range timestamp, timeout, or resource limit makes the
affected required evaluation incomplete. A validator MUST NOT turn its inability to evaluate into
an error-level packet finding. Core and every extension retain separate evaluation state.

<a id="ew-verdict-composition" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 9.5 Verdicts

A validator MUST attribute each finding to exactly one subject: the packet, its core registry, or
one extension registry its manifest binds.

- A `registry_binding` finding, and each outcome of a bound registry's
  [Part 02 §9.4](02_core_logical_schemas.md#ew-registry-conformance) result, belongs to the registry
  it concerns.
- A finding produced by a generated check or a declared rule belongs to the registry whose
  declaration generated or declares it.
- Any other finding about one resource entry, or the resource it locates, belongs to the registry
  whose declaration the entry's `declaration_id` names. For a reference, dependency, or lineage
  finding, that entry is the one holding the reference, the `requires` declaration, or the
  `derived_from` member, and for `packet.resource.required_missing` it is the absent singleton's
  declaration.
- Every other finding belongs to the packet, including every finding of the `json`, `shape`, and
  `entries` phases, `packet.resource.declaration_unknown`, and `packet.resource.lineage_cycle`.

A validator reports a **verdict** for the packet, for its core registry, and for each bound
extension registry. A verdict is `passed`, `failed`, or `incomplete`. A subject's verdict is:

1. `failed` where an error-level finding belongs to the subject, or its bound registry's Part 02
   result is `invalid`;
2. otherwise `incomplete` where an evaluation the subject requires did not complete or was withheld,
   or its bound registry's result is `unsupported` or `incomplete`; and
3. otherwise `passed`.

The packet verdict is the worst, in the order `failed`, `incomplete`, `passed`, of the verdict its
own findings give the packet, core's verdict, and every bound extension's verdict. Only a packet
verdict of `passed` supports the packet conformance claim of [§10](#ew-conformance-classes).

Where [Part 04 §10](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-manifest-conformance) or
[§14.5](#ew-validator-registry-boundary) withholds an evaluation, the subject it was withheld from is
at best `incomplete`. A finding in the `json`, `shape`, or `entries` phase withholds every registry's
evaluation; a core registry that fails binding or whose result is not `valid` withholds every
extension's packet checks; and an extension registry that fails binding withholds its own.

A validator's report MUST state the packet verdict, core's verdict, and each bound extension's
verdict, and MUST state each finding's subject. This specification defines no validation-report
schema.

<a id="ew-conformance-classes" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 10. Two conformance classes

A report says which of two claims it is making.

A packet conforms to this specification when it declares a profile defined here, carries every
resource that profile requires, satisfies every structural requirement of Part 02, completes every
required evaluation, and produces no error-level finding from the checks of this part. An invalid,
unsupported, or incomplete required evaluation cannot support a conformance claim. A conforming
packet MAY omit any optional instance, but every packet resource the manifest lists MUST resolve a logical
declaration. A payload that Escapewheel treats as opaque still requires a declaration; an extension
registry is the data-driven route for a resource core does not define.
[§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition) defines how the packet, core, and extension verdicts compose.

**Packet conformance** is a property of the bytes. It is evaluated on a packet regardless of how the packet was produced, and it is what `validate` reports. Provenance is SHOULD within it: a packet carrying none is conforming.

**Writer conformance** is a property of a process. A writer conforms when it satisfies this part, which includes recording provenance for the import activity and the inputs it read. A packet is not judged by it.

A validator MUST report packet conformance, and MUST NOT report a packet as non-conforming for an obligation that binds only a writer. Where it also checks writer obligations — a build-time run inside `import`, which has the process in hand — it MUST report the two separately, as it already does for core and extension verdicts.

<a id="ew-writer-acceptance-criteria" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 11. Minimum acceptance criteria

A writer conforms when it:

1. refuses a recipe that cannot produce a valid packet, before opening any source;
2. produces a packet that passes Tier 1, Tier 2, and the shim;
3. records provenance for what it read and what it ran;
4. leaves no manifest at the packet root when it refuses;
5. is **logically deterministic**: for the same recipe, the same source bytes and the same
   declared normalization, it produces the same canonical rows, the same values, the same assigned
   identifiers, the same resource identities and the same declared physical ordering.
   Identifier assignment MUST be a function of the recipe and the sources, never of wall-clock
   time, iteration order over an unordered collection, or the order a filesystem listed a
   directory.

Byte-identical output is **not** required and cannot be: a manifest carries `created_at` and
`validated_at`, and encoders may encode identical logical content differently between library
versions. A writer MAY offer a deterministic-build mode that pins those, and a packet
a validator MUST NOT judge a packet non-conforming for lacking one.

A recorded digest therefore says what a packet's bytes **are**, not what a second build would
produce. Two conforming builds of one dataset may record different digests for the same rows.

## 12. Alternatives are resolved before a commit

Where two or more `core.processing_runs` rows share `(session_id, activity_type, scope_kind, scope)`, the packet **MUST** carry a `core.preferred_runs` row naming one of them, and a writer **MUST** refuse to commit one that does not. Where an activity has more than one run in a session, every one of those runs **MUST** declare `scope` and `scope_kind` — without a scope the runs cannot be grouped, so an ambiguity cannot be found rather than not existing.

Both are checked at `error` level. A consumer MUST NOT silently choose; on a conforming packet that case is unreachable. A consumer that meets an unresolved alternative is holding a packet that failed validation and reports that. Nothing here forbids a packet holding two sortings of one probe or two curations of one sorting; what it requires is that the packet says which is current.

<a id="ew-reader-operations" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 13. Reader operations

Reader operations apply the logical identities and session scope defined by Part 02. They do not
widen a packet-local reference or infer identity from a label.

<a id="ew-reader-session-presentation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 13.1 Session presentation and correlation

A reader presenting one or more packets as one relation MUST carry a session discriminator on
every session-scoped table and MUST NOT carry one on a table that is not session-scoped. Part 02
§7.1 defines session scope.

The discriminator MUST distinguish the packet a row came from together with that packet's
`session_id`. A reader MUST NOT derive it from `session_name` or another `*_name`. A reader SHOULD
expose a compact integer for each such packet-and-session pair and SHOULD carry `session_name`
beside it as a non-identifying label.

A reader operation that correlates rows from two or more session-scoped relations MUST preserve
session scope. Within one packet, a correlation that does not follow a declared reference to a
packet-unique target MUST compare or match only rows with equal `session_id`. After presenting
packets together, a correlation MUST compare or match only rows with equal session discriminators.
If the caller supplies an explicit session restriction, the reader MUST apply it to every
participating relation and MUST NOT scope only one side of the operation. A declared packet-local
reference retains its exact key; the reader's discriminator supplies presentation context rather
than widening that reference.

Within one packet, a reader MUST join a unit-keyed relation to `core.units` through `unit_id`
alone. Across packets, it MUST include the session discriminator with `unit_id`; it MUST NOT widen
the packet-local unit reference itself. A reader SHOULD add the discriminator and label as scalar
projections over `session_id` rather than joining them from `core.sessions`, so an engine can prune
values a query does not select.

<a id="ew-reader-unit-selection" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 13.2 Exact unit selection and currency

A reader operation given an exact packet-assigned `unit_id` or an explicit table of units MUST
treat that input as the selected unit or units. It MUST NOT reject that selection solely because
the packet leaves a sorting or curation preference unresolved, and MUST NOT require
`sorting_run_id` or `curation_id` as redundant identity arguments.

An operation that offers to choose the packet's current unit set MUST continue to report an
unresolved alternative rather than silently select or concatenate competing results. Passing the
result of that guarded operation into an exact spike read remains guarded at the selection step.
Exact selection does not declare a non-preferred result current and does not make a packet that
violates §12 conforming.

Where an exact-unit reader operation accepts `session_id`, it MUST use it only as query and
time-conversion scope. It MUST apply the restriction to every selected spike row and to the clock,
origin, and attached observation support used for the result, and MUST NOT use it to disambiguate
`unit_id`. A selection that does not match the named session MUST NOT attach support from another
session.

<a id="ew-reader-declared-types" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 13.3 Declared identifier types

Where a reader's public API publishes a static type for a parameter, return value, or field that
directly carries a logical-table identifier, the reader MUST derive the corresponding host-language
scalar type from the incorporated registry's declared logical type. It MUST NOT infer the type from
an `_id` suffix or apply a logical-table identifier mapping to a catalog, manifest, vocabulary, or
discriminator-scope name governed by another declaration.

<a id="ew-registry-conformance-boundaries" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

## 14. Registry-document conformance boundaries

<a id="ew-checked-registry-context" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.1 Checked registry context

A **checked registry context** is an immutable in-process association of the exact evaluated
registry input; its decoded JSON value when decoding succeeded; its complete Part 02 result;
immutable representations of the local descriptor set and external-domain capabilities used; and,
for an extension, the exact checked core context supplied to its evaluation.

For a byte input, the context retains the exact bytes. For a mapping input, it retains an immutable
structural copy made before checking and records the skipped JSON phase required by Part 02 §9.4.
A valid context authorizes production or registry-derived checks only from the decoded document in
that same context. Another state authorizes no such interpretation. A context may be reused only
while all its associated elements remain identical. A path, declared version, mutable object
identity, or digest under an unspecified canonicalization does not prove that identity. This
specification defines neither a portable result cache nor a transferable validation receipt.

<a id="ew-registry-conformance-boundary" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.2 Required boundaries

An implementation MUST obtain one complete Part 02 result before it interprets a registry to:

- publish or release that registry;
- start a packet build, before checking a recipe, opening a source, or staging output;
- construct a public writer directly from registry input;
- validate a packet using an embedded or caller-supplied registry;
- perform an extension-author registry check; or
- generate Tier 1, Tier 2, or another conformance check from registry declarations.

A boundary that owns source bytes uses the byte entry point. A boundary given only a decoded
mapping uses the mapping entry point and does not claim byte-level JSON checks. A low-level
generator either establishes the checked context or requires one; a mapping accompanied by a
result, Boolean, or caller assertion is insufficient.

<a id="ew-reader-registry-independence" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.3 Reader independence

A reader MUST NOT require a registry result, checked context, or validation receipt to open a
packet, access its manifest or raw resources, present or correlate sessions, resolve
discriminators, references, or session scope, provide extension-aware access, or analyze one or
more packets. It may interpret declarations for those operations without making a conformance
claim, and the reference reader does not depend on the validator package to do so. An explicit
validation operation may evaluate the same input separately.

An unusable declaration need not produce a result for a reader operation. Its local failure or
partial-access behavior is not defined here, but ordinary analysis MUST NOT acquire a general
validation-receipt prerequisite.

<a id="ew-registry-producer-boundary" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.4 Producer and authoring reactions

Registry publication, release, and generation of registry-derived conformance checks complete only
from a `valid` context. An extension-author check reports success only for `valid`. For another
state, the operation stops and exposes the complete result.

A writer rejects a non-valid registry before opening a source or staging output. A public mapping
constructor copies and checks the mapping before retaining or interpreting it. Rejection does not
open an adapter. A structured rejection exception makes the complete portable result retrievable;
a command-line boundary offers machine-readable output containing the same result. Authoring may
create an explicitly unfinished draft when it makes no conformance or publication claim.

<a id="ew-validator-registry-boundary" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.5 Validator reactions

A packet validator obtains a complete result for every bound registry whose decoded bytes match
their digest under [Part 04 §10](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-manifest-conformance). For a
bound registry that cannot be acquired, `packet.registry.missing`, `packet.registry.unreadable`,
or `packet.registry.digest` is the packet-level outcome. The validator identifies the checked core
context directing packet checks and supplies that exact context to every extension evaluation. If
it is non-valid, each acquired extension is still evaluated as far as Part 02 permits.

A validator MUST direct packet checks only from the checked contexts of bound registries that
passed the `registry_binding` phase. It MUST NOT substitute a caller-supplied, bundled, or remotely
resolved registry for a bound one. A validator MAY check a caller-supplied registry document on its
own under Part 02 but MUST NOT use it to direct packet checks or report its result as the
packet's.

Only a `valid` context directs generation or execution of registry-derived packet checks. For a
non-valid result, the validator retains the complete result, skips every dependent packet check,
preserves invalid outcomes as registry-document findings, and does not fabricate packet findings
for unsupported, incomplete, or skipped work. Independent checks may continue.
[§5.4](#ew-validation-extensions) extends the packet checks to extension resources, and
[§9.5](#ew-verdict-composition) composes the verdicts. A non-valid required evaluation remains
insufficient to support a conformance claim.

<a id="ew-registry-result-reporting" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>

### 14.6 Portable reporting

Each reported result has an external subject label. A bound registry's label is its registry ID,
and a registry checked outside a packet receives a source-distinguishing label. A label does not replace or manufacture the document's own identity.

Public checking APIs, machine-readable command output, validation reports, and structured
exceptions expose the same Part 02 §9.5 representation rather than reducing it to a Boolean or
invalid-finding list. Human rendering may differ. Diagnostic `detail` is not interoperable
identity. The result schema begins at version 0.1.0; this boundary and reporting change does not by
itself change a registry meta-schema, core registry, extension registry, packet manifest, or their
version identifiers.

# Part 06 — Analysis Layer

Informative, except §2 and §5, which are normative.

<a id="ew-analysis-scope" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Scope

This part describes resources whose Part 02 declaration has content class `analysis`: derived
products computed from canonical core or extension resources and kept because a query cannot
answer the same question at acceptable cost.

<a id="ew-analysis-rules" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Rules

Normative.

An analysis resource MUST satisfy Part 02 §2.7's required lineage policy. Its
lineage may name the canonical core or extension resources it consumed or an intermediate analysis
resource; the acyclic chain MUST terminate in canonical resources.

An analysis resource is disposable. A writer MAY delete and recompute any of them without loss.

A writer SHOULD materialize an analysis resource only where doing so materially reduces repeated computational or I/O cost, gives an access layout core cannot, preserves the result of an expensive transformation, or provides a stable interface to an explicitly parameterised derivation. A cheap filter, projection, rename or small join SHOULD remain a view.

Where the justification is an expensive transformation or a parameterised derivation and a writer records the materialization's run, §5 requires that run to carry the transformation's parameters and not merely that it ran. A spectrogram whose window and overlap are unrecorded is not reproducible, and an irreproducible derived asset fails the disposability test below.

An analysis resource MUST NOT be the only place a fact exists. Anything a consumer needs that is
not recomputable from the canonical resources belongs among them; which canonical layer
it belongs to is decided by Part 09 §3's test, not by this rule.

## 3. A derived product earns its place by layout

Arithmetic does not earn it. Where the computation is cheap against data already held, a view is the correct answer and a materialized copy is a maintenance liability.

Measured on a real session, the arithmetic of aligning spikes to an event and a window costs 0.13 to 1.72 s against trains already in memory, while a materialized copy of four alignments comes to 100.6% of the spike table itself. A wide trial table is 708 rows in 7.5 ms.

The test is therefore: does materializing change the *shape* of access, or only pre-compute a cheap step?

<a id="ew-analysis-spike-events" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. `analysis.spike_events`

Core keeps each spike on the clock of the probe that measured it, sorted unit-major. That layout answers "this unit's spikes" well and "every unit's spikes in this window" not at all, because a window across many clocks means nothing.

`analysis.spike_events` is core's spikes mapped onto the reference clock and bucketed by time, sorted by unit within each bucket, so that a time filter and a unit filter both prune.

<!-- columns: analysis.spike_events -->
| column | type | presence | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `session_id` | `int32` | required | Session ID. |
| `unit_id` | `int32` | required | Unit ID. |
| `t_tick` | `int64` | required | Spike tick on clock_id, mapped through core.clock_anchors. |
| `clock_id` | `int32` | required | The clock these ticks are on, normally the session's reference. |
| `time_bucket_tick` | `int64` | required | First tick of the bucket this spike falls in, on clock_id. |
<!-- /columns -->

A row group lies inside one time bucket, so a window filter prunes at the row-group level. The bucket width cannot be recovered from the data, so it is a parameter of a recorded materialization run (§5).

**Rows are not preserved.** A spike whose tick falls outside the region where the mapping is defined MUST be omitted by the writer rather than given an extrapolated value, so this table MAY hold fewer rows than core's. A writer that records the materialization's run MUST record the count omitted among its parameters, even when it is zero: a derived table one row short otherwise looks exactly like a complete one.

**A conversion that collides is not materialized.** This table is keyed on the converted tick, and a conversion preserves distinctness only where it expands. Where a writer finds two spikes of one unit landing on one reference tick, it MUST NOT materialize the table, and where it records the run, it MUST record the reason among the run's parameters as it does for omitted rows. A histogram built on collapsed spikes undercounts them, so a reference clock too coarse to separate one unit's spikes makes this table the wrong instrument rather than a broken one, and §2 makes not building it an ordinary outcome.

Measured on one session, a ten-second population window costs 0.025 s here against 7.7 s over a ragged per-unit representation.

<a id="ew-analysis-materialization-provenance" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 5. Provenance of a materialization

Normative.

A writer MAY record a `core.processing_runs` row for a materialization and need not. Where it
records one, the row's parameters MUST carry every transform parameter the output cannot recover.
A singleton analysis catalog table and each analysis instance it catalogs are distinct packet
resources, each recording its own declaration-selected lineage.

## 6. Withdrawn resources

Four resources proposed in v0.1 are withdrawn. They were specified before anything measured them:

| resource | disposition |
|---|---|
| `analysis.spikes_by_trial` | withdrawn; a table per (event, window) pair, and the alignment is cheap |
| `analysis.trials_wide` | withdrawn; 708 rows in 7.5 ms as a view |
| `analysis.unit_condition_rates` | withdrawn; its columns are a task's shape, not a format's |
| `analysis.good_units` | withdrawn; a filter, not a table |

The columns those tables carried — `condition_id`, `stimulus_id`, `block_id` — describe a particular task rather than the format, and belong in an extension (Part 09).

<a id="ew-analysis-views-and-materializations" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. Views versus materialized products

Prefer a view where the query prunes and the result is small. Prefer a materialized product where
the physical layout of core cannot serve the access pattern at all, which in practice means a
different sort order or a different clock. View and materialization are realizations of a logical
kind, not kinds themselves.

<a id="ew-analysis-promotion" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 8. Promotion into core

Promotion changes the resource declaration's content class through an explicit revision of this
specification or its owning extension registry. It is a schema decision, not an operational
Boolean or a validation-state transition.

# Part 07 — Reference Implementation and Plan

Informative.

## 1. Scope

This part describes the reference implementation's shape and the phase plan. Nothing here constrains a conforming implementation.

## 2. Distribution layout

The reference implementation is three distributions with one command-line entry point.

| distribution | contents | depends on |
|---|---|---|
| `escapewheel-reader` | manifest and resource resolution, tabular access | — |
| `escapewheel-validator` | the checks of Part 05 | reader |
| `escapewheel-writer` | source adapters, the recipe, the packet writer | validator |

The split is not cosmetic. The validator's correctness is pinned to a registry version while the reader's API is not, and the writer depends on the validator because commit is validate-then-rename (05 §6).

### 2.1 One command, three providers

`escapewheel-reader` owns the `escapewheel` console script. The other two register subcommands through an entry-point group. Two distributions declaring the same script name is a conflict in which the last installed wins silently.

### 2.2 Dependency discipline

Validation requires an embedded SQL engine, because the rules are SQL. Reading does not: a complete read path goes through the columnar library alone, so the SQL engine is an optional extra.

This is what Part 02 §5.2's ban on nested types exists to preserve — a native reader stays viable in a language without an embedded SQL engine.

## 3. Command line

```text
escapewheel inspect <kind> <root>      what one source offers (05 §4.1)
escapewheel init --source k=path ...   draft a recipe, flagging contested resources
escapewheel import <recipe> --check    every problem, before any I/O (05 §3.5)
escapewheel import <recipe> --strict   refuse anything an adapter worked around
escapewheel import <recipe>            steps 2 to 8 (05 §2)
escapewheel verify <packet>            record what is true of external resources
escapewheel prune <packet>             remove what the packet no longer names (03 §9.3)
```

<a id="ew-reference-object-model" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. Object model

| type | responsibility |
|---|---|
| `Packet` | resolve the manifest; hand back resources |
| `Dataset` | present several packets as one relation per resource |
| `TableResource` | a table, as columnar batches or a scan |
| `ArrayResource` | an array, opened through the array library |
| `Validator` | run the generated and declared checks; return a report |
| `PacketWriter` | accumulate records; stage, validate, commit |

An adapter returns its rows as records, not a path, and they stay records whichever route delivers them. That is what lets an adapter be tested without a filesystem and stops normalization decisions leaking into I/O code. Out of process the transport carries a table and the receiving end returns records again, so nothing downstream of the boundary knows which route was taken. A packet-owned array crossing a protocol and a report are written where the build points (Part 05 §2).

An adapter carries six static declarations, all readable with nothing open: `PRODUCES`, the resource identifiers it can supply (Part 05 §3.4); `CONTRIBUTES`, those of them whose rows it supplies only in part, such as the clocks of its own streams (Part 05 §3.4); `STATES`, the recipe fields it can state, as `block.field` names such as `subject.subject_id` (Part 05 §3.4); `NEEDS`, the values a build must supply it, drawn from Part 03 §11.2's terms and the recipe's options; `ACCEPTS`, the options it reads and can run without, which a recipe may leave unset (Part 03 §11.2); and `CONSUMES`, the kinds whose output it reads, which is what orders a build. For each name in `STATES` the adapter exposes the value it holds. All six are class attributes here, and Part 03 §11.5's document is the same facts for an adapter a build cannot import; the requirements in Part 05 and Part 03 are on what a build can determine, not on how an implementation stores it.

An adapter released independently of the writer declares its protocols by naming constants the implementation exports rather than strings it spells, so an adapter resolves the versions it was written against and each speaks what it names. An adapter the writer ships declares none, and the writer runs it in process or serves it over any protocol of the group (Part 03 §11.1).

Converting records to a table takes the column set from every row and not from the first. `pyarrow.Table.from_pylist` infers its schema from the leading row and silently drops what later rows carry, which loses a column a source populates for some rows only — measured, one of thirteen resources of the NWB adapter. The rule it implements is Part 03 §11.3's, stated about what a delivery says rather than about how a table is built, because an adapter declaring its schema up front meets it by construction.

An adapter that produces a dense array across a protocol writes it to the location the build supplied and returns the reference (Part 03 §11.3). An adapter the writer runs in process may instead hand the array's values over, and the writer writes them to that location.

A build reports a `metadata` key whose value equals a column of the same row, which is how Part 02 §8.6's prohibition on restating a column reaches whoever wrote the bag. No rule can do it: the content is schemaless, and an adapter names a key in the source's vocabulary where core names a column in its own. The comparison is the same duplicate-value comparison used between two columns, applied to a key and a column; three keys in the packaged sessions restated a column.

Run it after changing the registry, the generator, or any rule.

<a id="ew-reference-performance-notes" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. Performance notes

These are properties of the reference implementation, not requirements. A number that would change if this implementation were replaced belongs here; one that follows from Parquet, Zarr or an object store belongs to Part 10, and this section does not repeat it.

- **Never convert a column to host objects inside a per-batch loop.** Every performance defect found in the reference writer has been this.
- **Read a large table in batches.** A 10⁸-row spike table decodes to many gigabytes if read whole.
- **Bound memory explicitly** where a job's working set is data-dependent, and give the SQL engine a spill directory: a memory limit without one bounds nothing.
- **Do not measure every column's encoding at write time.** Writing each column three ways over a
  65,536-row sample and keeping the smallest costs about **0.4 s** for a whole packet against a
  467 s build, so cost is not what refuses it: a measured choice records no reason for itself, and
  it differs between a `--sample` build and a full one. Counting each column instead, as
  Part 03 §5.5's rules do, costs 0.12 s to 0.20 s a packet and states its reason in the count
 .
- **Project a constant rather than joining for it.** The reference reader adds session identity to a
  view as a scalar expression over `session_id`. An engine can prune a projection a query does not
  select; it generally cannot prune a join whose output might filter or duplicate rows.

# Part 09 — Metadata Minimalism and Extensions

Normative.

## 1. Scope

This part defines how much metadata core requires, where richer metadata belongs, and how an extension declares it.

## 2. Tiers

| tier | contents | location |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | packet-level facts | the manifest |
| 2 | facts needed to interpret, validate, join, or reproduce | core tables |
| 3 | domain-rich, lab-specific facts | an extension's tables |
| 4 | human-readable context | `docs/` sidecars |
| 3-4 | such facts stated about one row, in the source's own vocabulary | that table's `metadata` column |

## 3. Core inclusion rule

A field belongs in core only if common interpretation, validation, joining, or analysis would break without it.

A field SHOULD be in core when it is needed to interpret units, clocks, channels, arrays, or spike times; to validate a required relationship; to join tables; to filter or group a common analysis; to reproduce processing or curation; or to trace provenance.

A field SHOULD be in an extension or a sidecar when it is institutional, administrative, lab-specific, rarely queried, free-form, procedural but not required for analysis, or biologically rich but not required to interpret the recording.

## 4. Required minimum

A conforming packet requires only what is needed to identify, interpret, validate, query, and reproduce. The registry states the minimum per resource; this part does not restate it.

An import MUST NOT fail because optional extension metadata is absent.

## 5. Extensions

<a id="ew-extension-definition" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.1 What an extension is

An extension is a registry document in the extension class defined by Part 02 §9.1. Its
`extension_id` is its namespace, and its full `logical_standard_version` selects the core context
against which core-dependent invariants run. It declares logical resources of any Part 02 kind,
including table columns, references, and rules where the kind is `table`, on the same applicable
terms as core.

An extension needs no code. Its registry document supplies the logical contract; physical-profile
support determines how a particular payload is read or written. Neither imports anything of the
reference implementation.

<a id="ew-extension-namespacing" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.2 Namespacing

Every declaration identifier in an extension registry MUST begin with the namespace equal to its
`extension_id`. The extension owns only that namespace. An extension namespace MUST NOT be `core`,
`analysis`, or `nwb`.

<a id="ew-extension-registration" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.3 Registration

A packet carrying extension resources MUST bind each extension registry that declares them with a
registry descriptor whose role is `extension`, under
[Part 04 §8](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-registry-bindings).
[Part 04 §10](04_packet_catalog.md#ew-packet-manifest-conformance) owns the checks of that
binding. A validator MUST NOT require an extension registry in order to validate core.

<a id="ew-extension-verdicts" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.4 Separate verdicts

An extension is validated on core's terms under
[Part 05 §5.4](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-validation-extensions), and its verdict and core's are
composed under [Part 05 §9.5](05_validation_and_import.md#ew-verdict-composition), so an extension's
findings never change core's verdict and core's findings never change an extension's. A consumer
MAY read core from a packet whose extension fails, and MUST NOT read that packet's verdict as though
the failure were external to it.

<a id="ew-extension-versioning" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.5 Independent versioning

An extension's `registry_version` is that extension registry's own version. It advances
independently of the core registry's `registry_version`; the core specification neither selects
nor assigns it.

<a id="ew-code-free-extension-handling" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 5.6 A writer requires no code of an extension

A writer MUST be able to interpret every extension logical declaration from the registry document
without extension plugin code. It MUST be able to build extension tables given that document and
their rows as files. This requirement does not grant generic physical support for every possible
opaque payload or adapter protocol; those capabilities remain profile and adapter concerns. A
writer MUST NOT require the extension itself to be installed, importable, or resolvable in the
writer's environment.

A writer MAY accept an extension that ships code — an adapter registered under an entry-point group, or a registry document loaded from an installed distribution — and MUST NOT treat either as the ordinary case. That is what turns §5.1's "needs no code" from a possibility into a promise.

### 5.7 How an extension's document and rows reach a build

An extension delivers a registry document in the format of Part 02 and, per table it declares, rows. Parquet written under the `delivery_root` a build supplies (`files-v0.1`) is one way and remains the route every implementation supports; an extension MAY deliver over any protocol of the group its profile names (Part 03 §11.1), since what the requirement rests on is a dependency closure that must not become the writer's and the process boundary is what answers that. Where a raw source needs converting first, a recipe names a command: an argument list carried as a list and run in the source's own root, never a shell line (Part 05 §3.7), so an extension isolates its dependencies however it likes and a build states no opinion about how.

An extension's registry document and Part 03 §11.5's declaration document are different things for different readers, and neither substitutes for the other: the first declares logical resources, while the second declares what the adapter carrying them produces, states, needs, and speaks.

Rows arrive carrying identifiers as the names their source used, and a writer numbers them, so an extension neither knows nor needs the packet's numbering.

How the registry document is produced is the extension's business, but an extension MUST NOT derive it from the rows it delivers for the packet being built. A declaration derived from one packet's data cannot disagree with that data, so it declares nothing, and two packets whose extensions each derived one no longer share a schema. Part 02 §5's three presence states are what carry the difference between a column this run did not compute and one the extension does not have.

An extension's command MUST produce the same rows from the same inputs, and MUST NOT let wall-clock time, an unfixed random seed, or iteration over an unordered collection reach a value it writes. Which program the command resolves to is the recipe author's: pinning library versions so two runs agree byte for byte is a build property, and a validator MUST NOT judge a packet non-conforming for lacking one. An extension that cannot be a function of its inputs delivers a file produced beforehand rather than a command the build runs.

### 5.8 What an extension may state about its own run

An extension MAY validate its own tables before returning them, against its own registry document and on the same terms a validator applies to core. A build MUST NOT rely on its having done so; its own validation runs over every staged resource whatever produced it.

An extension MAY write a report of its run at a path the recipe names, and a build MUST report what one contains where it is present. A build MUST NOT require one, and MUST NOT fail on a report's contents alone.

Where the report is a file, a build MUST remove that path before it runs the command and MUST treat a report it did not see appear as absent. A file at a path the recipe names is present after any run that ever wrote one, so last week's findings are otherwise reported as this run's with nothing to distinguish them.

Which channel carries a report is the protocol's. Under `files-v0.1` and `stream-v0.1` it is a file at the path the recipe names, supplied to the adapter as `report_path` (Part 03 §11.2), because rows have taken standard output and a file is the channel left. Under `flight-v0.1` it is `DoAction("report")`, because the connection is that channel and no file outlives the run; an adapter with a report MUST name `report` among its actions, and a build MUST NOT ask one that did not.

A report carries what a build cannot derive, and a build SHOULD ignore what it can: rows per table, which tables exist, and whether their schemas conform are each established without asking. What no artifact holds is what the command read, what it skipped and why, and whether a table that is short is legitimately short.

A report is JSON. An optional `problems` array carries an entry per finding, each with a `level` of `error`, `warning` or `info`, a `message`, and optionally the `resource` the finding concerns; an optional `read` array names what the command consumed; an optional `stated` object carries, per `block.field` name the source declares it states, the value the source holds (Part 03 §11.5). A build MUST report every problem it is given and MUST NOT refuse a report for carrying keys it does not recognise.

A build MUST look for a report after a failure as it does after a success, since the run that went wrong is the one with something to say.

## 6. Direction of reference

<a id="ew-core-extension-reference-direction" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.1 Core does not reference an extension

A core registry reference or declaration dependency MUST target the core registry. An extension
registry reference, dependency, or catalog binding MUST target either its selected core context or
the same extension registry. A core table MUST NOT declare a foreign key into an extension table,
and an extension MUST NOT reference another extension.

The reasons are separable verdicts (§5.4) and independent versioning (§5.5). "Interpretable when no extension is present" is *not* among them: a packet embeds its extension registries, so they are present whenever the resources are.

<a id="ew-core-extension-discrimination" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
### 6.2 Core may name an extension

A core table MAY name an extension resource through a `discriminator` column (Part 02 §8.3). A
consumer resolves a value by reading the named registry resource, taking its primary key, and
applying the declared discriminator shape.

**A discriminator names without depending.** It creates neither a shared verdict nor a version coupling, which is why it is the one mechanism core is allowed.

## 7. Promotion

A field moves from an extension into core through a revision of this specification, not by a writer deciding to emit it. Promotion is justified by §3's rule and by evidence that more than one producer needs it.

## 8. Anti-patterns

A reader MUST NOT make an extension's schema required in order to open core tables or arrays.

A writer MUST NOT:

- hide analysis-critical metadata inside free-form JSON;
- use an object path as the only way to identify a resource;
- treat the manifest as a store for all metadata.

A build MUST NOT block an import because optional institutional metadata is incomplete.

### 8.1 Untyped accretion

Core MUST NOT contain a table whose purpose is to hold arbitrary name-value pairs.

Nothing constrains the name, type, or meaning of what accretes in such a table, so two packets cannot be compared and neither can be checked. `core.trial_tags` was the one place this format permitted it and is withdrawn.

An extension needing a multi-valued attribute declares its own typed child table, as Part 02 §5.2 requires of everything else.

# Part 10 — Performance Notes

Informative. Part version **0.1.0**.

## 1. Scope

This part carries what a packet's bytes make true under `escapewheel-portable-v0.1`: what an
encoding costs, what a row group spans, what an object store answers. A number here supports a
rule that lives elsewhere and states none of its own; where a measurement justifies a requirement,
the requirement stays in the part that binds and this part carries the reading.

It is not a substitute for `conformance/`, which holds the probes that produced these numbers.
This part says what they mean.

**Every measurement below records its date, and none records the library versions it was taken
against except where noted.** Those were not kept. A number whose toolchain is unknown has unknown
currency; a number silently missing the field says nothing at all.

## 2. Encoding and compression

### 2.1 A tick column is delta-encoded, not dictionary-encoded

`core.media_frames.pts` over 7,570,450 frames, the same rows in the same row groups (2026-09-04; toolchain not kept):

| `pts` as | encoding | `pts` | whole table |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `float64` | `RLE_DICTIONARY` | 11.371 MB | 11.73 MB |
| `float64` | `BYTE_STREAM_SPLIT` | 0.273 MB | 0.66 MB |
| `int64` | `DELTA_BINARY_PACKED` | 0.043 MB | 0.43 MB |

Dictionary encoding is what a column declaring nothing else receives, and a monotonic ramp of 7.5 M
distinct values is its worst case: `pts` is **96.9%** of the table as written. `DELTA_BINARY_PACKED`
is defined for `INT32` and `INT64` alone, which is what Part 03 §5.5's rule for an ordered integer
turns on.

### 2.2 An integer identifier costs nothing to store and much to read

Over 287,896,563 spikes, the identifier columns are **8.1 MB, 1% of the table**, because a sorted
low-cardinality column compresses away; the integer form is **1.1% smaller** than the string one.
Storage is therefore not what decides the type — the read is, at **10.2x** on a join to
`core.units` (2026-09-02; toolchain not kept).

### 2.3 A repeated key column is free under a clustered sort

Four-column unit identity against one numbered `unit_id`, over the same spike table: **819 MB
either way** (2026-09-05; toolchain not kept). `core.media_frames` shows the same shape at
7,570,450 rows, where one distinct `session_id` costs **1.0005x**, 0.013 bits a row (2026-09-07; toolchain not kept).

### 2.4 An ascending identifier costs 30.95 bits a row under a dictionary

The first column of a table's sort order whose role is `identifier` ascends across the whole table,
and a dictionary over it stores the values once and an index into them again. Column-chunk bytes on
`ecephys_776259` (2026-09-10; pyarrow 25.0.1, Python 3.13.15):

| column | dictionary | delta | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `aind_unit_qc.synchrony.unit_id` | 48.6 kB | 0.6 kB | **79x** |
| `core.unit_coordinate_values.unit_id` | 46.8 kB | 0.6 kB | **75x** |
| `core.units.unit_id` | 26.3 kB | 0.5 kB | 57x |
| `core.unit_coordinates.unit_id` | 25.3 kB | 0.5 kB | 53x |
| `core.electrode_coordinates.electrode_id` | 24.3 kB | 0.5 kB | 48x |
| `core.electrodes.electrode_id` | 17.5 kB | 0.5 kB | 37x |

`core.unit_coordinates.unit_id` costs **30.95 bits a row** for an `int32`, which is almost the 32
bits the raw values take.

### 2.5 Four float columns in five do not repeat enough to index

Of the **32 float columns** reaching 2 kB under pyarrow's default across two packaged sessions,
**27 are smaller written plain**, by 1.15x to 1.76x. The five that repeat decide more than the
twenty-seven: `core.spike_events.amplitude` is quantised to the sorting run's amplitude step, so
286,896,563 rows hold few distinct values and the dictionary is **1.85x** better across **209 MB**
(2026-09-10; toolchain as above).

### 2.6 A packet's small tables are what an open transfers

Two spike tables are **1,168 MB** of `ecephys_776259` and are read behind a filter; the other 38
tables come to **1.375 MB** and are read entire. Those 38 are **2.622 MB** under pyarrow's default,
so the encoding rules take **1.91x** off what a consumer transfers before a filter can prune
anything — 1.80x on `ecephys_786867` over its 37 comparable tables, and **23.94x** with
`core.media_frames` counted, where 7,570,450 rows go from 30.949 MB to 0.429 MB.

Decoding gives none of it back: over the same 38 tables, warm and interleaved, **15.6 ms against
15.8 ms**, and 28.8 ms against 57.7 ms where `core.media_frames` is among them (2026-09-10; toolchain as above). Round trips are the one cost this leaves alone — a column chunk is
one ranged read at either size, so what an encoding changes is what each request carries (§4.1).

### 2.7 A rule read off the declaration picks worse on 82 columns of 116

What delta exploits is small successive differences and what a dictionary exploits is repetition,
and the registry declares neither. Deriving the choice from the sort key — delta for a key column,
a dictionary for everything else — was measured over the 116 column-and-packet pairs reaching 2 kB
under either choice and picked worse on **82**: `core.media_frames.pts` **188x worse** and
`core.clock_anchors.t_tick` 57x, both climbing steadily without being key columns, and
`core.unit_electrodes.electrode_id` 1.84x, a trailing key component that climbs within each unit
and resets (2026-09-10; toolchain as above).

A declaration says what a value means. It does not say how the values are spread, and a storage
choice turns on the second.

### 2.8 A dictionary page costs 27 bytes in every row group

A column holding one value gives a dictionary nothing to index that plain's compression does not
already remove. A constant `int32` under Zstandard (2026-09-15; pyarrow 25.0.1, Python
3.13.15):

| rows | dictionary | plain |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 | 92 B | 69 B |
| 6,781 | 95 B | 75 B |
| 171,280 | 666 B | 718 B |
| 1,000,000 | 3,577 B | 4,000 B |

The page is 27 bytes in each row group and outweighs the column below some tens of thousands of
rows; above them the dictionary is smaller by a steady 1.12x. The committed packets put the crossing
between `core.clock_anchors.session_id` at 41,275 rows, where the two are within 3 bytes, and at
76,646 rows, where the dictionary is smaller. Below 50,000 rows delta suits a single-valued integer
better than plain: it is never more than 6 bytes larger in either packet, and plain is up to 5.9x
larger, `core.array_channels.axis_index` being 546 bytes plain and 93 under delta.

### 2.9 Declarations mark the integers that climb within groups

A count over a whole column sees a climb only where the table is sorted by that column.
`core.spike_events.t_tick` climbs within each unit and `core.clock_anchors.anchor_index` within each
clock, so choosing by counts alone writes both plain: `anchor_index` 28x to 67x off its best,
`core.clock_anchors.t_tick` 30x to 32x, and the spike tables' `t_tick` 1.24x to 1.30x, 384 MB over
the best across both packets before any exception (2026-09-15; toolchain as above). The
`_tick` suffix, the `structural_index` and `sequence_position` roles and `assigned` identity mark
such columns. None states a distribution, so §2.7 stands, and a mark is wrong in places:
`core.unit_electrodes.channel_index` is a structural index cycling through 26 positions in every
unit.

### 2.10 A count sees repetition that a declaration cannot

Rules that each follow from a declaration leave 70 columns 1.15x or more off their best across the
two packets: a `free` string plain, an `open` or `closed` one a dictionary, a `data` integer plain,
and `session_id` plain. No declaration says how many rows share a value, and `session_id` wants
plain in a 12-row table and a dictionary in a million-row group. `core.spike_events.amplitude` and
`core.unit_quality_labels.confidence` are both floats: the first repeats and is 1.75x smaller under a
dictionary, and the second does not and is 1.47x larger (2026-09-15; toolchain as above).

### 2.11 The four rules leave 15 columns needing an exception

Columns 1.15x or more off their best in either packet (2026-09-15; toolchain as above):

| rules | columns | at 1.5x | at 2x |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| sort-key heuristic, with its exceptions | 193 | 61 | 4 |
| declarations of type, role, identity and vocabulary | 70 | 20 | 4 |
| those, plus plain under 32 rows, or for one value under 50,000 rows | 34 | 12 | 4 |
| counts alone | 26 | 11 | 9 |
| Part 03 §5.5's, declared order then counts | 15 | 3 | 1 |

The count holds from 50 to 200 rows a run, from 32 to 50 rows a value, and at every one-value
threshold from 20,000 rows up, where bytes choose 50,000: at 500,000 the rules sit 913 kB over the
best against 304 kB. Thresholds fitted to either packet alone leave 13 on the other. Under the rules
and their exceptions every column of both packets lands within 1.15x of its best, 317 of 365 and 322
of 372 exactly at it, and 157 kB and 71 kB over the best in all. That 0.014% and 0.012% of each
packet is what the rules give up against writing each column three ways and keeping the smallest.

### 2.12 A sampled build encodes its spike tables for the rows it holds

`--sample 2000` keeps the first 2,000 spikes of each probe's stream, shrinking the two spike tables
and changing the values of `core.units`, and each packet then encodes 8 columns differently from its
full build (2026-09-15; toolchain as above). Seven take the encoding their sampled values
call for, within 1.04x of the best. `core.spike_events.amplitude` does not: each stream is one
2,000-row row group holding 97 and 128 distinct amplitudes, under 50 rows a value, so it is written
plain at 1.56x and 1.60x off its best. Every other column of both sampled packets lies within 1.15x
of its best, so a sampled packet is encoded for the rows it holds and does not preview the full
build's layout in the tables a sample shrinks.

## 3. Row groups

A large table's row groups are sized for analytical scans (Part 03 §5.1). **100,000 to 1,000,000
rows** is a starting point to be adjusted by profiling; it is a number this format fixes rather
than a rule a packet can fail, which is why Part 03 §5.1 keeps the SHOULD and the number is here.

Where a table declares a time bucket, a row group SHOULD start at each bucket boundary:
a group cut at a fixed row count straddles boundaries and spans two buckets' worth of time.

## 4. The object store

### 4.1 An endpoint plateaus at about 120 requests a second

Whatever the concurrency (2026-09-06; toolchain not kept). Twenty sessions of metadata
is therefore about a second, which is what makes a union across packets cheap enough that no
summary table is needed to avoid it.

### 4.2 One file beats a partitioned prefix on the engine that matters

The same rows staged to S3 four ways — a single file, a declared member list, the same members
reached by globbing, and hive layout — with the member arms at 682.2 MB against the single file's
681.6 MB (withdrawn 2026-09-02; toolchain not kept). DuckDB's single file wins every
query cold and warm. Where the member arms win, the gain reproduces at the same ratio on local
disk, so it is scan parallelism rather than skipped round trips.

The write side is the other way: a re-sorting rewrites the whole file where partitioning writes one
member. No session has yet had a second sorting.

## 5. The Zarr codec floor is version-specific

Measured in `conformance/zarr-probe/` against **zarr-python 3.4.0 with numcodecs 0.17.0, Zarr.jl
0.10.2 on Julia 1.12.7, TensorStore 0.1.85, zarrs-python 0.2.3 and zarrita 0.7.5 on Node 22.22.2**
(2026-09-24) — the one measurement here whose toolchain was kept, and the one that shows why the
others should be. Each reader checks the int64 element sum of arrays zarr-python and the reference
writer wrote; zarrs reads through a strict codec pipeline, so no read falls back to Python.

| stored v3 pipeline | zarr-python | Zarr.jl | TensorStore | zarrs | zarrita |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bytes`; `bytes`, `gzip`; `bytes`, `zstd` | reads | reads | reads | reads | reads |
| `bytes`, `blosc` (`zstd`), each of `noshuffle`, `shuffle`, `bitshuffle` | reads | reads | reads | reads | reads |
| `sharding_indexed` around each sequence of the first row and around `bytes`, `blosc` (`zstd`); index `bytes`, `crc32c`; at `end` | reads | reads | reads | reads | reads |
| `sharding_indexed` with index `bytes` alone, at `end` | reads | reads | reads | reads | reads |
| `sharding_indexed` with index at `start` | reads | reads | reads | reads | fails |
| `bytes`, `blosc` (`lz4`) | reads | reads | reads | reads | reads |
| the reference writer's `numcodecs.delta`, `bytes`, `blosc` (`zstd`) | reads | fails | fails | fails | reads |

All five also read Zarr v2 zstd and Blosc-Zstd. zarrita fails an index at `start` with

A reading of this kind stops being true the moment a library moves, with nothing in the prose
changing to show it. That is what Part 03 §6.4's per-version reader floor exists to state as a rule,
and what this table exists to explain.

# Part 11 — Tier 2 rule language

Normative.

<a id="ew-tier-2-language-selection" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 1. Language identity, authority, and selection

This part defines the portable language and execution semantics for registry-declared Tier 2
rules. It maps the logical resources and types of Part 02 into rule evaluation. Part 02 owns
registry selection and rule declarations; Part 05 owns validation and reporting workflow. This
part MUST NOT redefine either subject.

The language identified by `escapewheel-rule-sql-v0.1` is based on the SQL framework and
SQL/Foundation editions, corrigenda, and IEEE floating-point edition identified in §9. Those
authorities apply to the SQL and numerical requirements in §§2–7 only to the extent selected here.
The narrower Escapewheel rules in this part govern where the external standards permit choices or
additional features.

This part expressly incorporates `registry/rule_language.schema.json`, which defines the closed
shape of a rule-language profile, and
`registry/rule_languages/escapewheel-rule-sql-v0.1.json`, which assigns the exact external basis,
ISO feature dependencies, contract clauses, type bindings, and execution settings of this
language. The JSON artifacts MUST conform to the incorporated profile schema. Their fields carry
the meanings defined here; neither artifact makes a parser, abstract syntax tree, database engine,
or validator implementation authoritative.

A validator claiming support for this language MUST support the identified contract as a whole.
It MUST NOT construct a different language by independently toggling profile dependencies or
settings. A changed language contract MUST receive a new language ID. A registry may change its
rules without changing the language ID only when the registry version changes and the rules retain
this contract's meaning.

<a id="ew-tier-2-query-profile" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 2. Query profile

A rule MUST contain exactly one SQL/Foundation query expression and no second statement. The
profile admits:

- query specifications with expression select lists, `WHERE`, `SELECT DISTINCT`, column-reference
  `GROUP BY`, and `HAVING`;
- derived tables, one-column scalar subqueries, `EXISTS`, and correlated subqueries;
- one optional top-level `WITH` containing ordinary common table expressions or §6's bounded
  recursive form;
- explicit inner and left outer joins qualified by `ON` or `USING`;
- `UNION` and `UNION ALL`;
- searched `CASE`, aggregate `DISTINCT`, and aggregate `FILTER (WHERE ...)`;
- window partitioning and ordering for the routines below, using ascending column references; and
- `PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY expression)` with implicit ascending order.

A comma, cross, natural, right, or full join is outside the profile. So are nested `WITH`, a
top-level result `ORDER BY`, window frames, order ordinals, `DESC`, and explicit `NULLS FIRST` or
`NULLS LAST`. An unqualified `*` is admitted only in `COUNT(*)` or an intermediate query whose
expanded names are unique; a final result MUST NOT project `*`. A derived table MUST have an
explicit `AS` correlation name. ISO line comments beginning with `--` are admitted; bracketed
comments are not.

Every window invocation MUST have a non-empty `ORDER BY`. For `LAG` and `ROW_NUMBER`, a validator
MUST establish statically that the partition and ordering columns together identify one input row;
otherwise the rule is invalid. Ties in `DENSE_RANK` intentionally receive the same rank.

The admitted literals are an ISO character string, `NULL`, an unsigned base-10 integer, an
unsigned base-10 decimal with digits on both sides of the point, an approximate numeric literal
using `e` or `E` and an optionally signed decimal exponent, and
`INTERVAL '<unsigned-base-10-integer>' DAY`. Unary minus is a separate operator. An exact numeric
literal MUST have no more than 38 digits.

The admitted operators are unary minus; numeric `+`, `-`, `*`, and `/`; a timestamp left operand
plus or minus a day interval; `=`, `<>`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, and `>=`; `IS NULL`, `IS NOT NULL`, and
`IS DISTINCT FROM`; `BETWEEN`, `IN`, `LIKE`, `EXISTS`, and their negated forms; and `AND`, `OR`,
and `NOT`. `IN` takes a non-empty scalar list or a one-column subquery. Other engine spellings,
including `!=`, are outside the profile.

| class | admitted routines |
|---|---|
| scalar | `ABS` over one approximate numeric; `COALESCE` over two or more common-type arguments; `CAST` only to `DOUBLE PRECISION` or `DECIMAL(38,0)`; one-argument `isnan` and `isfinite` over an approximate numeric |
| aggregate | `COUNT(*)`, one-argument `COUNT`, `COUNT(DISTINCT expression)`, one-argument `MIN` and `MAX`, and the ordered-set `PERCENTILE_CONT` form above |
| window | one-argument `LAG`, zero-argument `ROW_NUMBER`, and zero-argument `DENSE_RANK` |

`ABS`, `MIN`, `MAX`, and `LAG` return their argument's domain. `COALESCE` returns its arguments'
common domain, and `CAST` returns its target domain. `COUNT`, `ROW_NUMBER`, and `DENSE_RANK`
return `BIGINT`; `PERCENTILE_CONT` returns `DOUBLE PRECISION`. Comparisons, predicates, Boolean
operators, `isnan`, and `isfinite` return `BOOLEAN`. An aggregate MUST NOT be used as a window
routine, and a window routine MUST carry `OVER`. SQL keywords and routine names are
case-insensitive; identifiers follow §3.

A validator MUST parse and statically check a rule before executing it. It MUST reject a second
statement, any surface outside this positive profile, and every DDL, DML, transaction, catalog,
configuration, attachment, import/export, extension, macro, filesystem, network, parameter,
volatile, or environment-dependent surface before execution. An engine feature does not enter the
language merely because the engine implements it.

<a id="ew-tier-2-relation-environment" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 3. Relation environment, names, and dependencies

A base relation MUST use its canonical two-part registry resource ID, such as `core.units` or
`dynamic_foraging.trials`, with the exact lowercase spelling in the registry. A validator MUST NOT
expose flattened names as part of this language or resolve alternate spelling by case folding.

A core rule may name only core and analysis resources in the core registry. An extension rule may
name those resources and resources in its own extension registry, and MUST NOT name another
extension. Local aliases and common-table-expression names are one-part names. Every identifier
MUST match `^[a-z][a-z0-9_]*$`, be unique in its resolution scope, and compare code point for code
point. Delimiting an identifier does not admit another spelling or a keyword. Every other base
relation, including a system catalog or validator-created relation, makes the registry invalid.

Each visible table exposes exactly its registry-declared columns under §4. A resource absent from
the packet binds as an empty relation of that shape. A declared resource that cannot be read MUST
NOT be substituted with an empty relation for a rule that depends on it; that rule is incomplete
under §8.

Static validation MUST return the complete set of base registry resources named by a rule. Local
aliases, common table expressions, derived tables, literals, and comments do not enter that set. A
registry MUST NOT maintain a second dependency list for the same fact.

<a id="ew-tier-2-type-binding" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 4. Logical type binding and general expression semantics

The complete registry-to-SQL binding is:

| registry type | SQL domain |
|---|---|
| `int32` | `INTEGER` |
| `int64` | `BIGINT` |
| `float32` | `REAL` |
| `float64` | `DOUBLE PRECISION` |
| `bool` | `BOOLEAN` |
| `date` | `DATE` |
| `timestamp` | `TIMESTAMP(6) WITH TIME ZONE` |
| `string` | unbounded `CHARACTER VARYING` |
| `json_string` | unbounded `CHARACTER VARYING` |

`INTEGER` and `BIGINT` are signed 32-bit and signed 64-bit domains. `REAL` and
`DOUBLE PRECISION` are IEEE 754 binary32 and binary64. `DECIMAL(p,s)` denotes exact multiples of
10^-s with at most `p` digits, where 1 ≤ `p` ≤ 38 and 0 ≤ `s` ≤ `p`. The profile additionally
admits the domain of an exact-decimal literal and `DECIMAL(38,0)` as an explicit cast target. It
provides no JSON parsing or implicit distinction between `string` and `json_string`.

A cast to `DECIMAL(38,0)` accepts an integer or scale-zero decimal and preserves its mathematical
value. A cast to `DOUBLE PRECISION` accepts an exact or approximate numeric and rounds to nearest,
ties to even. No other explicit cast is admitted. An implementation may lower these domains to
engine-native representations only when values and operations retain the specified semantics.

ISO SQL null and three-valued Boolean semantics apply except where this part narrows them.
`COUNT` follows ISO SQL; `MIN`, `MAX`, and `PERCENTILE_CONT` ignore null inputs; grouping,
`DISTINCT`, and set operations treat nulls on ISO terms. Window and ordered-set ordering places
null after every non-null value.

A validator MUST bind and type-check the complete query before execution. No implicit cast may
convert character, Boolean, date, or timestamp values to or from a numeric domain. Identical
domains retain that domain. `INTEGER` with `BIGINT` yields `BIGINT`; an integer with
`DECIMAL(38,0)` yields `DECIMAL(38,0)`; `REAL` with `DOUBLE PRECISION` yields
`DOUBLE PRECISION`; and an exact with an approximate numeric yields the approximate domain,
widened to `DOUBLE PRECISION` if either operand has it. No other pair has an implicit common type.
These rules govern comparisons, `CASE`, `COALESCE`, set operations, and routine arguments. An
exact-decimal literal may instead compare with an exact numeric by mathematical value.

<a id="ew-tier-2-deterministic-semantics" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 5. Numeric, timestamp, and character semantics

Approximate values use this total order for comparison, grouping, distinctness, set operations,
`MIN`, `MAX`, and ordering:

```text
-infinity < finite values < +infinity < NaN
```

All NaN encodings compare equal for those operations; positive and negative zero compare equal.
Null retains §4's semantics. `isnan` and `isfinite` accept either approximate domain, return null
for null, and otherwise identify NaN and finite values respectively.

An exact-integer literal takes the first containing domain in the order `INTEGER`, `BIGINT`,
`DECIMAL(38,0)` and is invalid outside that range. An exact decimal with `p` digits and `s`
fractional digits takes `DECIMAL(p,s)`. A non-zero-scale exact decimal is admitted only in an
exact comparison, through conversion to an approximate common type, or as `PERCENTILE_CONT`'s
fixed `0.5`. An approximate literal converts to `DOUBLE PRECISION` with round-to-nearest,
ties-to-even.

Approximate arithmetic and `ABS` use the corresponding IEEE binary operation with
round-to-nearest, ties-to-even. Mixed approximate expressions use the wider domain; an exact value
combined with an approximate value converts to the approximate domain. IEEE infinity, NaN, and
signed zero result from overflow, invalid operations, and zero division rather than a host
exception.

Division MUST have at least one operand explicitly cast to `DOUBLE PRECISION` and returns
`DOUBLE PRECISION`. `PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5)` converts its ordering value to binary64, performs
linear interpolation there, and returns binary64.

Exact integer and scale-zero decimal addition, subtraction, multiplication, and unary minus are
mathematical over the common result domain. A rule whose admitted values can overflow `INTEGER` or
`BIGINT` MUST cast its operands to `DECIMAL(38,0)` first. Static validation MUST reject an
expression that can overflow without that widening. Overflow of `DECIMAL(38,0)` is incomplete
evaluation; wraparound, saturation, and truncation are forbidden.

The execution time zone is UTC. `TIMESTAMP(6) WITH TIME ZONE` denotes an instant at microsecond
precision. `INTERVAL '<digits>' DAY` denotes the stated integer multiplied by 86,400 elapsed
seconds. Only a timestamp left operand plus or minus such an interval is admitted and returns the
timestamp domain. A result outside the supported timestamp range makes evaluation incomplete and
MUST NOT wrap, clamp, saturate, or truncate.

Character operations compare stored Unicode scalar sequences in ascending scalar-value order,
without case folding or normalization. A scalar is in U+0000–U+D7FF or U+E000–U+10FFFF. In
`LIKE`, `_` matches one scalar and `%` matches zero or more scalars.

<a id="ew-tier-2-bounded-recursion" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 6. Bounded recursive common table expressions

A recursive common table expression is admitted only when a validator can establish that it:

1. declares an integer depth column;
2. assigns a non-negative integer literal to depth in every seed arm;
3. contains exactly one self-reference in the recursive arm joined through `UNION ALL`;
4. assigns the next depth as prior depth plus a positive integer literal; and
5. guards the recursive arm by comparing prior depth with a finite integer-literal upper bound.

Mutual recursion, multiple self-references, data-dependent bounds, non-increasing depth, and any
other recursive form make the registry invalid before execution. An execution limit is only a
backstop and does not prove that a query conforms.

<a id="ew-tier-2-finding-relation" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 7. Finding-relation semantics

A completed rule produces a typed, unordered bag of rows. Row order carries no meaning; duplicate
multiplicity does. An empty bag passes, and every row of a non-empty bag is an offending row for
the declared finding.

Every final column MUST have a unique lower-snake-case name. A simple reference may inherit its
declared name when unique. Every other expression, and either of two references with the same
inherited name, MUST use an explicit alias. Static validation rejects duplicate, unnamed, or
nonconforming final names before execution.

A report may serialize or sample fewer rows only under its reporting contract. Sampling MUST NOT
redefine the complete result or imply that omitted rows do not exist.

<a id="ew-tier-2-evaluation-outcomes" data-spec-anchor="canonical"></a>
## 8. Evaluation outcomes

SQL outside the selected profile makes its registry invalid. A validator MUST reject it before
execution and MUST NOT report that language defect as an offending packet row.

A validator lacking the exact selected language reports the registry's Tier 2 evaluation as
**unsupported**. A validator that supports the language but cannot complete a valid rule because
an input is unreadable, the engine fails, exact arithmetic overflows, a timestamp exceeds the
supported range, or a resource or timeout limit is reached reports that evaluation as
**incomplete**. It MUST NOT fabricate a packet finding from its inability to evaluate.

Only a completed query returning rows produces the rule's declared finding. An invalid,
unsupported, or incomplete required evaluation cannot support a claim that the affected core or
extension registry passed. Core and each extension retain separate evaluation state; one state
MUST NOT be attributed to another, relabeled as a rule failure, or omitted from a complete-packet
claim. Part 05 owns the reporting workflow and eventual report representation.
