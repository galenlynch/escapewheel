## 3. Correction: float seconds are not lossy here

Earlier notes in this project asserted that NWB is the weaker source because float seconds
cannot round-trip to integer ticks. **That is wrong**, and it was asserted without testing.

Measured: `round(index / 30000 * 30000)` recovers the exact `int64` sample index for every
sample tested, at session lengths of 1, 2, 3, 6 and 12 hours — zero mismatches in ~200 000
samples per length — and 500 000 consecutive samples near t = 3 h remain distinct floats.
`float64`'s mantissa has ample headroom for 30 kHz indices. NWB also records
`resolution = 3.333e-05`, so the rate needed for the conversion is present.

What actually separates the sources is §2's other rows: `amplitude_unit`, granularity, and
whether behaviour and brain regions are present at all.

## 5. Finding: clock alignment is anchor-point, not linear

R3 §3.4 models a clock's relation to its parent as `transform_slope` and
`transform_offset`, and states that piecewise or anchor-point alignment "needs a child
table. This revision does not define one; packets requiring it are out of scope for
`escapewheel-portable-v0.1`."

This dataset requires it. `nwb-zarr-smasher` aligns timestamps twice, both through observed
sync pulses rather than a fitted line:

```python
local_aligned = align_timestamps_to_anchor_points(
    stream.sample_numbers, sync_events.sample_number.values,
    main_events.sample_number.values / 30000)
harp_aligned  = align_timestamps_to_anchor_points(
    local_aligned, start_times, harp_times)
```

The raw stream carries the consequence directly: `times_seg0` is one `float64` per sample,
191.6M of them, because the correction is not two numbers.

Sample index remains exact and canonical as `t_tick`. What R3 §3.4 cannot express is the
relation between a probe clock and the behaviour or wall clock.

**Candidate revision R13.**

## 6. Finding: the codec floor does not admit the acquisition codec

Every compressed stream, including the sync stream, uses **WavPack**:

```text
Neuropix-PXI-100.46110   <i2  [191_631_595, 384]  codec wavpack
NI-DAQmx-107.PXIe-6341   <i2  [191_652_096, 1]    codec wavpack
```

R3 §7.2 sets the reader floor at `{none, gzip, zstd}` and permits blosc and
`sharding_indexed` under a manifest declaration. WavPack is none of these. R3 §7.3's own
evidence anticipated the situation — "specialized audio-style codecs do far better but are
not portable."

Three routes, none free: transcode on import and lose the compression on ~147 GB per probe;
extend the declaration mechanism to admit WavPack and accept that a conforming reader may
not open the arrays; or reference the source arrays externally under Part 03's external-asset
rule instead of copying them.

R14 admits all three and leaves the choice to the writer. **For this dataset the route is to
reference externally** (decided 2026-08-26): the arrays stay where they are, the packet
declares them with `external: true` and `codecs: ["wavpack"]`, and nothing is transcoded.

That choice exposed a defect in R3 §7.2, which declared codecs once per packet and made the
reader's capability check all-or-nothing. A reader lacking WavPack would have had to refuse
the entire packet, including baseline arrays it owns. **R15** moves the declaration to the
resource entry so a reader degrades per array instead.

## 7. Recommendation

Import from **SpikeInterface outputs plus the aind-data-schema JSONs**, not from NWB.

`spikes.npy` gives `t_tick` with no conversion; `postprocessed/` gives amplitudes with the
unit R11 §2 requires; per-stream granularity matches `core.probes`; and the
`spikesorted/`/`curated/` split matches `core.sorting_runs`/`core.curation_versions`
exactly. The metadata JSONs supply session, subject, probe, and manifest fields, which is
the extension-to-core mapping Part 09 §7 describes.

The smashed NWB remains the source for two things SpikeInterface does not carry: behaviour
(`core.trials`, `core.events`) and per-channel CCF locations
(`core.electrodes.brain_region`).

NWB is not disqualified on precision. It is not the primary source because it lacks the
amplitude unit, aggregates probes, and — in the ephys-only form — has an empty
`acquisition/` and no behaviour.

### 8.1 Curation labels: three sources, and they disagree

`curated/<stream>/properties/` carries three, for 586 units on one probe:

```text
KSLabel          mua 345   good 241
decoder_label    mua 291   noise 149   sua 146
default_qc       True 338  False 248
```

**`decoder_label` already uses exactly `sua`/`mua`/`noise`** — R11 §3's closed vocabulary,
native to the data. R11 §3 chose `sua` over the more common `good` to avoid colliding with
`analysis.good_units.is_good`; that choice matches what the decoder emits.

They disagree for 111 of 586 units:

```text
KS=good  decoder=sua      138        KS=good  decoder=mua       82
KS=mua   decoder=mua      209        KS=good  decoder=noise     21
KS=mua   decoder=noise    128        KS=mua   decoder=sua        8
```

So an importer cannot simply transliterate. R11 §3's rule that `good` becomes `sua` holds for
`KSLabel`, but applying it would contradict `decoder_label` for 103 units. **The importer must
choose which source is authoritative and record the rest**, which is what R11 §3's
`source_quality_label` guidance in `core.units.metadata` is for. The choice is not incidental:
it changes the label for 19% of units.

### 8.2 Amplitude unit

The SortingAnalyzer's `.zattrs` carries `settings: {'return_in_uV': True}`, so
`core.sorting_runs.amplitude_unit` is `uV`, read directly. `spikeinterface_info` gives
version 0.103.0 for `core.sorting_runs.sorter_version`. R11 §2's design holds against the
data it was designed from.

### 8.3 Sorter parameters

`processing.json` carries 90 `data_processes` across five named stages — Ephys
preprocessing, Spike sorting, Ephys postprocessing, Ephys curation, Ephys visualization —
each with parameters. Sufficient for `core.sorting_runs.parameters` and

### 8.4 `session_start_time` carries a timezone it does not have

```text
session.json   session_start_time  2025-09-25T12:43:56+00:00
               stream_start_time   2025-09-25T12:44:11.035095-07:00
```

Seven hours apart. The asset is named `..._2025-09-25_12-43-56` and the stream begins 15 s
after that value in **Pacific** time, so `session_start_time` is local time carrying a
`+00:00` offset it does not have. Session end is 69 min after session start while the stream
runs 85 min; the two intervals are offset rather than nested.

R3 §2.1 requires wall-clock columns to be `isAdjustedToUTC=true`. Importing this value
faithfully puts a seven-hour error into `core.sessions.session_start_time`, and R6 §3's
`tick_zero_plausible` rule permits a one-day window, so **it would not detect it**.

An importer MUST NOT trust the offset on `session_start_time`. Deriving it from
`stream_start_time`, which is correctly offset, is the available alternative.
