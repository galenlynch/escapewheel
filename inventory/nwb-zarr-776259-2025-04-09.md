## 1. It is Zarr, and that is the normal case

The NWB is a directory, not an HDF5 file: `hdmf-zarr`, `zarr_format: 2`, 254 arrays. AIND
writes NWB-Zarr for approximately all of its NWB output, so an NWB adapter targets
`hdmf_zarr.NWBZarrIO` first and `pynwb.NWBHDF5IO` second, not the reverse.

**zarr-python 3.3.0 cannot open it through the consolidated metadata.** `.zmetadata`
carries `fill_value: 0` on vlen-string arrays, which the v2 parser rejects:

```text
TypeError: Invalid type: 0. Expected a string.     zarr/core/dtype/npy/bytes.py:1204
```

`zarr.open(..., use_consolidated=False)` works, and every numeric and vlen-string array
then reads correctly. The same fault blocks the scalar datetimes — `session_start_time`,
`timestamps_reference_time`, `file_create_date` — which have to be decoded from their
chunk bytes directly. Any reader of AIND NWB-Zarr needs the workaround.

The packet floor is Zarr **v3** (R3 §7). These arrays are **v2**. R14 lets a packet point at
bytes it does not own, but says nothing about a referenced array conforming to a different
Zarr version than the profile declares. Open question, §7.

## 3. Time is the finding

Everything below is measured, and every one of these defects is invisible to an NWB
validator because NWB does not require a time base to be declared.

### 3.1 Times are on the HARP epoch, not on `session_start_time`

`session_start_time` and `timestamps_reference_time` are both
`2025-04-09T17:25:12-07:00`. NWB's contract makes `timestamps` seconds relative to that.
They are not: spikes, trials and behaviour all sit at **+1.157×10⁷ s**, about 134 days,
placing tick zero in late November 2024 — the HARP clock's own epoch.

This settles the open question of where tick zero sits: **on HARP, not on session start**,
and `session_start_time` does not locate it. R6's `tick_zero_utc` must be written from the
HARP epoch, and cannot be inferred from the NWB.

### 3.2 The epoch costs 24 bits of the mantissa

| Time base | float64 spacing | levels per 30 kHz sample |
|---|---|---|
| session-relative (0–6.4 ks) | 0.91 ps | 36,650,388 |
| HARP epoch (1.157×10⁷ s) | **1.86 ns** | **17,896** |

This corrects the note carried in `STATUS.md`. Float seconds do round-trip to integer
ticks exactly — *for session-relative seconds*, which is what was measured. AIND does not
write session-relative seconds. At the epoch magnitude it actually uses, the representation
retains 1.86 ns, and 1.86 ns is the precision floor for every alignment done downstream.

### 3.3 Spike times are not on any 30 kHz grid

`t × 30000` is uniformly distributed mod 1 (max |frac| = 0.5, mean 0.229) for every choice
of origin. Per probe the local step is ~3.3330×10⁻⁵ s, i.e. an effective **30,002.85 Hz**,
not 30,000 — a per-probe alignment has been baked into the stored seconds. The original
integer sample index is not in the file, and the anchors that produced the alignment are
not either.

So `core.spike_events.t_tick` **cannot be recovered exactly from this NWB.** Recovering it
means fitting a per-probe slope and offset and assuming the map is affine — which is the
assumption R13 exists because AIND's data violates.

Resolving affine-versus-piecewise from these timestamps is not possible: pinning the phase
across 1.9×10⁸ samples needs the rate to 2.6 ppb, and float64 at this epoch gives 56 ppm.
The two step values observed differ by exactly one float64 quantum, so the apparent 56 ppm
"drift" is the resolution floor, not a measurement.

### 3.3.1 The HARP grid is exact, so behaviour needs no fitting

Spike times are unrecoverable (§3.3) because a per-probe alignment was baked into them.
Behaviour is the opposite case, and it is exact.

Every HARP-clocked timestamp lies on a **32 µs grid — 31,250 Hz**, HARP's native fractional
resolution, measured against the epoch itself rather than the first sample:

| stream | n | `max |frac(t × 31250)|` |
|---|---|---|
| `left_lick_time`, `right_lick_time` | 3184 | **0.0** |
| `left_reward_delivery_time`, `right_reward_delivery_time` | 177 | **0.0** |
| `intervals/trials` `start_time`, `goCue_start_time` | 1416 | **0.0** |

Not "within float precision" — exactly zero, for all 4777 values, and `k / 31250` returns the
stored double bit-for-bit. Neighbouring rates fail cleanly: 100 kHz, 10 kHz, 1 kHz and 30 kHz
all land mid-grid (max |frac| ≈ 0.32–0.48), because 32 µs is not an integer number of any of
their quanta.

So `t_tick = round(t × 31250)` is lossless for every behaviour and trial time in the file, and
the resulting ticks are int64 in the range 3.62×10¹¹ — five orders of magnitude inside the
type. One clock serves all six streams; their ticks compare directly with no conversion.

**Binding tick 0 to UTC is a separate and softer question.** The grid is exact, but R6's
`tick_zero_utc` needs the HARP epoch in wall-clock terms, and the only source for that here is
`session_start_time`, which is second-resolution and which `session.json` gets wrong by seven
hours (§6.4). The tick domain is exact; the wall-clock binding inherits the metadata's
accuracy, which is why R6 makes it optional.

### 3.4 LFP timestamps are sign-flipped

Spikes, trials and behaviour run **+11,575,876 → +11,583,848 s**. All twelve LFP series run
**−11,575,876 → −11,569,489 s**. Same magnitude, opposite sign, in the same file.

### 3.5 LFP is a regenerated nominal grid, ~0.6 s wrong by the end

LFP `timestamps` are exactly uniform at **1250.000000 Hz** (diff ptp = 1.86 ns, one float
quantum). The probe's own aligned rate implies AP/24 = **1250.118550 Hz**. The 95 ppm
error accumulates to **0.61 s** over the 6388 s session, so LFP and spikes from the same
probe disagree by up to 0.6 s at the end — before the sign flip is even considered.

The twelve series also have twelve different `t0`, spread over 0.130 s (163 LFP samples, ~3900 AP samples), so
a session-level start time would be wrong for eleven of them. R20's per-axis coordinate
declaration is the right shape; a packet-level one is not.

### 3.6 What R20 buys here

Each LFP series stores 7,983,750 float64 timestamps: 63.9 MB, **767 MB across twelve**. The
grid is exactly regular, so R20's regular-sampling form replaces each array with three
integers. That is the case R20 §1 argued for, on real data.

### 3.7 Streams do not share an extent

```text
ephys      6,388 s
trials     6,214 s
behaviour  7,792 s     (licks continue ~26 min past the last spike)
session.json  6,399 s  (17:25:12 → 19:11:39)
```

Behaviour outruns the declared session by 23 minutes. `core.sessions` cannot carry one
extent that is true of every stream.

## 4. Unit quality: three sources, not one, and not our vocabulary

| Column | Values |

| `bombcell_label` | mua 3711, noise 1508, **good** 1077, **non_soma** 485 |
| `decoder_label` | mua 2635, noise 2444, sua 1702 |
| `unitrefine_label` | mua 2635, noise 2444, sua 1702 |
| `default_qc` | False 3445, True 3336 |

`unitrefine_label` and `decoder_label` are **identical for all 6781 units** — two names for
one classifier, so there are three sources, not four. `bombcell_label` and `decoder_label`
agree on **53.3%**.

R16 is confirmed: one designated quality label would be a fiction. But R11 §3 closed
`quality_label` to `sua`/`mua`/`noise` and requires a validator to **reject** anything else,
and bombcell emits `good` and `non_soma`. A conforming packet cannot carry bombcell's
labels as written. Open question, §7.

## 6. Consequences for the importer

1. **NWB is the weaker source, for a stronger reason than the one on record.** `STATUS.md`
   gives amplitude unit, probe aggregation and missing behaviour. The decisive reason is
   §3.3: the integer tick is gone. Sorter output still has sample indices.
2. **NWB is the only source that carries trials and behaviour** in one artifact — 708 trials
   × 68 columns, fused from Bonsai. `05`'s acceptance criteria require `core.trials`.
3. So the first packet is **not single-source**. Ticks and units come from the sorter;
   trials, behaviour events and CCF registration come from the NWB; identity and subject
   come from the aind-data-schema JSONs. That is an argument for adapters that return
   records rather than write packets, and a writer that merges them.
4. **`session.json` is the file with the timezone bug**, not the NWB: it says
   `2025-04-09T17:25:12+00:00` for a Pacific wall clock, a 7 h error. The NWB says
   `-07:00` and is right. Since the plan of record imports identity from the JSONs, the
   importer must not trust that offset.

## 7. Open questions this raised

- **Zarr version of a referenced array.** The floor is v3; AIND's NWB and its LFP are v2.
  R14 does not say whether an external resource may declare a different Zarr version, and
  R15's per-resource `codecs` has no `zarr_format` companion.
- **`quality_label` cannot stay closed.** R11 §3 requires rejecting values outside
  `sua`/`mua`/`noise`; bombcell emits `good` and `non_soma` on 1562 of 6781 units. Either
  the vocabulary reopens, or R16's label rows carry a per-source vocabulary, or the
  importer discards a source's distinctions to fit.
- **Session extent.** Behaviour outruns ephys by 23 minutes. Whether `core.sessions` carries
  the union, the ephys extent, or per-stream extents is unstated.
- **Recovering ticks from float seconds.** Whether a per-probe affine fit is sound is not
  answerable from the NWB alone; it needs the sorter's sample indices alongside, which is a
  measurement worth making before an NWB-only import path is offered at all.
