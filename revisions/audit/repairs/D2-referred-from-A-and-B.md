Decide between an indicative statement in Part 02 §7.1 (text given), a revision that adds the requirement and its checks, or dropping §12.3's dangling cross-reference.

'Refuse a failure' is not determinate as written: a failing call already fails the run (Part 05 §4.4), so what is forbidden is treating the unparseable detail as a further error or declining to report the failure; Part 12 §9's parallel is 'MUST NOT fail on a report's contents alone' and 'MUST NOT refuse a report for carrying keys it does not recognise'.
  - *Checks:* A writer test could observe it (a `flight-v0.1` adapter failing with a detail that is not JSON, and the build still reporting the failure and its message), but none exists: test_delivery.py has no failing Flight case and no validator or corpus case can see a build's report. A build can always comply.
  - *Placement:* Part 12 §9, directly after finding 96's sentence, is the only sensible home; it cannot stand in the part without that sentence. Part 05 §4.4 owns what a build reports of a failure in general and should not gain a Flight-specific rule (R146 moved the per-protocol channel out of it).
  - *Implementation and corpus:* escapewheel_writer/reading.py::_read_flight never parses the detail, so it cannot refuse over one, and it reports the message together with the raw detail bytes, not 'the message alone'. It complies with the prohibition and with Part 05 §4.4, and departs from the clause's second half. If finding 96 leads to the build parsing the entry, this rule becomes live code and needs a test.
  - *Wording:* `extra_info` is 'detail' in the parts. 'Refuse a failure' should be 'fail on'. A form consistent with Part 05 §4.4 would be: 'A build MUST NOT fail on a detail it cannot read as such an entry, and reports the failure with the message and the detail as they arrived.'
