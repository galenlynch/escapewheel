# Disputed rules

Two careful readings of each clause below disagree on whether a part still owes the rule. The second reading states both.

21 rules, in revision order. Nothing here has been applied. The audit record is [applied-targets.md](../applied-targets.md).

### 1. R3 §2.1

- **The revision decides:** wall-clock and calendar columns are disjoint from experimental time and the two MUST NOT be mixed: a `*_tick` column has no timezone, epoch or calendar semantics, nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp, and nothing administrative uses a tick
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “Every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis, and media frame timing record MUST declare a time basis: an integer tick and a `clock_id`.” (Part 01 §2.2 (ew-time-basis))
- **The two readings:** The clause's paragraph is headed 'Wall-clock columns only' and is first a scope statement for R3 §2.1's `timestamp` and `date` encoding rules; its last sentence adds the MUST NOT. Reading A (refuted, stated in pieces): each operative half is in a part. Part 01 §2.2 requires every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis and media frame timing record to declare 'an integer tick and a `clock_id`' (so nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp); Part 01 §3.9 says the tick-zero binding 'is the only route from a packet's ticks to real time; absent it, ticks relate to each other and to nothing else' (so a tick has no timezone, epoch or calendar semantics); Part 01 §2.3 states a session's extent 'as wall-clock time, not as a tick range'; Part 02 §5.3 types the two apart, and the meta-schema forces a `coordinate` column (the only role that may carry `clock_from`) to `int64`, so a registry cannot declare a tick as a `timestamp`. R6 later added `tick_zero_utc`, the one sanctioned bridge. Reading B (confirmed): no part says the two systems MUST NOT be mixed, and 'nothing administrative uses a tick' is stated nowhere in general form, only for a session's extent; an extension author could type a provenance time as a tick without contradicting any sentence. The dispute is whether the general prohibition is a rule of its own or the summary of the typed rules that realise it.

### 2. R3 §7.1

- **The revision decides:** arrays MUST be little-endian (the `bytes` codec's `endian` configuration)
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** No part or artifact states a byte order, and no accepted revision names R3 §7.1's sentence as withdrawn. Reading A (changed-by-later): R129 §3.4 rebuilt the Zarr rules and enumerated what still constrains a writer: 'A writer MAY use any codec permitted by the applicable Zarr specification, subject to the existing prohibition on `transpose` and the current regular-chunk-grid rule', and of the floor's bare `bytes` pattern, 'unmentioned configuration members do not participate in the floor match. Other configuration members remain governed by the applicable upstream Zarr or codec specification.' `endian` is such a member, so R129 handed it to upstream Zarr; Part 03 §6.4 now obliges a reader to expose every valid v3 array matching the floor, which includes a big-endian `bytes` codec. Reading B (confirmed): R129 never mentions byte order and does not list R3 §7.1 among what it replaces, so the MUST stands unapplied. The repository itself takes reading B and declines to restore: the pending draft `revisions/drafts/the-reader-floor-bounds-an-arrays-byte-order.md` lists R3 §7.1 as 'the byte-order and `fill_value` requirements this supersedes' and its §3.1 says they 'no longer bind' once accepted, arguing that restoring the sentence 'would contradict a floor the parts already state' and would make a packet referencing a big-endian external array non-conforming. Restoring the sentence is therefore the wrong repair on either reading; the open question is only whether R129 already withdrew it or the draft must.

### 3. R14 §2.3.2

- **The revision decides:** a validator MUST offer a separate, explicitly requested mode that resolves external URIs and checks `sha256` and `size_bytes` where declared
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode.” (Part 04 §5.3 (ew-external-resource-verification))
- **The two readings:** Part 04 §5.3 says only when the external levels run ('Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode') and places no obligation on a validator to offer them, so the part is weaker than R14 §2.3.2 as written. Whether a later revision made it so is disputable. Reading A (changed-by-later): R36 amends R14 §2.3 and restates the offer conditionally in its §2.3, 'A validator offering level 3 MUST offer 3a and MAY offer 3b, and MUST NOT run 3b unless separately asked', which presupposes a validator that does not offer level 3 and makes the checksum check optional. On this reading the unconditional MUST is gone, and the `sha256` half of the finding's rule is changed beyond dispute (reading every byte became MAY). Reading B (confirmed): R36 only split R15 §4's level 3 into a cheap and an expensive layer and did not say it withdrew the obligation to offer the mode; R80 §2.4 still says 'R14 §2.3.2 unchanged', though of structural validation staying off the network. Either way the part also omits R36 §2.3's own sentence, so a repair would state it: a validator offering the external levels MUST offer 3a, MAY offer 3b and 3c, and MUST NOT run the reading levels unless separately asked. Whether to prefix 'A validator MUST offer' is the open point.

### 4. R51 §2.1

- **The revision decides:** Declaring members makes the listing level (3a) compare each member's size rather than the aggregate
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** Reading one: 'Declaring members also makes level 3a per-member rather than aggregate' redefines what level 3a establishes for a resource with `members`, as R36 §2.3's table defines the level, so a validator at 3a compares each member's size. Reading two: the paragraph carries no keyword in a section that bolds its MAY and MUST, and continues 'the same listing that produces the total can compare each member instead, at no additional request', which describes what the record enables. No part states either: Part 04 §5.3's 3a row does not mention size at all (finding 28), §5.4 has no size sentence, and §10's `packet.resource.member_size` compares the manifest's sum with itself. No later revision addresses it (R136 §3.3 and R130 §3 touch only member digests and the sum check). If finding 28's size comparison is restored, reading one adds one clause to it; under reading two nothing is owed.

### 5. R65 §2.2

- **The revision decides:** This standard MUST NOT enumerate instruments
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “This standard names the concepts it defines — §3.16 is the one it defines for extracellular voltage — and it does not enumerate instruments.” (Part 01 §3.17)
- **The two readings:** Reading one: R65 §2.2 binds the standard with MUST NOT and Part 01 §3.17 says only that it 'does not enumerate instruments', so a later revision adding an instrument list would breach no keyword; Part 00 does bind the specification itself elsewhere ('a specification release MUST NOT contain an unresolved ownership conflict'). Reading two: the subject is the standard, which no writer, reader, validator or consumer can fail, and R111 §3.3 (stated at Part 00 §2.4: 'Every requirement binds one of them or states a property of an artifact') calls a keyword that does neither 'a defect in this specification rather than a rule anything can fail'; on that reading the indicative self-description is the admissible form, and it was the form of the incorporating commit (9fc3babd). The consequence an implementation can fail is stated with a keyword in Part 02 §8.3: 'core MUST NOT declare a detail table of it, since Part 01 §3.17 makes this standard name no instruments'. I lean to reading two: nothing an actor does changes with the wording.

### 6. R65 §2.3

- **The revision decides:** A consumer MUST NOT infer an instrument from an asset's semantic_type, its name, or its URI
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “MUST NOT infer an instrument from an asset's `semantic_type` or name” (Part 01 §3.17)
- **The two readings:** The URI item was in Part 01 §3.17 until the commit incorporating R128 (6fc02db5) removed it, together with 'its URI' in §3.18's 'labels rather than references'. Reading one, changed-by-later: R128 §3.3 says 'Part 01 MUST NOT make URI, container format, registry binding, physical encoding, or resource-level producing-run placement part of either concept', and R128 §3.6 removes `uri` from the three asset tables, so an asset as Part 01 defines it has no URI to infer from and the item cannot stand in §3.17. Reading two: R128's crosswalk classes the §3.17 change as a split that removes mechanics 'without changing what an array means', and R128 nowhere permits a consumer to read an instrument out of a location; the item should have moved to the part that owns the entry's `uri` (Part 04) and did not. Searched spec/parts for 'infer' beside URI: only Part 04 §5.3, on physical properties. R102 and R103 do not address it. If reading two is taken, the sentence belongs in Part 04, not Part 01.

### 7. R80 §2.3

- **The revision decides:** it MUST be stated that the packet's recorded checksums do not cover the default document
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** R80 §2.3: 'The consequences of not owning it are the point and MUST be stated rather than assumed. R52's checksums do not cover it, ...'. Reading one: the capitalised MUST is an instruction to the specification's text, and its content is a consequence rather than an obligation on any actor; the part already entails it, since a resolution document MUST NOT be a packet resource (Part 04 §5.5) and a digest is recorded only on a resource entry (Part 04 §4.3), so nothing is missing as a rule. Reading two: the Decision requires the consequence to be stated 'rather than assumed', which is exactly a refusal of that entailment, and no part states it: §5.5 says only that immutability does not reach the document, and Part 05 §6 only lists it in a promotion record. No later revision lifts the instruction. If reading two is taken, the sentence belongs in Part 04 §5.5 beside the default, e.g. 'The packet's recorded digests do not cover the default', and finding 60's restored sentence can carry it.

### 8. R93 §3.1

- **The revision decides:** Part 02 MUST NOT name a storage technology, a file format, an encoding, or a physical property of a stored resource, except to place such a rule outside its own scope
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “identities, relationships, and constraints without selecting a storage technology.” (Part 00 §8 (ew-numbered-part-ownership))
- **The two readings:** R93 §3.1: 'Part 02 MUST NOT name a storage technology, a file format, an encoding, or a physical property of a stored resource, except to place such a rule outside its own scope. Every clause of Part 02 MUST hold unchanged under any physical profile.' The nearest statement is Part 00 §8 (R122 §3.6), which binds the parts with MUST to a dependency direction in which Part 02 represents concepts 'without selecting a storage technology'; R122 says it supersedes only R93 §3.6's header-precedence sentence. Reading one: R93 §3.1 stands as written, 'name' is wider than 'select', and no part states it; Part 02 §6 ('a Zarr binding in another language cannot see them') and §8.6 ('Parquet reads the columns a query projects') name technologies in rationale, so the rule is both unstated and unobserved. Reading two: R93 never meant 'name' literally. Its §2 counts Part 02 naming a technology three times and says 'only one is a leak', passing the Zarr example that is still in the part (now §6), and its §3.2 says 'Naming one is not the test, and could not be' and states the operative rule as dependence; the Parquet sentence entered Part 02 §8.6 with R97, accepted the same day (commit 7b6bf57c). On that reading the rule is that no Part 02 clause selects or depends on a storage technology, which Part 00 §8 states with the same force, and the finding is refuted as stated. Restoring the literal sentence would contradict Part 02 §§6 and 8.6 as they stand and would need those sentences changed.

### 9. R93 §3.5

- **The revision decides:** Part 10 does not absorb Part 07's performance notes and MUST NOT repeat them
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** R93 §3.5: 'Part 10 does not absorb it, and MUST NOT repeat it', of Part 07's performance notes. No part forbids the repetition: Part 07 §6 and Part 10 §1 state the division in the indicative, R93 §3.4's neighbouring prohibition concerns a clause appearing in Part 10 and a normative part, and Part 00 §8 forbids only a second version of a rule another part owns. R146 §3.5 supersedes R93 §3.5 in part, withdrawing only the sentence that kept R43's timings in Part 06. Reading one: this is an instruction to whoever edits two informative parts, binding no actor of Part 00 §2.4 and no packet; its substance, which numbers belong where, is stated at the head of both parts, and an informative part cannot be the sole statement of an obligation (Part 00 §8), so there is no rule to restore at the ledger target. Reading two: it is a standing prohibition of the same kind as Part 00 §8's part-ownership rules, which the specification does state, and it is stated nowhere; it would then belong in Part 00 §8, not in Part 07.

### 10. R111 §3.3

- **The revision decides:** every MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY in the specification MUST either bind a named actor or state a property of an artifact
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “Every requirement binds one of them or states a property of an artifact.” (Part 00 §2.4 (ew-specification-actors))
- **The two readings:** Part 00 §2.4 states the content with unchanged scope: 'Every requirement binds one of them or states a property of an artifact.' The finding turns only on mood. Reading A (weakened): the clause says every keyword 'in this specification **MUST** do one of two things', keywords bind only in capitals (Part 00 §2.5), and no later revision lowered it; R122 and R155 revised §2.4 without restating the rule, and the sentence has been indicative since R111's acceptance (a6d84d74). Reading B (stated): by the clause's own terms it 'binds whoever writes this specification, and nothing else', and a clause failing it 'is a defect in this specification rather than a rule anything can fail'. The specification's writer is not one of §2.4's five actors, so a MUST in Part 00 would itself bind no named actor, and a universal indicative about the specification has the same consequence: a requirement binding nobody contradicts §2.4 and is a defect to correct. R155 §1 reads the sentence as a requirement ('while requiring every requirement to bind one of them or state a property of an artifact'). If reading A is taken, the restored sentence would be 'Every MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY in this specification MUST bind one of them or state a property of an artifact', and it would need to pass its own test as a predicate on the specification.

### 11. R111 §3.5

- **The revision decides:** a carried identifier gets no name column, and a table MUST NOT declare one for it
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “A carried identifier likewise gets no corresponding name column: its value is already the source's own identity.” (Part 02 §8.2)
- **The two readings:** The sentence is not in R111 §3.5's text; it is R53 §2.3.1's rule ('A **carried** identifier gets no name, and MUST NOT be given one') as R111's sweep restated it in Part 02 §8.2 ('a table MUST NOT declare one for it'). R126's incorporation (278c2d6a) rewrote §8.2: the MUST NOT moved to 'A table that merely carries or references an assigned identifier MUST NOT declare that same-base `*_name` column', and the `carried`-identity case became the indicative 'A carried identifier likewise gets no corresponding name column'. Reading A (changed-by-later): R126 §3.6 restates the name rules for the registry's 'one complete conformance contract' and decides only 'A table that merely carries or references an assigned identifier **MUST NOT** declare that corresponding same-base `_name` column'; R126 §3.5's `registry.identity.name` is scoped 'assigned-name ownership', its crosswalk says §8.2 now 'states the assigned-name and carried/referenced-name invariant', and the reference check tests assigned identifiers only. On that reading the prohibition for a `carried` identifier is no longer a registry conformance rule and the indicative is what R126 left. Reading B (weakened): R126 §3.6 says nothing about an identifier whose identity is `carried`, never names R53 §2.3.1 or the sentence, and its 'carries' means a table holding an assigned identifier it does not own; so the keyword was dropped in incorporation without a decision, and 'likewise' reaches it only by implication. If reading B is taken, the restored sentence is 'A table MUST NOT declare a corresponding `*_name` column for a `carried` identifier', which no invariant of the contract would check.

### 12. R120 §3.2

The records are revisions/accepted/R42-*.md and R73-*.md (Status: Withdrawn). ### 14. R120 §3.4

- **The revision decides:** an alias does not imply that every former statement remains at that destination
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “A replaced or split section may put a former ID beside one honest primary successor as an alias:” (revisions/CONTRIBUTING.md §8.4 (ew-stable-section-anchors))
- **The two readings:** R120 §3.4: 'An alias **MUST NOT** imply that every former statement remains at that destination'. Guide §8.4 keeps 'A replaced or split section may put a former ID beside one honest primary successor as an alias' and 'Do not give a retired subject a misleading alias', and leaves to human review 'whether an alias names an honest successor'. Reading 1 (stated): the sentence is a rule of interpretation, and 'one honest primary successor' of 'a replaced or split section' says exactly that an alias marks the main successor and not the home of everything the old section held. No other document carries the sentence (searched spec/CONTRIBUTING.md, spec/README.md, Part 00 and R121-R155 for 'alias').

### 15. R125 §3.11

- **The revision decides:** such a validator MUST disable external state and side effects defensively
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** R125 §3.11 closes with a list of what 'a validator claiming support' MUST do: create schema-qualified packet-local relations, statically check every rule, lock the deterministic settings, 'disable external state and side effects defensively', lower types, and execute only checked queries. Every other item restates R125 §§3.2-3.5 and is in Part 11 §§2-5. Reading 1 (not a separate rule): this item restates R125 §3.2, which Part 11 §2 carries as 'It MUST reject a second statement, any surface outside this positive profile, and every DDL, DML, transaction, catalog, configuration, attachment, import/export, extension, macro, filesystem, network, parameter, volatile, or environment-dependent surface before execution'; a checked query then cannot reach external state or cause a side effect, and switching the engine's facilities off as well is a technique no packet, registry or report can observe. Reading 2 (missing): 'defensively' asks for a second barrier in the engine in case the static check is wrong, R125 §2.4 names the engine's file-reading functions as the motive, and no part requires that barrier. No later revision touches it; R149 §3.3 only renames the language.

### 16. R125 §3.2

- **The revision decides:** an ISO feature identifier in `iso_feature_dependencies` identifies a definition the profile depends on; it is not a claim of full ISO conformance, does not permit every form the feature covers, and does not permit syntax by itself; the complete permission and semantic boundary is the cited Part 11 clause set

- **The two readings:** The clause's three sentences carry no keyword and define what a listed identifier means. Part 11 never says it in terms: §1 only lists "ISO feature dependencies" among what the descriptor assigns and says the fields "carry the meanings defined here"; rule_language.schema.json has no description. Reading A (refutes): every consequence is stated with force. §1 limits the external authorities "only to the extent selected here" (no full-conformance claim), §2 makes a validator reject "any surface outside this positive profile" (an identifier admits no syntax and not every form of its feature), and §1 forbids "independently toggling profile dependencies or settings"; the sentence is a gloss. Reading B (confirms): Part 00 §2.2 makes prose own the semantics of declared fields, §1 promises the meanings are defined in Part 11, and the meaning of this field is not, so the definition is lost even though its effects survive. No later revision bears on it (R149 §3.3 only adds E021-04 and renames the descriptor). If restored it belongs in Part 11 §1 as: "An ISO feature or subfeature identifier in `iso_feature_dependencies` identifies a definition on which the profile depends. It is not a claim of full ISO implementation conformance, does not permit every form that feature covers, and does not permit syntax by itself; §§1–8 are the complete permission and semantic boundary."

### 17. R126 §3.1

- **The two readings:** No part states the prohibition; the candidate satisfies it. ### 19. R129 §3.10

- **The revision decides:** a view created by a reader or analysis system over packet resources is an operation and MUST NOT be treated as an independently declared packet resource merely because the operation exists
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** R129 §3.10 has the MUST NOT and no part has the sentence; no later revision withdraws it. Reading 1 (refuted): the rule follows from stated ones. Part 04 §2 makes the manifest the only source of packet resources ('The manifest binds every resource instance'), Part 05 §10 requires every listed resource to resolve a declaration, and Part 03 §2 says every current interface is materialized and 'a view requires a separately specified language and evaluation contract', so an operation that writes no manifest entry cannot be a packet resource. Reading 2 (confirmed): Part 00 §3.1.2 and Part 06 §6 call a view 'a realization of a logical kind', which invites treating a reader's view as a resource of that kind; the clause exists to rebut that, binds analysis systems as well as readers, and the keyword in Part 04 §2 binds only a reader's resolution of resources. If reading 2 is taken, the sentence belongs in Part 03 §2 (ew-portable-profile-technologies) beside the view sentence, as: 'A view created by a reader or analysis system over packet resources is an operation and MUST NOT be treated as an independently declared packet resource solely because the operation exists.'

### 20. R129 §3.2

- **The revision decides:** the descriptor's interface names are the exact vocabulary for this profile; an implementation MUST NOT substitute an unincorporated name
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “The descriptor owns the profile's exact identifiers, kind-to-interface mapping, interface forms, ownership compatibility, variants, reader floors, floor roles, overriding orders, and anchors.” (Part 03 §1 (ew-portable-profile-scope))
- **The two readings:** The clause sentence is in R129 §3.2 with MUST NOT force. No later revision touches it (R137, R143, R144, R154 cite R129 without changing §3.2), and the sentence was never in a part: R129's own incorporation (cbfbccf3) wrote Part 03 §§1-2 as they stand. ### 21. R129 §3.8

- **The revision decides:** it MUST compare those observations with a descriptor that has passed the schema and generic checker, rather than with a packet claim or a local duplicate floor constant
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “A validator MUST assess floor membership for every packet-owned resource whose interface declares a reader floor” (Part 05 §5.3 (ew-validation-shim))
- **The two readings:** The clause's MUST is in R129 §3.8 and no later revision changes it (R137 answers only §3.8's deferral of finding IDs). Reading A (refuted, stated in substance): Part 05 §5.3 says 'A validator MUST assess floor membership ...' judged under Part 03 §§5.5 and 6.4; Part 03 §5.5 says 'A validator assessing a Parquet resource MUST compare each column chunk's footer-reported compression and encodings with the floor's `compressions` and `encodings`', naming the descriptor's members, and 'The descriptor is the exact machine-readable owner of these members'; §6.4 gives the Zarr floors as exactly the descriptor's; Part 03 §1 says 'A conforming distributed copy MUST pass the schema and the generic cross-member checker at a packaging or equivalent conformance boundary'. 'Rather than with a packet claim' is carried by Part 04 §5.3's MUST NOT infer from 'the packet, an adapter claim, or a stand-in' and by Part 04 §6 and the closed manifest schema, which leave no packet field to claim it. Reading B (survives): no part says the thing compared against must be the checked descriptor itself rather than a hand-kept constant that restates the floor; R129 §6 treats 'no reader, writer, or validator maintains an unchecked copy of the profile mapping or floor' as evidence to establish, and that prohibition appears in no part (searched 'duplicate', 'constant', 'unchecked', 'bundled', 'copy'). The difference is unobservable in a packet verdict while the constant agrees with the descriptor, which is why two readers could differ on whether it is a rule lost or an implementation-structure detail.
