# Disputed rules

Two careful readings of each clause below disagree on whether a part still owes the rule. The second reading states both.

21 rules, in revision order. A third reading then adjudicated each dispute and, where the rule is owed, tried to show the restoration to be a mistake. Its verdicts: not owed (15), owed, reworded (5), owed, and needs a decision (1). "Not owed" means no part changes and the entry names what the ledger entry should record instead. Entries are cited across these files by class letter and entry number: C7 is entry 7 of class C, D3 of class D, and E18 of the disputed rules.

### 1. R3 §2.1

- **The revision decides:** wall-clock and calendar columns are disjoint from experimental time and the two MUST NOT be mixed: a `*_tick` column has no timezone, epoch or calendar semantics, nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp, and nothing administrative uses a tick
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “Every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis, and media frame timing record MUST declare a time basis: an integer tick and a `clock_id`.” (Part 01 §2.2 (ew-time-basis))
- **The two readings:** The clause's paragraph is headed 'Wall-clock columns only' and is first a scope statement for R3 §2.1's `timestamp` and `date` encoding rules; its last sentence adds the MUST NOT. Reading A (refuted, stated in pieces): each operative half is in a part. Part 01 §2.2 requires every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis and media frame timing record to declare 'an integer tick and a `clock_id`' (so nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp); Part 01 §3.9 says the tick-zero binding 'is the only route from a packet's ticks to real time; absent it, ticks relate to each other and to nothing else' (so a tick has no timezone, epoch or calendar semantics); Part 01 §2.3 states a session's extent 'as wall-clock time, not as a tick range'; Part 02 §5.3 types the two apart, and the meta-schema forces a `coordinate` column (the only role that may carry `clock_from`) to `int64`, so a registry cannot declare a tick as a `timestamp`. R6 later added `tick_zero_utc`, the one sanctioned bridge. Reading B (confirmed): no part says the two systems MUST NOT be mixed, and 'nothing administrative uses a tick' is stated nowhere in general form, only for a session's extent; an extension author could type a provenance time as a tick without contradicting any sentence. The dispute is whether the general prohibition is a rule of its own or the summary of the typed rules that realise it.
- **Third reading: not owed.** Not owed: the prohibition is a summary of rules the parts state and the meta-schema enforces (a `_tick` name is an int64 coordinate on a declared clock; wall-clock columns are `timestamp`; ticks reach real time only through a tick-zero binding). Record the realising sections in the ledger: where = [{ part = "03", anchor = "ew-parquet-reader-floor" }, { part = "02", section = "5.3" }, { part = "02", anchor = "ew-logical-column-naming" }, { part = "01", anchor = "ew-time-basis" }, { part = "01", anchor = "ew-clock-concept" }], with a note that the general MUST NOT is not restated because it names no actor and the typed rules realise it.
- **Fix or revision:** ledger-only: no part changes, so no two implementations could behave differently. Putting the general MUST NOT into a part would add an actorless, undecidable requirement, which Part 00 §2.4 and spec/CONTRIBUTING.md §4 do not admit as a text fix.
- **By lens:**
  - *Clause:* R3 §2.1's paragraph 'Wall-clock columns only' scopes the `timestamp` and `date` encoding rules to wall-clock columns, then says the two systems 'are disjoint and MUST NOT be mixed: nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp, and nothing administrative uses a tick'. The MUST NOT names no actor and no artifact part, and 'administrative' and 'aligns data' are defined nowhere.
  - *Stated in parts:* Part 01 §2.2: every event, interval, spike time, dense time axis and media frame timing record 'MUST declare a time basis: an integer tick and a `clock_id`'. Part 01 §3.9: a tick-zero binding 'is the only route from a packet's ticks to real time; absent it, ticks relate to each other and to nothing else', and a binding and a session's start time 'MUST NOT be conflated'. Part 01 §2.3 states a session's extent as wall-clock time, 'not as a tick range'; Part 02 §5.3 types the two apart ('Tick columns MUST be `int64`. Wall-clock columns MUST be `timestamp`'); Part 03 §5.5 carries the TIMESTAMP, INT96 and DATE rules.
  - *Carried by artifact:* spec/artifacts/registry.schema.json (column `allOf`) makes role `coordinate` and a `_tick` name imply each other, forces a coordinate to `int64`, and admits `clock_from` only on a coordinate; Part 02 §4 states the naming relation and Part 02 §8.4 requires a coordinate's clock source to reference `core.clocks.clock_id`. A registry, core or extension, therefore cannot declare a tick as a `timestamp` or a `timestamp` as a tick; in logical_schemas.json every `_tick` column is an int64 coordinate and every `timestamp`/`date` column has role `data`.
  - *Later revisions:* R6 cites 'two disjoint time systems (R3 §2.1)' as motivation and then adds `core.clocks.tick_zero_utc`, a `timestamp` whose only use is to place ticks in real time (Part 01 §3.9 gives the formula). R13, R24, R43 cite §2.1 for other sentences. None restates the general prohibition.
  - *Restoration risk:* A restored 'nothing that aligns data uses a timestamp' would sit against Part 01 §3.9's sanctioned route from ticks to wall-clock time through `tick_zero_utc`, and the registry rule `core.clocks.tick_zero_plausible`, which compares that timestamp with the session start. 'Nothing administrative uses a tick' has no actor who could tell whether it complied: an extension column `x_tick` on a declared clock is a position on that clock whatever its author calls it, and no check could separate it from experimental time.
  - *Never in a part:* `git log -S'MUST NOT be mixed' -- spec/` and `-S'no timezone'` find no commit that put the sentence in a part; only the initial commit of the revision record carries it. Nothing was lost at incorporation: the parts took the typed rules instead.
  - *Residual:* The one thing reading B names, an extension author typing a provenance time as a tick, is a modelling judgement no sentence of the clause makes decidable. It is not a rule of its own but the summary of the typed rules above.

### 2. R3 §7.1

- **The revision decides:** arrays MUST be little-endian (the `bytes` codec's `endian` configuration)
- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** No part or artifact states a byte order, and no accepted revision names R3 §7.1's sentence as withdrawn. Reading A (changed-by-later): R129 §3.4 rebuilt the Zarr rules and enumerated what still constrains a writer: 'A writer MAY use any codec permitted by the applicable Zarr specification, subject to the existing prohibition on `transpose` and the current regular-chunk-grid rule', and of the floor's bare `bytes` pattern, 'unmentioned configuration members do not participate in the floor match. Other configuration members remain governed by the applicable upstream Zarr or codec specification.' `endian` is such a member, so R129 handed it to upstream Zarr; Part 03 §6.3 now obliges a reader to expose every valid v3 array matching the floor, which includes a big-endian `bytes` codec. Reading B (confirmed): R129 never mentions byte order and does not list R3 §7.1 among what it replaces, so the MUST stands unapplied. The repository itself takes reading B and declines to restore: the pending draft `revisions/drafts/the-reader-floor-bounds-an-arrays-byte-order.md` lists R3 §7.1 as 'the byte-order and `fill_value` requirements this supersedes' and its §3.1 says they 'no longer bind' once accepted, arguing that restoring the sentence 'would contradict a floor the parts already state' and would make a packet referencing a big-endian external array non-conforming. Restoring the sentence is therefore the wrong repair on either reading; the open question is only whether R129 already withdrew it or the draft must.
- **Third reading: owed, and needs a decision.** Do not restore the sentence: it contradicts Part 03 §6.3's writer permission and v3 floor, no check or code enforces it, and it was never in a part. The open draft settles the item (and the clause's `fill_value` sentence) on acceptance; recheck its baseline against the v2 reader sentence restored in §6.4 on 2026-10-01, and until it is accepted treat the ledger's `applied` for this sentence as pending.
- **The choice:** How is R3 §7.1's byte-order sentence closed? (1) Recommended: accept and incorporate the byte-order draft after rechecking its baseline against the restored §6.4 sentence; the draft's §5 then gives the ledger note, and until acceptance the audit records the finding as 'pending the draft', not as a restoration. Parts, descriptor schema (0.4.0), reader floor test and validator finding change as the draft's §5 table lists; the corpus conforms unchanged. Needs a revision, which the draft is. (2) Ledger-only: keep `applied` at Part 03 §6.1 with a note that R129 §3.4's writer permission and floor-matching rule left byte order to the Zarr specification. No part or code changes, but the note rests on an inference about R129, and the v2 floor keeps obliging readers to decode big-endian v2 arrays that zarrs-python refuses and Zarr.jl misreads. (3) Restore 'A packet-owned array MUST be little-endian' in Part 03 §6.1 and add it to §6.4's 'subject to' list: needs a new validator check and a writer guard, narrows what §6.4 permits, and so is a revision too; nothing measured supports it (draft §2.2: every probed reader decodes big-endian v3).
- **Fix or revision:** revision: on either reading the sentence must not be restored by a fix, since a writer storing a big-endian `bytes` codec conforms to Part 03 §6.3 today and would not after. Withdrawing it in terms is what the open draft does; a ledger note alone would assert that R129 withdrew a sentence R129 never mentions.
- **By lens:**
  - *Still decided:* R3 §7.1 says 'Arrays MUST be little-endian' and no accepted revision names the sentence as withdrawn. R21 §3.1 says 'R3 §7.1 is unchanged' for packet-owned arrays. R129 §3.4 says a writer 'MAY use any codec permitted by the applicable Zarr specification, subject to the existing prohibition on `transpose` and the current regular-chunk-grid rule', an exception list that omits byte order, but R129 never mentions byte order and its crosswalk replaces no row of R3 §7.1; it was written from parts that had never stated the rule (`git log -S'little-endian'` finds no commit in a part). So R129 conflicts with the sentence without having decided against it.
  - *Contradiction:* Restoring the sentence contradicts Part 03 §6.3 twice. The writer permission ('MAY use any codec permitted ..., subject to §6.2's `transpose` prohibition and the regular-grid rule') admits a `bytes` codec configured `endian: big`. The v3 floor rule ('Members the pattern does not state do not participate, and a codec stated by its name alone has no configuration members') puts that array inside the floor every reader MUST expose. It also contradicts the open draft, whose §3.1 says a writer MAY store an array in either byte order.
  - *Exceeds the clause:* The clause is unscoped ('Arrays'), written before R14 and R21 admitted external and v2 arrays. Read literally today it would make a packet non-conforming over the byte order of external bytes its writer cannot change (Part 03 §8.2), which R3 did not decide; in Zarr v2 byte order is in `dtype`, which the clause's 'in Zarr v3 this is the `bytes` codec's `endian`' does not reach.
  - *Actor:* 'Arrays MUST be little-endian' is a property of an artifact and is decidable from metadata for v3 (`bytes` `endian`) and v2 (`dtype`'s first character). It names no part of the packet; a restored form would need 'A packet-owned array'.
  - *Checks:* No check observes it. The validator's `shim/arrays.py` checks Zarr version, `transpose`, the floor, the chunk grid and `fill_value` (`array.no_fill_value`, citing Part 03 §6.1, which does not state it) and has no byte-order check; no registry rule reads array metadata and the corpus has no big-endian array (the big-endian set is under `conformance/zarr-probe/endian/`, a probe, not a packet).
  - *Placement:* Part 03 §6.1 or §6.4 would be the owner. The draft places the superseding MAY in §6.4 (`ew-array-codecs`) and leaves §6.1 unchanged.
  - *Implementation and corpus:* `escapewheel_writer/arrays.py` `write_zarr` passes `dtype=spec.values.dtype` to `zarr.create_array` and neither sets nor checks `endian`; its module docstring claims Part 03 §6.1 pins little-endian and an explicit `fill_value`, which the part does not say. The validator would need a new error-level check to honour a restored MUST, and the reader floor would have to exclude arrays every probed reader decodes (draft §2.2). A restoration is a behaviour change, not a text fix.
  - *Draft:* revisions/drafts/the-reader-floor-bounds-an-arrays-byte-order.md (Status Draft, awaiting acceptance per revisions/STATUS.md) settles the item completely once accepted and incorporated: §3.1 states the positive rule (either byte order; byte order is `bytes` `endian` in v3 and the type string's first character in v2), says in terms that R3 §7.1's little-endian and `fill_value` requirements 'no longer bind', and §5 keeps R3 §7.1 `applied` at Part 03 §6.1 for its version, chunk-grid and `transpose` sentences with a note of the supersession. It also covers what the finding does not: the `fill_value` sentence of the same clause, the v2 floor's real defect (two of five probed readers fail a big-endian v2 array), the validator's `fill_value` citation and the writer docstring. It does not settle it before acceptance: until then the ledger's `applied` at §6.1 vouches for a sentence no part states.
  - *Draft baseline:* The draft is based on `5117217`. The 2026-10-01 restorations (commit range ending `bccc6b75`) added to Part 03 §6.3 the sentence 'A conforming reader MUST expose the logical values of every valid v2 array that the v2 reader floor admits.' It agrees with the draft, which narrows what the v2 floor admits and whose §2.2 already reads the floor as an obligation, but the draft's 'revise in place' row for the v2 floor now lands beside a sentence it has not seen, so its baseline should be rechecked under revisions/CONTRIBUTING.md §3 before acceptance.

### 3. R14 §2.3.2

- **The revision decides:** a validator MUST offer a separate, explicitly requested mode that resolves external URIs and checks `sha256` and `size_bytes` where declared
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode.” (Part 04 §5.3 (ew-external-resource-verification))
- **The two readings:** Part 04 §5.3 says only when the external levels run ('Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode') and places no obligation on a validator to offer them, so the part is weaker than R14 §2.3.2 as written. Whether a later revision made it so is disputable. Reading A (changed-by-later): R36 amends R14 §2.3 and restates the offer conditionally in its §2.3, 'A validator offering level 3 MUST offer 3a and MAY offer 3b, and MUST NOT run 3b unless separately asked', which presupposes a validator that does not offer level 3 and makes the checksum check optional. On this reading the unconditional MUST is gone, and the `sha256` half of the finding's rule is changed beyond dispute (reading every byte became MAY). Reading B (confirmed): R36 only split R15 §4's level 3 into a cheap and an expensive layer and did not say it withdrew the obligation to offer the mode; R80 §2.4 still says 'R14 §2.3.2 unchanged', though of structural validation staying off the network. Either way the part also omits R36 §2.3's own sentence, so a repair would state it: a validator offering the external levels MUST offer 3a, MAY offer 3b and 3c, and MUST NOT run the reading levels unless separately asked. Whether to prefix 'A validator MUST offer' is the open point.
- **Third reading: not owed.** Not owed: R36 §2.3, amending R14 §2.3, restated the offer rule conditionally and that sentence is now in Part 04 §5.3; the network prohibition and the requested mode are stated there too. Append to the ledger note for R14 §2.3.2: 'the duty to offer the external mode stands as R36 §2.3 restated it, conditional on a validator offering an external level, with the digest level optional'.
- **The choice:** None is needed to close the finding. If an unconditional duty is wanted it is a new decision with two options: (1) recommended, leave Part 04 §5.3 as it stands, where a validator that offers no external level conforms and its report states the level it reached; (2) by revision, replace the conditional sentence with 'A validator MUST offer level 3a in a separately requested mode and MAY offer level 3c', stating which locations the mode must reach, which binds every validator including a build's in-process one.
- **Fix or revision:** ledger-only: no part changes. Adding the unconditional duty would make a validator without an external mode non-conforming where Part 04 §5.3 admits it today, so that would be a revision, not a fix.
- **By lens:**
  - *Checks:* No registry rule, manifest check or corpus case observes which modes a validator offers; it is observable only in a validator's own suite.
  - *Implementation and corpus:* The reference validator offers all three: `escapewheel_validator/scripts/cli.py` has `--external` (3a), `--store-digests` (3b) and `--checksums` (3c). R15 §6 records that the validator's external mode 'does not exist yet', so the unconditional MUST was unmet when decided. Nothing in code turns on the choice.
  - *Clause:* R14 §2.3.2: 'Structural validation MUST NOT require an external resource to be reachable. A validator MUST offer a separate, explicitly requested mode that resolves external URIs and checks `sha256` and `size_bytes` where declared; findings from that mode are reported against the resource entry'.
  - *Parts today:* The basis is stale on one point. Since commit `5ee501c1` (2026-10-01) Part 04 §5.3 reads: 'Structural validation MUST NOT reach the network. Levels 3a to 3c run only in a requested mode. A validator that offers any of levels 3a to 3c MUST offer level 3a and MAY offer level 3c. A validator MUST NOT run level 3c on a request for level 3a or 3b alone.' and its 3a row includes 'its recorded `size_bytes` holds'. So R36 §2.3's sentence is no longer missing; only the unconditional 'MUST offer' is absent.
  - *Changed by later:* R36 ('Amends R14 §2.3, R15 §4') rewrites the offer rule in its §2.3: 'A validator offering level 3 MUST offer 3a and MAY offer 3b, and MUST NOT run 3b unless separately asked.' It changes the clause's sentence beyond dispute in one half (the `sha256` check a validator had to offer becomes MAY) and restates the other half conditionally on a validator that offers the level. R51 §2.3 keeps 'the three levels ... separate flags, as R36 §2.3 requires'; R80 §2.4's 'R14 §2.3.2 unchanged' is said of structural validation staying off the network, not of the offer.
  - *Contradiction if restored:* An unconditional 'A validator MUST offer' beside the sentence restored on 2026-10-01 makes that sentence's condition ('that offers any of levels 3a to 3c') dead text, so both would have to be rewritten together; the evaluation of the restored R36 §2.3 offer rule chose the conditional form knowing this.
  - *Actor and determinacy:* A validator can be named, but 'offer a mode that resolves external URIs' has no determinable extent: Part 03 §4 admits any absolute URI, and a validator that reaches only `file:` locations 'offers' the mode. It mandates a tool feature rather than an outcome on a packet or a report, which spec/CONTRIBUTING.md §4 reserves MUST against, and it would make a validator embedded where no network exists (a build's own pre-commit validation, Part 00 §2.4) non-conforming.
  - *Ledger:* The entry already targets Part 04 §5.1 and `ew-external-resource-verification`, which state the network prohibition, the requested mode and R36's offer rule; only the note needs the sentence below.

### 4. R51 §2.1

- **First reading:** missing
- **The two readings:** Reading one: 'Declaring members also makes level 3a per-member rather than aggregate' redefines what level 3a establishes for a resource with `members`, as R36 §2.3's table defines the level, so a validator at 3a compares each member's size. Reading two: the paragraph carries no keyword in a section that bolds its MAY and MUST, and continues 'the same listing that produces the total can compare each member instead, at no additional request', which describes what the record enables. No part states either: Part 04 §5.3's 3a row does not mention size at all (finding 28), §5.4 has no size sentence, and §10's `packet.resource.member_size` compares the manifest's sum with itself. No later revision addresses it (R136 §3.3 and R130 §3 touch only member digests and the sum check). If finding 28's size comparison is restored, reading one adds one clause to it; under reading two nothing is owed.

- **Fix or revision:** fix: R51 §2.1 decided it, no later revision changed it, and the reference validator and its suite already behave so. A validator comparing only the aggregate conforms to the part's present words, so the edit is not meaning-preserving in the strict sense of the guide's §1 test; it is the same kind of restoration as the 3a row's `size_bytes` (the restored R36 §2.3 sentence on level 3a) and should ride with it.
- **Observed by a check:** yes, or the sentence binds nothing.
- **By lens:**
  - *Still decided:* R51 §2.1 (Decision): 'Declaring members also makes level 3a per-member rather than aggregate. `size_bytes` on the resource is a total, so two members changing in opposite directions leave it correct'. It redefines what a level establishes, the same standing as R36 §2.3's unkeyworded table, whose `size_bytes` half was restored to the 3a row on 2026-10-01 (the restored R36 §2.3 sentence on level 3a). R51 §2.3, R130 §3 and R136 §§3.2 to 3.5 touch the store-digest level, the member sum and member digests, and none withdraws it; R136 extends `members` to packet-owned prefixes, which 3a does not concern.
  - *Contradiction:* None. Part 04 §5.3's 3a row says 'its recorded `size_bytes` holds' of the entry; §10's `packet.resource.member_size` makes the entry's total equal the members' sum inside the manifest, which is why the aggregate alone cannot see offsetting changes. §5.4's 'A member array is a record, not a resolution rule' binds a reader, not a validator's comparison. No open draft touches members.
  - *Exceeds the clause:* The clause decides the size comparison only. The reference validator also reports declared members the store lacks and store objects the manifest does not declare; no clause puts those in 3a by name (they follow from 'the listing digested MUST be the declared one'), so the sentence leaves them out. It is scoped to an external entry, as level 3a and R51 §2.1 are.

Without it two validators both reporting 3a can disagree on a packet whose members changed in opposite directions, which is the determinacy the clause supplies.
  - *Checks:* Observable: `escapewheel_validator/shim/external.py` `_compare_members` reports `external.members` at `error` for members whose size differs, and `escapewheel-validator/tests/test_shim_external.py` has the opposite-directions case, citing Part 04 §5.4, which does not yet say it.
  - *Placement:* Part 04 §5.4 (`ew-prefix-resources`) defines `members` and already refines a level for them (the store-digest level, which §5.3 points to with 'Level 3b remains the comparison of recorded store digests (§5.4)'). A second clause in the 3a row would also work but overloads the table; §5.4 is where the test and the code already cite.
  - *Implementation and corpus:* Complies: `_compare_external` calls `_compare_members` whenever an external entry declares `members`, under `--external` alone. The fixtures that declare external members (`fixtures/packets/crc_prefix` and three others) are already judged by this comparison wherever level 3a runs, so no packet changes verdict and no new check is needed.
  - *Wording:* R51's sentence carries rationale and 'level 3a per-member rather than aggregate' as shorthand. State what the level establishes, keep the entry total (it still holds by `member_size` when every member holds), and give the reason in one clause.

### 5. R65 §2.2

I lean to reading two: nothing an actor does changes with the wording.
- **Third reading: not owed.** Not owed: the sentence is stated in the indicative in Part 01 §3.17 and §3.5, which is the only admissible form for a statement binding the specification itself, and its enforceable consequence is keyworded in Part 02 §8.3. Keep `applied` and add a note and target: where = [{ part = "01", section = "3.17" }, { part = "02", anchor = "ew-discriminator-semantics" }], note = "stated in the indicative because it binds the specification, which no actor can fail (Part 00 §2.4); Part 02 §8.3 carries the checkable consequence".
- **Fix or revision:** none for the parts; ledger-only for the record. No implementation could behave differently under either wording.
- **By lens:**
  - *Clause:* R65 §2.2: Part 01 §3.17 'is amended to state positively what it previously permitted by silence: an array asset holds any sampled quantity, whatever instrument produced it, and this standard **MUST NOT** enumerate instruments'. The subject of the keyword is the standard itself.
  - *Not a rule:* Part 00 §2.4: 'Every requirement binds one of them or states a property of an artifact', the artifacts being a packet, table, column or manifest entry, and R111 §3.3 calls a keyword doing neither 'a defect in this specification rather than a rule anything can fail'. No writer, reader, checker, validator or consumer can breach 'this standard MUST NOT enumerate instruments'; only a later revision could, and a revision is not bound by a part's keyword. Part 00 §2.3's 'a specification release MUST NOT contain an unresolved ownership conflict' is not a counter-example: it states a property the tree tools check, whereas what counts as enumerating instruments has no test.

  - *Never keyworded:* `git log -S'MUST NOT** enumerate' -- spec escapewheel_poc_docs_v0.2` is empty: the incorporating commit wrote the indicative and no later commit weakened it. R104 §2.3 quotes the indicative form as the position the standard holds, and R78 cites R65 §2.2 only for 'any sampled quantity'.
  - *Restoration risk:* A capitalised MUST NOT would be a requirement no actor can follow and no check can observe, the form R111 §3.3 removed from the parts; it would also invite the question whether `core.probes`, which R104 §2.3 had to explain is in core because electrodes are, breaches it.

### 6. R65 §2.3

- **The revision decides:** A consumer MUST NOT infer an instrument from an asset's semantic_type, its name, or its URI
- **First reading:** weakened; the part says “MUST NOT infer an instrument from an asset's `semantic_type` or name” (Part 01 §3.17)
