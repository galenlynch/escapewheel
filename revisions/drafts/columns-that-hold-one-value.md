# Columns That Hold One Value

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Draft |
| **Amends** | R53 §2.3.1 (which tables get a generated name), `02` (the `name` column on seven tables), R2 §5 and §7.2 (what a rule may name) |
| **Depends on** | R53 (an identifier is an integer, and its owner carries a name), R33 (a rational rate), R13 (one alignment model, the same shape), R81 (the same defect, found the same way) |
| **Profile** | `escapewheel-portable-v0.1` |

§3 is normative; every other section is informative.

MUST / MUST NOT / SHOULD / MAY are used in the RFC 2119 sense, as in the base specification.

## 1. Scope

R3 §3.0 forbids a table restating what something else already declares, and nothing compares two
columns of one table to see whether they do. A sweep over both packaged sessions —
**`ecephys_776259`** (twelve probes, 6,781 sorted units) and **`ecephys_786867`** (ten probes,
4,505 units) — comparing every pair of columns in every table under five million rows found two
duplications and one coincidence that reads like one.

This part strikes the duplicated name columns, makes the two rate forms exclusive, and adds the
report that found both.

## 2. What the sweep found

Informative.

### 2.1 A generated name beside a declared one

R53 §2.3.1 gives every assigned identifier in a primary key a `<base>_name` column, generated by
`build.py` rather than declared by a revision. Seven tables already had a `name` column from `02`,
so they now carry both: `core.devices`, `core.probes`, `core.event_streams`, `core.media_assets`,
`core.clocks`, `core.array_assets` and `analysis.array_assets`.

Four of the seven are written, and in each the two columns are equal on every row, with a distinct
value per row rather than one value repeating:

| table | rows | distinct values |
|---|---:|---:|
| `core.devices` | 20 and 21 | all |
| `core.probes` | 12 and 10 | all |
| `core.event_streams` | 10 and 10 | all |
| `core.media_assets` | 4, in `ecephys_786867` only | 4 |

The two columns differ in what they promise. `name` is **required** on six of the seven —
`core.probes` alone declares it nullable — and its
description is generic — "Human-readable name." — where the generated `<base>_name` is nullable and
carries R53 §2.5's job: the bridge between two packets that numbered the same thing differently,
which R85 §2.2 requires be unique within its packet. So the column with the obligation is optional
and the column with the guarantee says nothing about what it is for.

This is R81's finding repeated, and it hid for the same reason: the generated column was added by a
rule, not by anyone reading the table it landed on.

### 2.2 A float rate beside the rational it rounds

`core.clocks.tick_rate_hz` is `float64`, nullable, described as "required unless exact tick rate
provided" — a fallback for a source that states only a float. In both packets it is written on
every row **alongside** `tick_rate_num` and `tick_rate_den`, and equals `tick_rate_num` on all
**13** rows of `ecephys_776259` and all **11** of `ecephys_786867`, because `tick_rate_den` is
**1 in every row of both**. The fallback is being
used as a copy.

`core.clocks.tick_rate_declared` requires at least one of the two forms and nothing forbids both,
so a packet may carry `tick_rate_hz` of 30000.0 beside a rational of 30000/1001;
`escapewheel_reader.observation` reads the rational and never notices. That is the shape R58 §2.3
left and R60 had to retract: two columns admitting a disagreement no rule can see.

### 2.3 Identifiers that coincide

Three identifier columns of `core.sorting_runs` hold pairwise equal values across all 12 rows in
`ecephys_776259` — `sorting_run_id`, `probe_id` and `clock_id` — and three of `core.units` do
across all 6,781: `probe_id`, `sorting_run_id` and `curation_id`.

Nothing is wrong. `PacketWriter._preassign` numbers each identifier kind independently by sorted
name (R53 §2.5), and with one probe, one sorting, one curation and one clock apiece named in a
consistent order, four independent numberings land on the same integers. The hazard is that a
consumer joining on the wrong one of them works perfectly on both packets and breaks on the first
session with two sortings of one probe.

## 3. Decision

### 3.1 One name column per table

The generated `<base>_name` is **struck** on the seven tables that declare a `name`, and each
table's `name` is **renamed** to `<base>_name`, keeping the presence `02` gave it. `build.py`
**MUST NOT** generate a name for a table that already declares one.

The rename rather than the strike, because the two columns are unequal in what they promise and
each has half of what is wanted: `02`'s presence, and R53 §2.5's meaning. After it each of the
**sixteen** tables carrying a generated name carries exactly one, and the six that were `required`
stay required.

**A `*_name` names what a `*_id` of the same base identifies.** Sixteen do; the six that do not —
`dimension_name` on both array-dimension tables, `insertion_name`, `software_name`, `task_name`,
`sorter_name` — each name something the packet assigns no identifier to, so an unpaired name says
what it names and the absence of a partner is the signal. The seven bare `name` columns are where
that pairing is broken: `core.devices.name` sits beside `device_id` and does not say so.

Normalizing the other way — `device_name` to `name`, since the table is devices — is **rejected**,
because the other half of the pair cannot follow. A `*_id` carries its base for foreign keys: 17 of
26 appear on more than one table, `session_id` on 36, and **89 of 97** reference column mappings
join columns of the same name. Bare `id` on each owner would make every one of those a rename, and
the **8** that are renamed today all carry a role in the base — `parent_clock_id`,
`reference_clock_id`, `supersedes_run_id`, `coordinate_array_asset_id`, `input_array_asset_id` and
two more self-references, with `core.events.stream_id` the one abbreviation among them — so making
the exception universal would destroy what it signals. A `*_name` could normalize alone, since only
2 of 20 appear on more than one table and neither is a reference; it must not, because it would
then disagree with the column it exists to explain.

The surviving column takes R53 §2.3.1's description, so what a reader is told about
`core.devices.device_name` no longer depends on which of two columns they happened to read.

### 3.2 A clock states one rate form

`core.clocks.tick_rate_hz` **stays**, `float64` and nullable, which is what it already is; it
**MUST NOT** become optional, since the rules below name it and R94 §3 forbids a rule naming a
column a packet may omit. A clock **MUST NOT** declare both
`tick_rate_hz` and `tick_rate_num`. `core.clocks.tick_rate_declared` already requires at least one,
and `core.clocks.tick_rate_one_form` refuses both:

```sql
SELECT clock_id, tick_rate_hz, tick_rate_num, tick_rate_den FROM core_clocks
 WHERE tick_rate_hz IS NOT NULL AND tick_rate_num IS NOT NULL
```

The pair makes exactly one form present, which removes §2.2's disagreement by leaving nothing to
disagree with.

Exclusivity rather than a strike, for two reasons. R13 settled this shape one column over — a clock
declares a linear transform or anchor points, never both, and `core.clocks.one_alignment_model`
refuses the pair — so a second representation of one fact already has a treatment here. And forcing
the rational would make a writer invent one: a source stating **29999.7 Hz** has no small ratio,
`float.as_integer_ratio()` giving 8246254744947917/274877906944, so a writer would either emit that
or choose a denominator, which is a rounding decision this format does not make on a source's
behalf (R66 §2.1).

Both packets fail the new rule today, all 24 clock rows carrying both forms. The writer keeps
the rational and stops emitting the float, `tick_rate_den` being 1 in every row of both.

### 3.3 Two equal columns are reported, never failed

A validator **SHOULD** report, at `info`, two columns of one table holding equal values in every
row where that value is not constant. It **MUST NOT** report a failure: §2.3 measures why, since
four independent numberings coinciding is a fact about a packet rather than a defect in it. What a
consumer must not do is already stated — R85 §2.1 forbids reading identity out of a name, and the
same reasoning covers reading it out of a number that happens to match.

The level is what makes it useful. R26 §2 makes an advisory rule off by default, which would leave
this finding where it has been; `info` is what R15 §4 gives a codec claim that cannot be verified,
and it puts the observation in the report without letting it fail a build.

The cost is bounded by the footer rather than by a row sample. Two columns can hold equal values in
every row only if their per-chunk minimum, maximum and null count agree, which Parquet already
records, so the comparison starts from statistics and reads values only for what survives. Measured
over `ecephys_776259`: **2,126** column pairs across 32 tables reduce to **51** in **0.06 s**, with
no values read, and none of the 51 is on a table over a million rows. The prefilter is exact rather
than heuristic — it can only admit pairs, never reject a real one — so the check runs on every
packet without a bound anyone has to choose.

## 4. What this does not change

- **No value is lost from any packet.** Each struck name holds what a surviving column holds, and
  §3.2 keeps both rate forms; what stops is writing the same fact twice.
- **No key or reference changes.** The struck columns are in no key and carry no reference.
- **A float rate stays expressible.** §3.2 forbids stating both forms, not the float, so a source
  that measured a rate rather than deriving it still records what it measured.
- **R53 §2.3.1 still generates a name.** For the nine tables with no `name` of their own —
  `core.units`, `core.electrodes`, `core.sessions` among them — nothing changes.

## 5. Consequence

333 columns become **326**: seven generated names struck, and seven `name` columns renamed rather
than removed. Tier 2 rules go from 69 to **70**, and references are unchanged.

`registry/build.py` skips its R53 §2.3.1 pass for a table declaring `name`, and renames that column
in the same block. `registry/check.py` gains §3.3's check. The writer stops emitting the duplicated
name and the float rate beside the rational; `escapewheel_reader.observation` keeps its float
branch, which §3.2 makes reachable only for a clock that states no rational. The seven renames
reach the writer's adapters, which write `"name"` today for a device, a probe, an event stream, a
media asset, a clock and an array asset.

Six required columns come under R85 §2.2 for the first time. That rule is stated over a `*_name`
and a bare `name` is not one, so renaming brings `core.devices`, `core.clocks`,
`core.event_streams`, `core.media_assets` and both array-asset tables under its requirement that
the value be unique within its packet, and a writer meeting a source that names nothing must
compose one — which two adapters already do and R89 §2.1 recorded for a calibration. Nothing
breaks: `name` is unique and non-null in every written table of both packets, 20 and 21 devices,
12 and 10 probes, 10 and 10 streams, 13 and 11 clocks, 14 and 11 array assets and 4 media assets.

**Both packets must be rebuilt.** Renaming a column leaves a file the registry no longer describes.
R87 §2.1 and R88 §2 already require that rebuild, so this adds none of its own.
R61 §3 makes a correction a rebuild, and this one re-reads no external resource.
